'''Yunus ibn Matta''' ({{langx|ar|{{Script|Arab|يُوْنُس ٱبْن مَتّىٰ}}|Yūnus ibn Mattā}}) is a [[Prophets and messengers in Islam|prophet]] of [[God in Islam|God]] in [[Islam]] corresponding to [[Jonah]] son of Amittai in the [[Hebrew Bible]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=says|first=Quran Academy|title=5 Lessons from the Story of Prophet Yunus|url=https://quranacademy.io/blog/5-lessons-from-the-story-of-prophet-yunus/|access-date=2021-08-12|website=Quran Academy|archive-date=2021-08-12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210812183206/https://quranacademy.io/blog/5-lessons-from-the-story-of-prophet-yunus/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|date=2017-12-26|title=An account of Yunus ibn Matta and his respected father|url=https://www.al-islam.org/hayat-al-qulub-vol-1-allamah-muhammad-baqir-al-majlisi/account-yunus-ibn-matta-and-his-respected|access-date=2021-08-12|website=Al-Islam.org|language=en|archive-date=2021-08-12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210812181117/https://www.al-islam.org/hayat-al-qulub-vol-1-allamah-muhammad-baqir-al-majlisi/account-yunus-ibn-matta-and-his-respected|url-status=live}}</ref> Jonah is the only one of the [[Twelve Minor Prophets]] in the Hebrew Bible to be named in the [[Quran]].<ref name="Islam, pg. 3482">''Encyclopedia of Islam'', ''Yunus'', pg. 348</ref> The tenth chapter of the Quran, [[Yunus (surah)|Yunus]], is named after him.{{sfn|Johns|2003|p=66}}

In the Quran, Yunus is mentioned several times by name, as an apostle of god, and as ''Dhu l-Nun'' ({{langx|ar|ذُو ٱلنُّوْن|4=The One of the Fish}}).{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=67}}<ref>{{Cite web|last=Tier|first=Dr SHAZIA SIDDIQI Islamic Society of Southern|title=The power of repentance|url=https://www.oleantimesherald.com/the-power-of-repentance/article_2b218122-12a3-5850-a098-b07517680f79.html|access-date=2021-08-12|website=Olean Times Herald|date=23 April 2020|language=en|archive-date=2021-08-12|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210812180647/https://www.oleantimesherald.com/the-power-of-repentance/article_2b218122-12a3-5850-a098-b07517680f79.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Quranic mentions ==
In [[Quran 21:87|Al-Anbiya 21:87]]<ref>{{cite quran|21|87|s=r|b=n}}</ref> and [[Quran 68:48|Al-Qalam 68:48]], Yunus is called Dhul-Nūn ({{langx|ar|ذُو ٱلنُّوْن|lit=The One of the Fish}}).{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=67}} In An-Nisa 4:163 and Al-An'am 6:86, he is referred to as "an apostle of God".{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=67}} Surah 37:139-148 retells the full story of Yunus:{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=67}}{{Blockquote|So also was Jonah among those sent (by Us).<br />When he ran away (like a slave from captivity) to the ship (fully) laden,<br />He (agreed to) cast lots, and he was condemned:<br />Then the whale did swallow him, and he had done acts worthy of blame.<br />Had it not been that he (repented and) glorified Allah,<br />He would certainly have remained inside the Fish till the [[Last Judgment|Day of Resurrection]].<br />But We cast him forth on the naked shore in a state of sickness,<br />And We caused to grow, over him, a spreading plant of the gourd kind.<br />And We sent him (on a mission) to a hundred thousand (men) or more.<br />And they believed; so We permitted them to enjoy (their life) for a while.|Quran, chapter 37 ([[As-Saaffat]]), [[ayah|verses]] 139–148<ref>{{cite quran|37|139|e=148|s=ns}}</ref>}}The Quran does not mention Yunus' heritage,{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=67}} but Muslim tradition teaches that Yunus was from the [[tribe of Benjamin]].<ref name="Islam, pg. 3482"/>

== Hadithic mentions ==
Yunus is also mentioned in a few incidents during the lifetime of [[Muhammad in Islam|Muhammad]]. In some instances, Yunus' name is spoken of with praise and reverence by Muhammad. According to historical [[Hadith|narrations about Muhammad's life]], after ten years of receiving revelations, Muhammad went to the city of [[Ta’if]] to see if its leaders would allow him to preach his message from there rather than [[Mecca]], but he was cast from the city by the people. He took shelter in the garden of Utbah and Shaybah, two members of the [[Quraysh (tribe)|Quraysh]] tribe. They sent their servant, [[Addas]], to serve him grapes for sustenance. Muhammad asked Addas where he was from and the servant replied Nineveh. "The town of Yunus the just, son of Amittai!" Muhammad exclaimed. Addas was shocked because he knew that the pagan Arabs had no knowledge of Yunus. He then asked how Muhammad knew of this man. "We are brothers," Muhammad replied. "Yunus was a Prophet of God and I, too, am a Prophet of God." Addas immediately accepted Islam and kissed the hands and feet of Muhammad.<ref>Summarized from ''The Life of the Prophet'' by [[Ibn Hisham]] Volume 1 pp. 419–421</ref>

One of the Hadith of Muhammad, in ''[[Sahih al-Bukhari]]'', says that Muhammad said "One should not say that I am better than Yunus".<ref>{{Hadith-usc|bukhari|4|55|608|usc=yes}}</ref>{{sfn|Wheeler|2002|page=172}}{{sfn|Graham|1977|page=167}}{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}} Ibn Abi al-Salt, an older contemporary of Muhammad, taught that, had Yunus not prayed to Allah, he would have remained trapped inside the fish until Day of Resurrection{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}} but, because of his prayer, Yunus "stayed only a few days within the belly of the fish".{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}}

The ninth-century Persian historian [[Al-Tabari]] records that, while Jonah was inside the fish, "none of his bones or members were injured".{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}} Al-Tabari also writes that Allah made the body of the fish transparent, allowing Yunus to see the "wonders of the deep"{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=74}} and that Yunus heard all the fish singing praises to Allah.{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=74}} [[Kisai Marvazi]], a tenth-century poet, records that Yunus' father was seventy years old when Yunus was born{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}} and that he died soon afterwards,{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}} leaving Yunus' mother with nothing but a wooden spoon, which turned out to be a [[cornucopia]].{{sfn|Vicchio|2008|page=73}}

== Tombs ==
{{See also|List of Islamic structures in Mosul#Mosque of the Prophet Jonah}}
[[File:Ruins_of_the_Mosque_of_Yunus.png|thumb|Photograph of the ruins of the [[Al-Nabi Yunus Mosque|mosque of Yunus]], following its destruction by ISIL]]

[[Nineveh]]'s current location is marked by excavations of five gates, parts of walls on four sides, and two large mounds: the hill of [[Kuyunjik]] and hill of Nabi Yunus.<ref>{{cite web|date=2013-03-19|title=Link to Google map with Nineveh markers at gates, wall sections, hills and mosque|url=https://www.google.com/maps?q=%22Al-Nabi+Yunus%22+OR+hill+OR+gate+OR+wall+loc:+Mosul,+Iraq&hl=en&ie=UTF8&sll=37.6,-95.665&sspn=36.011613,56.337891&t=h&hq=%22Al-Nabi+Yunus%22+OR+hill+OR+gate+OR+wall&hnear=Mosul,+N%C4%ABnaw%C4%81,+Iraq&z=13|access-date=2014-06-29|publisher=Goo.gl|archive-date=2021-03-08|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210308193556/https://www.google.com/maps?q=%22Al-Nabi+Yunus%22+OR+hill+OR+gate+OR+wall+loc:+Mosul,+Iraq&hl=en&ie=UTF8&sll=37.6,-95.665&sspn=36.011613,56.337891&t=h&hq=%22Al-Nabi+Yunus%22+OR+hill+OR+gate+OR+wall&hnear=Mosul,+N%C4%ABnaw%C4%81,+Iraq&z=13|url-status=live}}</ref> A [[Al-Nabi Yunus Mosque|mosque]] atop Nabi Yunus was dedicated to Jonah and contained a shrine, which was revered by both Muslims and Christians as the site of Jonah's tomb.<ref>{{cite web|date=25 July 2014|title=ISIS destroys 'Jonah's tomb' in Mosul|url=http://english.alarabiya.net/en/News/middle-east/2014/07/25/ISIS-destroys-tombs-of-two-prophets-in-Mosul.html|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140727203131/http://english.alarabiya.net/en/News/middle-east/2014/07/25/ISIS-destroys-tombs-of-two-prophets-in-Mosul.html|archive-date=27 July 2014|access-date=28 July 2014|publisher=[[Al Arabiya]]|quote=The radical Islamic State of Iraq and Syria (ISIS) group has destroyed shrines belonging to two prophets, highly revered by both Christians and Muslims, in the northern city of Mosul, al-Sumaria News reported Thursday. "ISIS militants have destroyed the Prophet Younis (Jonah) shrine east of Mosul city after they seized control of the mosque completely," a security source, who kept his identity anonymous, told the Iraq-based al-Sumaria News.}}</ref> The tomb was a popular pilgrimage site{{sfn|Farhan|Lawandow|Samuel|2017}} and a symbol of unity for Jews, Christians, and Muslims across the Middle East.{{sfn|Farhan|Lawandow|Samuel|2017}} On July 24, 2014, the [[Islamic State]] destroyed the mosque containing the tomb as part of a campaign to destroy religious sanctuaries it deemed to be [[idolatry in Islam|idolatrous]].{{sfn|Ford|Tawfeeq|2014}}{{sfn|Farhan|Lawandow|Samuel|2017}}

After Mosul was taken back from the Islamic State in January 2017, an Assyrian palace built by [[Esarhaddon]] dating to around the first half of the [[7th century BCE]] was discovered beneath the ruined mosque.{{sfn|Farhan|Lawandow|Samuel|2017}}{{sfn|Ensor|2017}} ISIL had plundered the palace of items to sell on the [[black market]],{{sfn|Farhan|Lawandow|Samuel|2017}}{{sfn|Ensor|2017}} but some of the artifacts that were more difficult to transport remained in place.{{sfn|Farhan|Lawandow|Samuel|2017}}{{sfn|Ensor|2017}}

=== Other Muslim tombs ===
Other reputed locations of Jonah's tomb include the [[Arab citizens of Israel|Israeli Arab]] village of [[Mashhad, Israel|Mashhad]], located on the ancient site of [[Gath-hepher]] in [[Israel]];{{sfn|Limburg|1993|page=39}} the [[Palestinian territories|Palestinian]] [[West Bank]] town of [[Halhul]], {{convert|5|km|abbr=on}} north of [[Hebron]];{{sfn|Friedman|2006|page=64}} and a sanctuary near the city of [[Sarepta]] in [[Lebanon]].{{sfn|Costa|2013|page=97}} Another tradition places the tomb at a hill now called Giv'at Yonah, "Jonah's Hill", at the northern edge of the Israeli town of [[Ashdod]], at a site covered by a modern lighthouse.

'''Safa and Marwa''' ({{langx|ar|{{Script|Arab|ٱلصَّفَا وَٱلْمَرْوَة}}|Aṣ-Ṣafā wal-Marwah}}) are two small hills, connected to the larger Abu Qubais and Qaiqan mountains, respectively,<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|last1=Hamw|first1=Mahmoud M.|title=Makkah Al-Mukarramah: History and Milestones|last2=Isa|first2=Abdul Ghani|year=2019}}</ref> in [[Mecca]], [[Saudi Arabia]], now made part of ''Al-[[Masjid al-Haram]]''. [[Muslim]]s travel back and forth between them seven times, in what is known as ''saʿī'',({{langx|ar|سَعِي|lit=seeking/searching or walking}})<ref name="AtoZ">{{cite book|last=Mohamed|first=Mamdouh N.|url=https://archive.org/details/hajjumrahfromtoz00moha|title=Hajj to Umrah: From A to Z|publisher=Amana Publications|year=1996|isbn=0-915957-54-X|url-access=registration}}</ref> during the ritual pilgrimages of ''[[Hajj|Ḥajj]]'' and ''[[Umrah]]''.

Muslims run between the two mountains, which they believe was made a ritual as a tribute to [[Hagar in Islam|Hajar]]'s search of water for her child dying of thirst until she found a water source in the [[Zamzam Well]]. The space between the two mountains in which the pilgrims run is called ''al-Mas'aa''.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1">{{Cite web|date=2018-01-06|title=IN PICTURES: The story of a Muslim ritual with roots dating back 5,000 years|url=https://english.alarabiya.net/en/features/2018/01/06/IN-PICTURES-The-story-of-a-Muslim-ritual-that-goes-back-5-000-years.html|access-date=2020-07-06|website=Al Arabiya English|language=en}}</ref>

== Geography ==
[[File:Tavaf.jpg|thumb|Path of movement between Safa and Marwa, illustrated alongside the Tawaf or circumambulation of the [[Kaaba]]]]
Safa is a small mountain located at the bottom of the Abu Qubais Mountain, about {{cvt|130|m|yd}} southeast of the [[Kaaba|Ka'bah]], which is the beginning of the Sa'ee. As for Marwa, it is also a small mountain of white stone, located {{cvt|300|m|yd}} to the northeast of the Ka'bah and it is connected to Qaiqan Mountain, marking the end of the Sa'ee. Safa, Marwah and the Masa'a (space between the two mountains) were located outside the [[Masjid al-Haram]] and were separate until the year 1955/56 (1375 AH), when the project to annex the two sites into the Masjid al-Haram was undertaken for the first time, and they were subsequently annexed.<ref name=":0" /> The distance between Safa and Marwa is approximately {{convert|450|m|yd|abbr=on}}, therefore, seven trips back and forth amount to about {{Convert|3.2|km|abbr=on|0}}.

== History ==

=== Geological perspective ===
{{See also|Hijaz Mountains|Sarawat Mountains|label 1=Hijaz mountain range|label 2=Sarawat mountain range}}
The individual geological history of the two mountains is relatively unknown. Marwa has been described as smoother and lighter in color than Safa, with some even calling it white, such as Majd ad-Din Ferozabadi, az-Zubaidi, al-Fayoumi. al-Alusi further went on to say that Safa's color was comparable to a tint of red. Safa and Marwa are a part of the [[Hijaz Mountains|Hejaz]] [[mountain range]], which run parallel to most of the Saudi coast on the [[Red Sea]]. The Hejaz themselves are part of the larger [[Sarawat Mountains|Sarawat]] range, which is characterized by young and jagged mountains.<ref>{{Cite book|last=Farmer, G. Thomas.|title=Climate change science. Volume 1, The physical climate : a modern synthesis|date=2013|publisher=Springer|others=Cook, John (Climatologist)|isbn=978-94-007-5757-8|location=Dordrecht|oclc=826009050}}</ref>

=== Islamic narrative ===
{{Main|Abraham in Islam}}
In [[Islam]]ic tradition, the civilization of Mecca started after [[Abraham in Islam|Ibrāhīm]] ([[Abraham]]) left his son [[Ishmael in Islam|Ismāʿīl]] ([[Ishmael]]) and wife [[Hagar in Islam|Hājar]] ([[Hagar]]) in the valley,<ref name="Lings1983">{{cite book |last=Lings |first=Martin |author-link=Martin Lings |title=Muhammad: His Life Based on the Earliest Sources |publisher=Islamic Texts Society |year=1983 |isbn=978-0-946621-33-0|title-link=Muhammad: His Life Based on the Earliest Sources }}</ref><ref name="Glasse1991">{{cite encyclopedia |last=Glassé |first=Cyril |title=Kaaba |encyclopedia=The Concise Encyclopedia of Islam |publisher=[[HarperSanFrancisco]] |year=1991 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=dlPuAAAAMAAJ |isbn=0-0606-3126-0}}</ref> which Muslims believe was a command by God. When their provisions were exhausted, Hajar eventually ran out of food and water and could no longer breastfeed Ismail.<ref name=":1" /> She thus ran back and forth seven times between Safa and Marwa hoping to find water.<ref name=":1" /> To make her search easier and faster, she went alone, leaving the infant on the ground. She first climbed the nearest hill, Safa, to look over the surrounding area. When she saw nothing, she then went to the other hill, Marwah, to look around. While Hagar was on either hillside, she was able to see Ishmael and know he was safe. However, when she was in the valley between the hills she was unable to see her son, and would thus run whilst in the valley and walk at a normal pace when on the hillsides. Hagar traveled back and forth between the hills seven times in the scorching heat before returning to her son. Allah then sent Angel [[Jibril]] ([[Gabriel]]) to their aid and a spring of water appeared from the ground. The well was named [[Zamzam Well|Zamzam]] and the journey back and forth Safa and Marwa was made a ritual during Hajj and Umrah.<ref name=":1" /> The two mountains are mentioned by name in the [[Quran 2:158]].

=== Before Muhammad ===
{{See also|Pre-Islamic Arabia}}
The early [[Ansar (Islam)|Ansari]] Muslims gave up the custom of ''Sa'ee'' seeing it as idol worship and ''[[Shirk (Islam)|shirk]]'' and a sign of the pre-Islamic period of ignorance (''[[jahiliyyah]]''). It is in this context that Verse 158 of Surah 2 was revealed ([[Sahih al-Bukhari|Sahih Bukhari]], Vol. 6, Book 60, Hadith 22/23).<ref>{{Cite book|author=Bukhārī, Muḥammad ibn Ismāʻīl |title=The English translation of Ṣaḥīḥ al Bukhārī with the Arabic text|date=1996|publisher=Al-Saadawi Publications|others=Khan, Muhammad Muhsin.|isbn=1-881963-59-4|location=Alexandria, Va.|oclc=35673415}}</ref> In another narration by [[Abdullah Yusuf Ali]] in his commentary on Verse 2:158, he argues that the verse had been revealed because the pagans of [[Quraysh]] had placed two idols atop the two hills and the Muslims felt hesitant to walk between the hills, seeing it as idol worship or as an act of ''shirk''.<ref>{{Cite book|title=The Holy Qur'an|translator=Ali, Abdullah Yusuf |isbn=978-1-84870-568-5|location=Ware|oclc=870650664}}</ref> [[Anas ibn Malik]] also said that he felt hatred in walking between the two hills as he saw it as a pre-Islamic custom from the ''jahiliyyah'' until Allah had revealed Verse 2:158 (Sahih Bukhari, Vol. 2). [[Aisha]] confirmed the verse had been revealed with regards to the Ansar, who said it was sinful to walk between the hills as they used to visit the idol [[Manat (goddess)|Manat]] at Qudaid (near [[Mecca]]) in the state of [[Ihram]] before going about with the rituals of their pilgrimage in pre-Islamic times. Several similar reasons have been given by multiple scholars of Islam, including [[al-Suyuti]] in his Asbab an-Nuzul<ref>{{Cite book|last=as-Suyuti|first=Imam Jalaludin 'Abdul Rahman bin Abi Bakr|title=Reasons and Occasions of Revelation of Quran: Asbab Nuzul |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wx5LDwAAQBAJ&pg=PA27 |publisher=Dar al-Kotob al-Ilmiyah (DKI)|year=2008|isbn=9782745184108|location=[[Beirut]]|pages=27}}</ref> and [[George Sale]] in his Preliminary Discourse to the Quran.<ref>{{Cite book|last=Wherry|first=Elwood Morris|title=A Comprehensive Commentary on the Qurán: Comprising Sale's Translation and Preliminary Discourse|year=2019|isbn=9783337821944|pages=22}}</ref>

The names ''Safa'' and ''Marwa'' are similar to two hills near to [[Jerusalem]]: [[Mount Scopus]] (known in Hellenistic times as ''Sapha'') and [[Moriah]] (site of the [[binding of Isaac]], traditionally identified with [[Temple Mount]]), which has led to a theory that the ritual was originally practiced at Jerusalem, or that the hills of Mecca were named after the hills at Jerusalem.<ref>{{cite web |last=Ellis |first1=Paul D. d'A. |title=Jerusalem, City of Islam: The Case for Identifying the Qurʾan's 'Sacred Place of Prostration' as the Jerusalem Temple |url=https://www.academia.edu/76385952}}</ref>

== Significance in the Hajj and Umrah ==
{{See also|Hajj|Umrah}}
Performing the ''Sa'ee'' serves to commemorate Hajar's search for water for her son and God's mercy in answering prayers. Two walkways guide pilgrims from Safa to Marwa, and from Marwa to Safa, with two narrower walkways in the center to serve elderly and [[Disability|disabled]] pilgrims. The walkways between the two mountains are collectively called the ''Mas'aa'' ({{Langx|ar|المسعى|translit=al-Mas'aa|lit=way, place of walking}}) and are air-conditioned. Water pumped from the Zamzam Well is also available on the way. ''Sa'ee'' is an integral part and ''[[Fard|rukn]]'' of Hajj and Umrah.

'''Shaikh Ahmad Khatib al-Minangkabawi''' (26 June 1860 – 9 October 1915) was a [[Minangkabau people|Minangkabau]] Islamic teacher. He was born in Koto Tuo, [[Dutch East Indies]], and died in [[Mecca]], [[Ottoman Empire]].<ref>Fadhlan Mudhafier, Syeikh Ahmad Khatib Al-Minangkabawy: Pemikiran dan Perjuangannya, Masa 1276-1334 Hijriah, 2013</ref> He served as the head (''imam'') of the [[Shafi'i]] school of law at the mosque of Mecca (''[[Masjid al-Haram]]''). He was known for being a teacher of Islamic reformist leader [[Ahmad Dahlan]], who founded [[Muhammadiyah|Muhammadiyah Society]] and [[Hasyim Asyari]],  who founded [[Nahdlatul Ulama]] in the early 20th century.<ref>Fred R. Von der Mehden, Two Worlds of Islam: Interaction Between Southeast Asia and the Middle East, 1993</ref>

Although Ahmad Khatib was an orthodox Sunni Muslim, he still hoped to reconcile the [[Matrilineality|matrilineal system]] in [[Minangkabau people|Minangkabau]] with the laws of inheritance prescribed in the Quran. Through his Minangkabau students who studied in Mecca as well as those he taught in Indonesia, he encouraged a modified Minangkabau culture based on al-Quran and the Sunnah.

==Biography and family==
Khatib was born on 26 June 1860 in Koto Tuo, Ampek Angkek, [[Agam Regency|Agam Division]], [[West Sumatra]] in the then [[Dutch East Indies]].

His parents were Abdullatief Khatib and Limbak Urai. In 1870 he attended a Dutch school and then continued his studies at Kweekschool in [[Bukittinggi]], Sumatra.<ref name="roi1">{{cite web |url=http://www.republika.co.id/berita/dunia-islam/khazanah/12/01/16/lxvt00-syekh-ahmad-khatib-alminangkabawi-dari-minang-ke-masjidil-haram-1 |title=Syekh Ahmad Khatib Al-Minangkabawi, Dari Minang ke Masjidil Haram (1) |first=Devi Anggraini |last=Oktavika |work=Republika Online |date=16 January 2012 |access-date=10 April 2013}}</ref> Later, he moved to the [[Ottoman Empire]] to receive a nominal Islamic education under the guidance of the local jurists and settled in Mecca for the rest of his life.

His eldest son Abdulkareem owned a bookstore in Makkah. While his other son Abdulmalik Alkhatib was an ambassador of the Ashraf to Egypt. Another of his sons, Sheikh Abdulhameed Alkhateeb, was the first [[Saudi Arabia|Saudi Arabian]] ambassador to the [[Islamic Republic of Pakistan]]. His grandson, [[Fouad Abdulhameed Alkhateeb]], was a Saudi Arabian ambassador, humanitarian, author, and businessman. In his capacity as a diplomat, he represented his homeland in Pakistan, Iraq, the United States of America, Nigeria, Turkey, Bangladesh, Nepal, and finally as Saudi ambassador to Malaysia.

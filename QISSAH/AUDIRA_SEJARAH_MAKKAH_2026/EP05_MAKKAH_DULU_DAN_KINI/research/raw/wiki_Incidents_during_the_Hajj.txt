{{short description|Accidents during the annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca}}
[[File:Day of Hajj. Mecca, Saudi Arabia.jpg|thumb|300px|[[Mount Arafat|Plains of Arafat]] on the day of [[Hajj]], {{circa}} 2003.]]
There have been numerous incidents during the [[Hajj]], the [[Muslim]] pilgrimage to the cities of [[Mecca]] and [[Medina]], that have caused loss of life. Every follower of [[Islam]] is required to perform the Hajj in Mecca at least once in their lifetime, if able to do so; the pilgrimage is one of the [[Five Pillars of Islam]]. During the month of the Hajj, Mecca must cope with as many as three million pilgrims.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2 January 2007 |title=Pilgrims mark end of peaceful hajj: Circling of Kaaba brings to close perhaps largest-ever pilgrimage to Mecca] |work=BBC News |url=https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna16442316 |url-status=live |access-date=18 November 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170905054144/http://www.nbcnews.com/id/16442316/ |archive-date=5 September 2017 |quote=This year’s hajj was likely the biggest ever, with authorities estimating that around 3 million people participated.}}</ref>

[[Commercial aviation|Plane travel]] makes Mecca and the Hajj more accessible to [[pilgrim]]s from all over the world. As a consequence, the Hajj has become increasingly crowded. City officials are required to control large crowds and provide food, shelter, sanitation, and emergency services for millions. Despite these efforts, incidents have still occurred.

==Crushes and failures of crowd control==
[[File:Amellie - Stoning of the devil 2006 Hajj.jpg|thumb|Stoning of the Devil, 2006]]
The dense, surging crowds, trekking from one station of the pilgrimage to the next, can cause a [[Crowd collapses and crushes|progressive crowd collapse]]. At densities above six<ref name=Benedictus3Oct>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/oct/03/hajj-crush-how-crowd-disasters-happen-and-how-they-can-be-avoided|title=Hajj crush: how crowd disasters happen, and how they can be avoided|work=The Guardian|date=3 October 2015|access-date=28 November 2021|first=Leo|last=Benedictus|archive-date=2 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190702223626/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/oct/03/hajj-crush-how-crowd-disasters-happen-and-how-they-can-be-avoided|url-status=live}}</ref> to seven<ref name=Seabrook>{{cite magazine|first=John|last=Seabrook|magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|url=http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/02/07/crush-point|title=Crush Point|date=7 February 2011|access-date=28 November 2021|archive-date=26 May 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180526191658/https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/02/07/crush-point|url-status=live}}</ref> persons per square meter, individuals cannot move, groups are swept along in waves, individuals jostle to find breath and to avoid falling and being trampled, and hundreds of deaths can occur as a result. The [[Stoning of the Devil]] (''ramī aj-jamarāt'') ceremony is particularly crowded and can be dangerous. Pilgrims ritualistically throw pebbles at three walls (formerly pillars [[Stoning of the Devil#Replacement of the pillars|before 2004]]) which represent the three places where the Hadiths describe how the Devil tempted Abraham. It is one of a series of ritual acts that are performed during the Hajj.

==Protests and violence==
*692: The [[Siege of Mecca (692)|siege of Mecca]] occurred when [[Umayyad]] general [[Al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]] besieged Mecca on 25 March 692. The city was bombarded using [[catapult]]s and continued during the [[Hajj]] rituals. It resulted in the [[Kaaba]] being damaged. It was then rebuilt again.<ref>{{The History of al-Ṭabarī |volume=21 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=CArtUGnu9rkC |page=225}}</ref>
*924: The Hajj caravan returning from Mecca to Iraq was [[924 Hajj caravan raid|attacked and destroyed]] by the [[Qarmatians]].<ref>{{Das Reich des Mahdi | pages=226–227}}</ref>
*925: The Hajj caravan setting out from Kufa is stopped by Qarmatian attacks and forced to return to Iraq.<ref>{{Das Reich des Mahdi | page=227}}</ref>
*930: On the first day of [[Hajj]], [[Qarmatians|Qarmatian]] leader [[Abu Tahir al-Jannabi]] led an [[Sack of Mecca|attack on Mecca]] and set about massacring the pilgrims. While killing pilgrims, he taunted them with verses of the [[Koran]] as they did so,<ref>{{Das Reich des Mahdi | pages =229–230}}</ref> and verses of poetry: "I am by God, and by God I am ... he creates creation, and I destroy them". About 30,000 pilgrims were killed. The bodies of the pilgrims were left to rot in the streets or were thrown down the [[Well of Zamzam]]. The [[Kaaba]] was looted, houses were plundered, and slaves seized. He removed the [[Black Stone]] and it was in his possession for 21 years.
*1502: [[4th Portuguese India Armada (Gama, 1502)|The Mira]], a ship carrying Hajj-pilgrims returning from Mecca was set ablaze on the orders of [[Portugal|Portuguese]] explorer [[Vasco da Gama]]. Around several hundred pilgrims were killed.<ref>{{Cite web |title=BBC – History – British History in depth: Vasco da Gama |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/history/british/tudors/vasco_da_gama_01.shtml |access-date=2023-07-16 |website=www.bbc.co.uk |language=en-GB}}</ref>
*1695: On 7 September, [[Piracy|pirates]] led by [[Henry Every]] [[Capture of the Grand Mughal Fleet|hijacked]] [[Grand Mughal]] vessels which were making the annual pilgrimage to Mecca. The pirates then subjected their captives to several days of horror, [[Rape|raping]] and [[murder]]ing prisoners at will, and using [[torture]] to force them to reveal treasure.<ref name="Neverland">{{Cite book |last=Rennie |first=Neil |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=VXUMAgAAQBAJ |title=Treasure Neverland: Real and Imaginary Pirates |date=2013 |publisher=OUP Oxford |isbn=9780191668654 |location=Oxford |language=en |access-date=27 June 2017}}</ref> Some of the women committed suicide by jumping into the sea.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Doug Lennox |title=Now You Know Pirates: The Little Book of Answers |publisher=Dundurn |year=2008 |isbn=978-1-55002-806-5 |edition=illustrated |page=70}}</ref> 
*1757: The [[1757 Hajj caravan raid]] was the plunder and massacre of the [[Hajj]] caravan of 1757 on its return to [[Damascus]] from [[Mecca]] by [[Bedouin]] tribesmen. An estimated 20,000 pilgrims were killed as a result of the raid. 
*1926: On 18 June 1926 Egyptian soldiers playing music while escorting the ''[[mahmal]]'', were confronted by angry Najdis{{clarification needed|date=September 2026}}, who disliked the ''mahmal'' as an innovation and considered music un-Islamic. The Egyptians fired on them, killing 25.<ref>{{Cite book|last=Tagliacozzo and Toorawa|first=Eric and Shawkat M.|title=The Hajj: Pilgrimage in Islam|publisher=Cambridge University Press|year=2015|isbn=978-1107612808|location=Cambridge|pages=207}}</ref>
*In 1986, a group of Iranian pilgrims were caught with [[C-4 (explosive)|C-4 explosives]] in their bags. Later investigation sparked theories that the explosives were put there by Iranian authorities.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://en.radiofarda.com/a/amid-new-revelations-1986-hajj-terror-plot-must-finally-be-fully-investigated/29574827.html|title = Amid New Revelations, 1986 Hajj Terror Plot Must Finally be Fully Investigated}}</ref>
*[[1987 Mecca incident|31 July 1987]]: A clash between Iranian demonstrators and Saudi security forces led to the deaths of more than 400 pilgrims and thousands injured.
*9 July 1989: Two bombs exploded, killing one pilgrim and wounding another 16. The perpetrators were from Kuwait and most of them were later executed.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Times |first=Youssef M. ==Airplane crashes related to the Hajj==
*22 January 1973: A Nigeria Airways Boeing 707-3D3C operated by [[Royal Jordanian]] [[Kano air disaster|crashed]] at [[Kano (city)|Kano]], [[Nigeria]], killing 176 Hajj pilgrims returning from Mecca.
*4 December 1974: [[Martinair Flight 138]] crashed near [[Colombo]], [[Sri Lanka]], killing all 191 people aboard – 182 Indonesian hajj pilgrims bound for Mecca, and 9 crew members.
*15 November 1978: [[Loftleiðir Flight 001]] crashed at [[Colombo]], [[Sri Lanka]], killing 175 (mostly Indonesian) Muslim pilgrims returning from the Hajj and 8 crew members.
*26 November 1979: [[PIA Flight 740|Pakistan International Airlines Flight 740]] had an in-flight fire and crashed after takeoff from the old Jeddah International Airport on 26 November 1979 killing all 156 on board the Boeing 707.
*11 July 1991: [[Nigeria Airways Flight 2120]] (operated by [[Nolisair|Nationair]]) was a chartered passenger flight from Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, to Sokoto, [[Nigeria]] which had an in-flight fire and crashed shortly after takeoff from King Abdulaziz International Airport, killing all 247 Hajj pilgrims and 14 crew members on board the DC-8.
*7 April 1999: [[Turkish Airlines Flight 5904]] was a flight from [[Adana]], [[Turkey]] to Jeddah. The plane was supposed to pick up returning pilgrims but crashed shortly after takeoff. All 6 occupants on board, who were crew members, were killed.

==Disease==
[[Hajj cough|Mingling of visitors]] from many countries, some of which have poor health care systems still plagued by preventable infectious diseases, can lead to the spread of [[epidemic]]s. If an outbreak were to occur on the road to Mecca or Medina, pilgrims could exacerbate the problem when they returned home and passed their infection on to others. This was more of a problem in the past when modern medicine was less advanced. One such disease, which prompted a response from the Saudi government, is [[meningitis]] as it became a primary concern after an international outbreak following the Hajj in 1987. Due to post-Hajj outbreaks globally of certain types of meningitis in previous years, it is now a [[visa (document)|visa]] requirement to be [[Vaccination|immunised]] with the [[Neisseria meningitidis|ACW135Y]] vaccine before arrival. Every year, the Saudi government publishes a list of required vaccines for pilgrims, which for 2010 also included [[yellow fever]], [[polio]], and [[meningitis]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=WHO – 22 October 2010, vol. 85, 43 (pp 425–436) |url=https://www.who.int/wer/2010/wer8543/en/index.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131113001315/http://www.who.int/wer/2010/wer8543/en/index.html |archive-date=13 November 2013 |access-date=11 October 2014 |publisher=World Health Organization}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Barasheed |first1=Osamah |last2=Rashid |first2=Harunor |last3=Heron |first3=Leon |last4=Ridda |first4=Iman |last5=Haworth |first5=Elizabeth |last6=Nguyen-Van-Tam |first6=Jonathan |last7=Dwyer |first7=Dominic E. |last8=Booy |first8=Robert |date=November 2014 |title=Influenza Vaccination Among Australian Hajj Pilgrims: Uptake, Attitudes, and Barriers |journal=Journal of Travel Medicine |volume=21 |issue=6 |pages=384–390 |doi=10.1111/jtm.12146 |pmid=25145836 |doi-access=free}}</ref>

===Notable disease outbreaks===
{{see also|1863–1875 cholera pandemic|1817–1824 cholera pandemic}}
During the 1821 Hajj, about 20,000 Meccan pilgrims died due to a [[Cholera outbreaks and pandemics|cholera epidemic]]. The disease had started in [[India]] in 1817.<ref name=":0">{{Cite journal |last1=Zumla |first1=Alimuddin |last2=McCloskey |first2=Brian |last3=Endericks |first3=Tina |last4=Azhar |first4=Esam I. |last5=Petersen |first5=Eskild |date=1 September 2017 |title=The challenges of cholera at the 2017 Hajj pilgrimage |journal=The Lancet Infectious Diseases |language=en |volume=17 |issue=9 |pages=895–897 |doi=10.1016/S1473-3099(17)30454-1 |issn=1473-3099 |pmc=7129522 |pmid=28803813}}</ref>

In 1905 the [[El Tor]] strain of [[cholera]] was discovered in six pilgrims returning from Hajj at the El-Tor quarantine camp in Egypt.{{citation needed|date=September 2026}}

The [[1972 Yugoslav smallpox outbreak]], the last major [[smallpox]] outbreak in Europe, was caused by an [[Albanians|Albanian]] who brought the disease back from Mecca.<ref>{{Cite web|last=MATIC|first=Katarina SUBASIC and Jovan|title=Memories Of Smallpox Outbreak Stir Nostalgia For Tito's Time|url=https://www.barrons.com/articles/memories-of-smallpox-outbreak-stir-nostalgia-for-tito-s-time-01588303808|access-date=2021-07-06|website=www.barrons.com|language=en-US}}</ref>

In 2009, 73 cases of [[2009 flu pandemic|H1N1]], which is also known as [[Swine influenza|swine flu]], were recorded at the final days of Hajj. Five pilgrims died due to the virus.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Firouz |first=Caren |date=29 November 2009 |title=Saudi official: 5 dead from swine flu at hajj |url=https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna34193091 |access-date=13 March 2020 |website=MSNBC |language=en}}</ref>

== Extreme heat ==
{{Main|2024 Hajj extreme heat disaster}}
Much of the pilgrimage and its rituals are performed outdoors, on foot, and by elderly people, making pilgrims more susceptible to the intense heat present in Mecca during the summer. Outdoor rituals include standing on the plain of [[Mount Arafat]] from sunrise to sunset and walking for several hours in the outskirts of Mecca on other days.<ref name=":02">{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=16 June 2024 |title=At Least 19 Hajj Pilgrims Dead In Saudi Arabia: Jordan, Iran Officials |url=https://www.barrons.com/news/at-least-19-hajj-pilgrims-dead-in-saudi-arabia-jordan-iran-officials-31d2348f |access-date=2024-06-16 |website=www.barrons.com |language=en-US}}</ref> Stress due to heat has been exacerbated by increases in regional temperatures induced by [[climate change]], which Saudi researchers stated in a March 2024 study in the ''[[Journal of Travel Medicine]]'' represented an average rise of [[Dry-bulb temperature|dry-bulb temperatures]] by 0.4 °C (0.7 °F) and [[Wet-bulb temperature|wet-bulb temperatures]] by 0.2 °C (0.4 °F)  per decade. Both are strongly correlated with increased heat stroke and heat exhaustion, and Hajj dates that coincided with Mecca's "hot cycle" in May through September, 1982–1995 and 2015–2024, led to greater risk to pilgrims. During the 1982–1995 hot cycle, rates of heat stroke and heat exhaustion peaked at 134.2 and 858.8 per 100,000 pilgrims respectively, both occurring in August 1985 where the average daily wet-bulb temperature during the Hajj was {{Convert|25.1|C|F}}. The average daily wet-bulb temperatures during Hajj between 1979 and 2019 exceeded the [[National Weather Service|US National Weather Service]] danger threshold of {{Convert|24.6|C|F}} on 38 days, nearly half of which took place in 2015–2019. The study also showed that Hajj pilgrims from countries with colder average temperature were 4.5 times more likely to die than local pilgrims.<ref name=":1" />

Attempts to mitigate the heat include portable water stations and [[Evaporative cooler|misting systems]], as well as expansive [[air conditioning]] in the floor of the [[Masjid al-Haram|Great Mosque of Mecca]] and in surrounding tents. This contributed to a 74.6% reduction in heat stroke cases and a 47.6% decrease in fatality rate compared to the prior 1982–1995 hot cycle, but still represented greater rates compared to the prior cold cycle.<ref name=":1">{{cite journal | url=https://doi.org/10.1093/jtm/taae042 | doi=10.1093/jtm/taae042 | title=Escalating climate-related health risks for Hajj pilgrims to Mecca | date=2024 | last1=Yezli | first1=Saber | last2=Ehaideb | first2=Salleh | last3=Yassin | first3=Yara | last4=Alotaibi | first4=Badriah | last5=Bouchama | first5=Abderrezak | journal=Journal of Travel Medicine | volume=31 | issue=4 | pmid=38457640 | pmc=11149718 }}</ref>

In 2023, temperatures reached {{Convert|48|C|F}}, leading to at least 8,400 pilgrims suffering from heat-related illnesses, with the actual number likely being much greater due to the number of unreported cases.<ref>{{Cite web |last1=Holleis |first1=Jennifer |last2=Rasper |first2=Anke |date=11 June 2024 |title=Hajj 2024: Saudi Arabia braces for extreme heat |url=https://www.dw.com/en/saudi-arabia-hajj-2024-dominated-by-rising-temperatures/a-69333743 |access-date=2024-06-16 |website=dw.com |language=en}}</ref>

In 2024, temperatures in Mecca reached above {{Convert|46|C|F}} on 16 June<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Pelham |first=Lipika |date=16 June 2024 |title=At least 14 Hajj pilgrims die in intense heat |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cyee6gn8x6xo |access-date=2024-06-17 |website=www.bbc.com |language=en-GB}}</ref> and {{Convert|52|C|F}} on 17 June. Medical personnel had to treat at least 2,764 pilgrims (among around 1.8 million total) due to heat-related illness on 16 June 2024 alone.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=17 June 2024 |title=Jordan says at least 14 pilgrims die during Hajj amid searing heat in Mecca |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/6/17/jordan-says-at-least-14-pilgrims-die-during-hajj-amid-searing-heat-in-mecca |access-date=2024-06-17 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en }}{{Dead link|date=August 2025 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> [[Jordan]]'s foreign ministry reported on 16 June that 14 Jordanian pilgrims died after suffering from heat stroke due to the extreme heat, and that 17 more pilgrims were missing.<ref name=":2" /> Between 14 and 19 June 2024, at least 1,301 pilgrims had died due to extreme heat in Mecca during the Hajj of 2024, with temperatures exceeding {{Convert|50|C|F|abbr=on}}.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-06-19 |title=Relatives search for missing in Saudi Arabia as hajj death toll tops 900 |url=https://www.france24.com/en/middle-east/20240619-relatives-search-missing-saudi-arabia-hajj-death-toll-tops-900 |access-date=2024-06-19 |website=France 24 |language=en}}</ref>

== Other fatal events ==
Of the millions of pilgrims each year, many are elderly and some die of their illnesses, exacerbated in some cases by the heat and exertion (for example, in the 1927 pilgrimage season, at least 1,500 pilgrims died<ref>{{Cite web|date=2018-06-01|title=File 1707/1924 'Arabia:- Jeddah Situation Reports. (1924–1930)' [202r] (408/898)|url=https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100084998361.0x000009|access-date=2022-02-11|website=Qatar Digital Library|language=English}}</ref>).
*On 25 November 1908, the passenger-cargo ship [[SS Sardinia (1888)|SS ''Sardinia'']] caught fire off Malta's [[Grand Harbour]] and ran aground, resulting in at least 118 deaths. Most of the casualties were Moroccan pilgrims on their way to Mecca.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Gulf of Corcovado |url=http://www.tynebuiltships.co.uk/G-Ships/gulfofcorcovado1888.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190708121648/http://www.tynebuiltships.co.uk/G-Ships/gulfofcorcovado1888.html |archive-date=8 July 2019 |access-date=18 January 2020 |website=Tyne Built Ships}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |date=7 October 2012 |title=The Tragic end of the 'Maltese Titanic' |work=[[The Malta Independent]] |url=https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2012-10-07/news/the-tragic-end-of-the-maltese-titanic-317022/ |url-status=live |access-date=18 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200118021148/https://www.independent.com.mt/articles/2012-10-07/news/the-tragic-end-of-the-maltese-titanic-317022/ |archive-date=18 January 2020}}</ref>
*On 21 May 1930 the [[Fabre Line]]'s SS ''Asia'', having brought 700 Algerian pilgrims to Mecca for the Hajj and then loaded some 1,500 pilgrims, c. 1,200 of them Yemenis returning to Aden and c. 300 from French Somaliland returning to Djibouti, caught fire in Jeddah harbour. At least 112 pilgrims died, possibly many more.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Human Village – Pélerinage et pandémie: le hajj en CFS au révélateur du naufrage de l'Asia|url=https://human-village.org/spip.php?article1301|access-date=2022-02-11|website=human-village.org}}</ref>
*Before the beginning of the first day of the December 2006 Hajj, 243 pilgrims had died, according to a statement by the Saudi government.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Millions descend on Mecca for haj |url=http://www.iol.co.za/index.php?from=rss_World&set_id=1&click_id=3&art_id=vn20061228102521827C202045 |access-date=11 October 2014 |publisher=Iol.co.za |archive-date=25 December 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181225120329/https://www.iol.co.za/index.php?from=rss_World&set_id=1&click_id=3&art_id=vn20061228102521827C202045 |url-status=dead }}</ref> The majority of deaths were reportedly related to heart problems, exhaustion in the elderly and people with weak health, caused by the heat and tiring physical work involved in the pilgrimage. After the conclusion of the Hajj, the [[Nigeria]]n government reported that 33 nationals had died mostly "as a result of hypertension, diabetes and heart attack", not due to epidemic illnesses, and rejected assertions that Nigerian pilgrims died in an accident on a road to [[Mina, Saudi Arabia|Mina]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Nigeria: Hypertension, Diabetes Kill 33 Nigerian Pilgrims in Saudi Arabia |url=http://allafrica.com/stories/200701080951.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120930182929/http://allafrica.com/stories/200701080951.html |archive-date=30 September 2012 |access-date=11 October 2014 |publisher=Allafrica.com}}</ref> [[Egypt]]'s official news agency has reported that by 30 December (10 Dhu al-Hijjah), 22 Egyptian pilgrims had died.<ref>{{Cite web |title=People's Daily Online–22 Egyptian pilgrims dies during Mecca hajj |url=http://english.people.com.cn/200612/31/eng20061231_337299.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121008192851/http://english.people.com.cn/200612/31/eng20061231_337299.html |archive-date=8 October 2012 |access-date=11 October 2014 |publisher=English.people.com.cn}}</ref> Four Filipino pilgrims in their 50s died during the pilgrimage of illnesses or other 'natural causes', and were buried in Mecca.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Inquirer.net |url=http://newsinfo.inquirer.net/breakingnews/metroregions/view_article.php?article_id=41844 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081215193154/http://newsinfo.inquirer.net/breakingnews/metroregions/view_article.php?article_id=41844 |archive-date=15 December 2008 |access-date=11 October 2014 |publisher=Newsinfo.inq}}</ref> The [[Pakistan]]i Hajj Medical Commission has announced that approximately 130 Pakistani pilgrims died during the Hajj season in Saudi Arabia, "mostly aged and victims of pneumonia and heart patients", and that 66 pilgrims were admitted to Saudi hospitals for similar ailments.
*In early December 2006, a coach bus carrying pilgrims from holy sites in [[Medina]] to Mecca crashed 55 miles north of the port of [[Rabegh]] near [[Jeddah]], killing 3 [[United Kingdom|Britons]] and injuring 34 others, including two children.<ref>{{Cite news |date=10 December 2006 |title=Third Briton died in Jeddah crash |work=BBC News |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/middle_east/6164337.stm |url-status=live |access-date=30 December 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070110210010/http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/world/middle_east/6164337.stm |archive-date=10 January 2007}}</ref>
*In 2009, 77 pilgrims performing Hajj were killed due to [[flood]]s.<ref>{{Cite web |title=77 Muslims killed while performing Hajj by Saudi floods {{!}} TopNews |url=https://topnews.us/content/28575-77-muslims-killed-while-performing-hajj-saudi-floods |access-date=13 March 2020 |website=topnews.us |archive-date=3 April 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200403214810/https://topnews.us/content/28575-77-muslims-killed-while-performing-hajj-saudi-floods |url-status=dead }}</ref>
*In November 2011, thirteen Afghans died and a dozen others were wounded as a result of illness and traffic accidents.<ref>{{Cite news |title=13 Afghan pilgrims die during hajj |date=9 November 2011 |url=http://www.pajhwok.com/en/2011/11/09/13-afghan-pilgrims-die-during-hajj |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141019013339/http://www.pajhwok.com/en/2011/11/09/13-afghan-pilgrims-die-during-hajj |archive-date=19 October 2014 |access-date=11 October 2014 |publisher=Pajhwok.com|last1=Siddiqui |first1=Abdul Qadeer }}</ref>

==Official responses==
[[File:2.5m Hajj pilgrims visited jamarat yesterday, according to Saudi public security monitors - Flickr - Al Jazeera English.jpg|thumb|right|The Saudi government has created a [[closed-circuit television|CCTV]] network to oversee security during the event.]]
Critics{{who|date=September 2026}} say that the Saudi government should have done more to prevent such tragedies. The Saudi government insists that any such mass gatherings are inherently dangerous and difficult to handle, and that they have taken a number of steps to prevent the problems. The fatalities in the largest tragedy in September 2015 are alleged to have been downplayed by the Saudis by as many as 1,700.<ref name= ap_crush />

One of the biggest steps, which is also controversial, is a new system of registrations, passports, and travel visas to control the flow of pilgrims. This system is designed to encourage and accommodate first-time visitors to Mecca and Medina, while restricting repeat visits. Pilgrims who have the means and desire to perform the Hajj several times have protested what they see as discrimination, but the Hajj Commission has stated that they see no alternative if further tragedies are to be prevented.{{citation needed|date=September 2026}}

Following the 2004 stampede, Saudi authorities embarked on major construction work in and around the Jamaraat Bridge area. Additional accessways, footbridges, and emergency exits were built, and the three cylindrical pillars were replaced with concrete walls to enable more pilgrims simultaneous access to them without the jostling and fighting for position of recent years. The government has also announced a multimillion-dollar project to expand the bridge to five levels; the project was planned for completion in time for the [[Islamic calendar|1427 AH]] (December 2006 – January 2007) Hajj.<ref>{{Cite news |date=10 January 2006 |title=Hajj ritual sees new safety moves |work=BBC News |url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/middle_east/4600310.stm |url-status=live |access-date=12 January 2006 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20060112102841/http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/middle_east/4600310.stm |archive-date=12 January 2006}}</ref>
Following the 2006 incident, the Jamaraat Bridge and the pillars representing [[Devil (Islam)|Satan]] were demolished and reconstructed.  A wider, multi-level bridge was built, and massive columns replaced the pillars themselves.  Now, each level of the bridge allows easier and safer access to the columns representing Satan. In addition, the stoning ceremony must be carried out according to pre-determined schedules to prevent over-crowding and the attendant risks. The Jamaraat basin has been expanded from its current circular shape into an oval to allow better access to the pillars. The new arrangements provide for separate access and departure routes.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.hajinformation.com/main/k40.htm|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20140104133445/http://www.hajinformation.com/main/k40.htm|url-status=dead|title=Al-Jamarat Plaza – Hajj|archivedate=January 4, 2014|website=www.hajinformation.com}}</ref>
However, a security breakdown is mentioned as cause for the 2015 stampede.<ref name="premiumtimesng.com">{{Cite news |date=27 September 2015 |title=Hajj tragedy caused by Saudi Arabia's 'criminal negligence' – Nigerian Islamic Group – Premium Times Nigeria |url=http://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/headlines/190686-hajj-tragedy-caused-by-saudi-arabias-criminal-negligence-nigerian-islamic-group.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150928115455/http://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/headlines/190686-hajj-tragedy-caused-by-saudi-arabias-criminal-negligence-nigerian-islamic-group.html |archive-date=28 September 2015 |access-date=19 October 2016|newspaper=[[Premium Times]]}}</ref> A group of pilgrims who had cast their own stones and were returning to their camp, instead of taking the route designated for returning pilgrims, took the route meant for those who were coming and crossed the other group of pilgrims heading straight to the jamaraat.<ref name="premiumtimesng.com" /><ref>{{Cite web |last=Yan |first=Holly |title=What caused the deadly stampede at the Hajj? |date=25 September 2015 |url=http://www.cnn.com/2015/09/25/middleeast/hajj-pilgrimage-stampede/index.html |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160909011352/http://www.cnn.com/2015/09/25/middleeast/hajj-pilgrimage-stampede/index.html |archive-date=9 September 2016 |access-date=19 October 2016 |publisher=CNN}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Report: Road blockade caused hajj stampede |url=http://dailytrust.com.ng/news/general/saudi-gazette-says-road-blockade-cause-of-hajj-tragedy/112732.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151001031850/http://www.dailytrust.com.ng/news/general/saudi-gazette-says-road-blockade-cause-of-hajj-tragedy/112732.html |archive-date=1 October 2015 |access-date=28 September 2015|newspaper=[[Daily Trust]]}}</ref>

== Background ==
{{Main|Hajj}}
[[File:The sprawling Jamarat bridge - Flickr - Al Jazeera English.jpg|thumb|right|The Jamaraat Bridge complex]]
[[File:Some perspective on Mina - Flickr - Al Jazeera English.jpg|thumb|right|Tent city at [[Mina, Saudi Arabia|Mina]] in 2009]]
[[File:2015 Mina Disaster Map.svg|thumb|335px|right|2015 Mina disaster map–Area surrounding streets 204 and 223]]
The [[Hajj]] is an annual pilgrimage in [[Mecca]] prescribed as a [[Fard|duty]] for Muslims to perform at least once during their lifetime if they can afford to do so physically and financially.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Islamic Practices|url=http://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/essays/islamic-practices|work=[[Berkley Center for Religion, Peace, and World Affairs]]|publisher=[[Georgetown University]]|date=n.d.|access-date=6 October 2015|archive-date=7 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151007080821/http://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/essays/islamic-practices|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Long |first=Matthew |title=Islamic Beliefs, Practices, and Cultures |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA86 |year=2010 |access-date=6 October 2015 |publisher=[[Marshall Cavendish]] |location=Singapore |isbn=978-0-7614-7926-0 |page=86 |archive-date=16 October 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151016024055/https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA86 |url-status=live }}</ref> As traditionally performed, the Hajj consists of a series of rites including the [[Stoning of the Devil]] ({{langx|ar|{{large|رمي الجمرات|}}}} ''{{transliteration|ar|ramī al-jamarāt}}'')<ref>{{cite book|last=Burton|first=Richard Francis|author-link=Richard Francis Burton|title=Personal Narrative of a Pilgrimage to El Medinah and Meccah, Volume 2|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=kXcOAAAAQAAJ&pg=PA226|year=1857|publisher=Longman, Brown, Green, Longmans, and Roberts|page=226|quote=The word ''jamrah'' is applied to the place of stoning, as well as to the stones.|access-date=1 October 2015|archive-date=9 April 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170409053008/https://books.google.com/books?id=kXcOAAAAQAAJ&pg=PA226|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite book|first=Abū|last=Dāʼūd|author-link=Abū Dāʼūd|title=Sunan Abu Dawud: Chapters 512–1337|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=SwvYAAAAMAAJ|year=1984|publisher=Sh. M. Ashraf|page=506|isbn=9789694320977 |access-date=1 October 2015|quote=''Jamrah'' originally means a pebble. It is applied to the heap of stones or a pillar.|archive-date=9 April 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170409083218/https://books.google.com/books?id=SwvYAAAAMAAJ|url-status=live}}</ref> which is done at the [[Jamaraat Bridge]] in [[Mina, Saudi Arabia|Mina]], a district a few miles east of Mecca. The Jamaraat Bridge is a pedestrian bridge from which pilgrims can throw pebbles at the three ''jamrah'' ("pebble") pillars. The stoning ritual is the last major ritual and the part of the Hajj with the most potential for disaster given its large crowds, confined spaces, and precise scheduling.<ref>{{cite news|title=Hajj 360°|first1=Basma|last1=Atassi|first2=Mohammed|last2=Haddad|url=http://interactive.aljazeera.com/aje/hajj360/index.html|publisher=[[Al Jazeera English|Al Jazeera]]|date=17 September 2014|access-date=18 December 2015|archive-date=14 December 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151214162024/http://interactive.aljazeera.com/aje/hajj360/index.html|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Stoning of Satan hajj ritual in Mina fraught with ever-present dangers |first1=Edd K. |last1=Usman |url=http://www.mb.com.ph/stoning-of-satan-hajj-ritual-in-mina-fraught-with-ever-present-dangers/ |work=Manilla Bulletin |date=25 September 2014 |access-date=18 December 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151224210218/http://www.mb.com.ph/stoning-of-satan-hajj-ritual-in-mina-fraught-with-ever-present-dangers/ |archive-date=24 December 2015}}</ref>

The 2015 Hajj occurred during a time of regional turmoil (including wars in [[Syrian Civil War|Syria]], [[Iraqi Civil War (2014–2017)|Iraq]], [[Yemeni Civil War (2015–present)|Yemen]], and [[Libyan Civil War (2014–present)|Libya]]), the highest temperatures in Mecca in 20 years and the threat of [[MERS]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.economist.com/blogs/economist-explains/2015/09/economist-explains-14|author=M.R.|title=Why the haj is safer than ever|newspaper=[[The Economist]]|date=21 September 2015|access-date=1 October 2015|archive-date=2 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151002095944/http://www.economist.com/blogs/economist-explains/2015/09/economist-explains-14|url-status=live}}</ref> 

A number of [[Incidents during the Hajj#Stampedes and failures of crowd control|Hajj-related crowd crush disasters]] have occurred in the past,<ref>{{cite news|title=Satan stoned – most dangerous hajj rite|url=http://www.news24.com/World/News/Satan-stoned-most-dangerous-hajj-rite-20111106|publisher=[[News24 (website)|News24]]|access-date=1 October 2015|agency=[[Agence France-Presse]]|date=6 November 2011|archive-date=25 October 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191025193122/https://www.news24.com/World/News/Satan-stoned-most-dangerous-hajj-rite-20111106|url-status=dead}}</ref> with 1,426 people being suffocated and trampled to death in a [[1990 Mecca tunnel tragedy|1990 tunnel tragedy]], and at least 701 people killed in crowd crushes between 1991 and 2005.<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /> 346 people were killed in a [[2006 Hajj stampede|similar Jamaraat incident in 2006]], which prompted the [[Politics of Saudi Arabia|Saudi government]] to improve the infrastructure of the city and its procession routes.<ref name=bbc1>{{cite web|title=Hajj stampede: At least 717 killed in Saudi Arabia|url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-34346449|work=[[BBC News]]|date=24 September 2015|access-date=1 October 2015|archive-date=10 May 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190510185719/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-34346449|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Casualties ===
The exact [[Casualty (person)|casualty]] total for the Mina crowd collapse is disputed. The Saudi government officially reported {{As of|alt=769|2015|09|26}} deaths,<ref name="AP-10-12" /> a number that was not changed, while Iranian media sources have proposed totals as great as 4,173, after a withdrawn early number given by Saudi authorities.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /><ref name=":2" /> Independent estimates range between {{As of|alt=2,236|2015|11|27}}<ref name="AFP-27-11" /> and {{As of|alt=2,431|2015|11|29}} (see [[#Disaster|nationalities of victims]] table) people killed, with the most recent Associated Press estimate giving a death toll of {{As of|alt=2,411|2015|12|10}}, based on "media reports and officials comments from 36 of the over 180 countries that sent citizens to the hajj".<ref name="AP-10-12" />

Estimates of the injured and missing also vary greatly; Saudi reports claim 934 injured;<ref name=bbctoll /> Iranian reports are much higher, estimating more than 2,000 injuries.<ref name="IRNA-28-10">{{cite web|title=Mina tragedy needs impartial probe|url=http://www.irna.ir/en/News/81776427/|agency=[[Islamic Republic News Agency]]|date=28 September 2015|access-date=18 October 2015|archive-date=28 September 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150928171721/http://www.irna.ir/en/News/81776427/|url-status=live}}</ref> On the day of the disaster, the Saudi Civil Defence directorate stated that casualties were of multiple nationalities and announced the deployment of 4,000 personnel to the crush site alongside 220 emergency response units.<ref name=AJE /> Pilgrims were redirected away from the crush site,<ref name=Naar>{{cite news|title=Hajj: More than 700 dead in Mina stampede|first=Ismaeel|last=Naar|url=http://english.alarabiya.net/en/News/middle-east/2015/09/24/Hajj-100-dead-and-390-injured-in-Mina-stampede.html|publisher=[[Al Arabiya]]|date=24 September 2015|access-date=18 October 2015|archive-date=25 September 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150925081903/http://english.alarabiya.net/en/News/middle-east/2015/09/24/Hajj-100-dead-and-390-injured-in-Mina-stampede.html|url-status=live}}</ref> and the [[Saudi Red Crescent Authority]] was mobilised.<ref name=bbc1 /> Medics at Mina's emergency hospital said they alone received almost 700 people on the day of the incident. The eight hospitals around the Hajj landmarks and the six main hospitals in the city of Mecca were operating at full capacity after the crush, medics said.<ref name="aljazeera.com">{{cite web|url=http://www.aljazeera.com/blogs/middleeast/2015/09/human-cost-hajj-stampede-150926133507886.html|title=The human cost of the Hajj stampede|first=Basma|last=Atassi|publisher=[[Al Jazeera English|Al Jazeera]]|date=26 September 2015|access-date=2 October 2015|archive-date=4 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151004062812/http://www.aljazeera.com/blogs/middleeast/2015/09/human-cost-hajj-stampede-150926133507886.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

By 2 October 2015, the Saudi Arabia Health Ministry stated they had completed the [[DNA profiling]] of all unidentified pilgrims who were killed or injured in the crush.<ref>{{Cite news|title=Hajj stampede: Saudi completes DNA profiling of victims|url=http://www.dailytrust.com.ng/news/general/hajj-stampede-saudi-completes-dna-profiling-of-victims/113438.html|first=Abbas|last=Jimoh|date=2 October 2015|access-date=2 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151003181845/http://www.dailytrust.com.ng/news/general/hajj-stampede-saudi-completes-dna-profiling-of-victims/113438.html|archive-date=3 October 2015|url-status=dead|newspaper=[[Daily Trust]]}}</ref> DNA samples of the next of kin of Hajj stampede victims were collected at Al-Nour Specialist Hospital, Mecca.<ref>{{cite news|title=DNA sampling for Hajj stampede victims begins|first=Ashwaq|last=Al-Tuwairqi|url=http://english.alarabiya.net/en/News/middle-east/2015/10/04/DNA-sampling-for-Hajj-stampede-victims-begins.html|work=[[Saudi Gazette]]|via=[[Al Arabiya News]]|date=4 October 2015|access-date=15 October 2015|archive-date=6 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151006035112/http://english.alarabiya.net/en/News/middle-east/2015/10/04/DNA-sampling-for-Hajj-stampede-victims-begins.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

== Eyewitness accounts ==
Early statements by eyewitnesses indicated that the crush was caused by the closure of the eastern part of Street 206, which forced pilgrims to travel on Street 223, colliding with a mass of people moving the opposite direction on Street 204.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Closure of Street 206 blamed for the tragedy|url=https://en-maktoob.news.yahoo.com/closure-street-206-blamed-tragedy-062003489.html|work=[[Saudi Gazette]]|via=[[Yahoo! News]] Maktoob|agency=SyndiGate Media|date=26 September 2015|access-date=13 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160305105936/https://en-maktoob.news.yahoo.com/closure-street-206-blamed-tragedy-062003489.html|archive-date=5 March 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref>

Alhaji Samaila Dabai Yombe, Deputy Governor of the Nigerian state of [[Kebbi State|Kebbi]], who was present at the incident, stated that the deaths happened due to a blockage of the route to Jamaraat Bridge. 
<blockquote>
What actually happened was that all the pilgrims scheduled to [[Stoning of the Devil|throw Jamrat]] at that time were channeled to one particular street. At a time we got to a certain point around 8:00{{nbs}}a.m., a military vehicle was set across to create a barrier and then some of the Saudi soldiers were standing by, suggesting that you cannot go beyond that point. [...] About 5,000{{nbs}}people coming from the same direction were not aware of the road block in front, which resulted to a tight and stationary human traffic, which made it very difficult for us to even stand. So, we continued to squat to make room for fresh air while the temperature was about 47 degrees Celsius. [...] Pilgrims, in efforts to get fresh air, attempted to scale fences of tents on both sides of the road. Very few succeeded, while most people just succumb to the situation. It was at this juncture that we saw dead bodies piling up around us.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Hajj Tragedy: Saudis lied, no stampede, says Nigerian deputy governor|url=http://thenewsnigeria.com.ng/2015/09/hajj-tragedy-saudis-lied-no-stampede-says-nigerian-deputy-governor/|work=The News (Nigeria)|date=27 September 2015|access-date=13 October 2015|archive-date=28 September 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150928091417/http://thenewsnigeria.com.ng/2015/09/hajj-tragedy-saudis-lied-no-stampede-says-nigerian-deputy-governor/|url-status=live}}</ref>
</blockquote>

Ishaq Akintola, a Nigerian Professor of [[Islamic eschatology]], gave an eyewitness account of the disaster: 
<blockquote>
on that fateful day, we found out that some of those who had thrown their own stones made a U-turn instead of moving ahead to take a detour. They came through the route meant for entrance and not exit. They came towards us. They were in a very large group and the road was not spacious enough to allow a free flow of those of us coming to throw stones at the Devil and those who had stoned the Devil. The road could not take those coming and those going. And I discovered that most of those who took the wrong way were Egyptians [...] as I heard them speak the Egyptian dialect of Arabic. [...] I studied in Egypt for five years. I know the dialect.<ref>{{Cite news|title=How Nigerians died in Hajj stampede—Survivor, Prof. Akintola |first=Bayo |last=Akinloye |url=http://www.punchng.com/feature/interview/how-nigerians-died-in-hajj-stampede-survivor-prof-akintola/  |date=4 October 2015 |access-date=13 October 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151007233647/http://www.punchng.com/feature/interview/how-nigerians-died-in-hajj-stampede-survivor-prof-akintola/ |archive-date=7 October 2015|newspaper=[[The Punch]]}}</ref>
</blockquote>

=== Recommendations by independent experts ===
Najmedin Meshkati, Professor of Engineering and International Relations at the [[University of Southern California]], who is an expert in accident investigation, recommended in a self-authored opinion piece in the ''World Post'' that, 
<blockquote>
the Saudi government should embark on the immediate creation of an independent investigation commission/panel. This interdisciplinary commission/panel should be chaired by a nationally renowned Saudi statesman or scholar, with members selected from Saudi Arabia and affected countries based on their technical expertise and to include responsible governmental entities, first responder agencies, and academics of requisite disciplines for accident investigation. [...] And it should be empowered by the subpoena power and charged to conduct a comprehensive, systematic and interdisciplinary investigation by employing the system-oriented, robust 'AcciMap'{{what|date=September 2026}} methodology to write the most technically-sound report on the root-causes of this tragedy.<ref name="Meshkati">{{Cite web|title=The Hajj (Mina) Stampede Tragedy in Saudi Arabia, and How to Respond|first=Najmedin|last=Meshkati|url=http://www.huffingtonpost.com/najmedin-meshkati/hajj-mina-stampede-traged_b_8224830.html|work=[[Huffington Post|The World Post]]|date=1 October 2015|access-date=20 October 2015|archive-date=9 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151009050255/http://www.huffingtonpost.com/najmedin-meshkati/hajj-mina-stampede-traged_b_8224830.html|url-status=live}}</ref>
</blockquote>

Amer Shalaby is a Professor of Civil Engineering at the [[University of Toronto]]'s Transportation Research Institute, former Hajj pilgrim, and consultant to Saudi government on crowd management. Shalaby, who specializes in transportation planning for large-scale events, proposes using "smart phones and other static and dynamic sensors" to provide authorities real-time information that could identify potential trouble places during the Hajj. He also suggests that "some of the effective methods of highway traffic flow management, such as flow metering, could be adapted for streamlining crowd flows in Mecca".<ref>{{cite news|title=Professor Amer Shalaby on improving transportation at the world's largest gathering|first=Emily|last=Meyertholen|url=http://news.engineering.utoronto.ca/navigating-the-pilgrimage-to-mecca-professor-amer-shalaby-on-improving-transportation-at-the-worlds-largest-gathering/|publisher=[[University of Toronto]]|date=16 December 2015|access-date=16 December 2015|archive-date=22 December 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151222120228/http://news.engineering.utoronto.ca/navigating-the-pilgrimage-to-mecca-professor-amer-shalaby-on-improving-transportation-at-the-worlds-largest-gathering/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Keith Still, Professor of Crowd Science at [[Manchester Metropolitan University]] in Britain, who helped redesign the Jamaraat (the pillars representing the devil stoned by pilgrims) after a disaster in 2004, said there was criticism at the time that the upgrades at the Jamaraat had not been extended to other areas. He said "For complex systems that flow in and out, if you make one change along the way it can have knock-on and ripple effects elsewhere. Change any one part of system with 3 million people, and there's a danger of an accident like this."<ref name=Paul30Sept />

Mohammed Ajmal, a physician specializing in [[Emergency Medicine]] and former manager of a medical center established to treat Indian Hajj pilgrims, details issues with both the design of the Mina tent city and quick access to disaster medical care at the site. Ajmal discussed structural design flaws in Mina, calling it a "badly designed death trap—in times of disaster" as it attempts to funnel tens of thousands of people through [[Three-way junction|T intersections]], such as the junction of Streets 204 and 223 where the crush occurred. [[Asphyxia#Compressive asphyxia|Compression asphyxia]] is the cause of death for most victims of a crowd collapse, and can occur within ten minutes; Ajmal notes that Mina's design prevents medical care from arriving within that time stating that people at crush sites are "destined to die" within minutes, before any medical help can reach the site.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Mina Stampeded: Could it have been avoided!|first=Mohammed|last=Ajmal|url=https://www.greaterkashmir.com/todays-paper/mina-stampeded-could-it-have-been-avoided|work=[[Greater Kashmir]]|date=5 October 2015|access-date=8 October 2015|archive-date=23 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210723113624/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/todays-paper/mina-stampeded-could-it-have-been-avoided|url-status=live}}</ref>

Amid deteriorating diplomatic relations and after months of negotiations concerning visa requirements and safety logistics, in May 2016 Iran suspended participation with the upcoming Hajj scheduled for September 2016.<ref name=Schemm12May>{{cite news|url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/iran-suspends-participation-in-annual-hajj-pilgrimage-as-relations-with-saudi-plummet/2016/05/12/a0e1c970-1848-11e6-924d-838753295f9a_story.html|title=Iran suspends participation in the hajj as relations with Saudi Arabia plummet|date=12 May 2016|access-date=12 May 2016|newspaper=The Washington Post|first=Paul|last=Schemm|archive-date=13 May 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160513120842/https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/iran-suspends-participation-in-annual-hajj-pilgrimage-as-relations-with-saudi-plummet/2016/05/12/a0e1c970-1848-11e6-924d-838753295f9a_story.html|url-status=live}}</ref> Iran previously suspended participation with the Hajj of 1988 and 1989, after 400 Iranian pilgrims were killed by Saudi security forces.<ref name=Schemm12May /> On 18 May 2025, after a decade Saudi Arabia resumed Hajj flight from Iran into the kingdom.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Saudi airline resumes first Hajj flights with Iran since 2015: Official |url=https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/mena/saudi-airline-resumes-hajj-flights-with-iran |access-date=2025-05-18 |website=Khaleej Times |language=en}}</ref>

== Description as "stampede" ==
{{more|Crowd collapses and crushes}}{{See also|Stampede}}
Some academics who study crowd movements and crushing disasters have questioned the use of the term "stampede".<ref name=Benedictus3Oct>{{cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/oct/03/hajj-crush-how-crowd-disasters-happen-and-how-they-can-be-avoided|title=Hajj crush: how crowd disasters happen, and how they can be avoided|work=The Guardian|date=3 October 2015|access-date=4 October 2015|first=Leo|last=Benedictus|archive-date=2 July 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190702223626/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/oct/03/hajj-crush-how-crowd-disasters-happen-and-how-they-can-be-avoided|url-status=live}}</ref> "The rhetoric of 'stampede' is often used to imply that the crowd is animalistic or mindless, but from a [[crowd psychology]] point of view, I'm sure that there was a logical explanation for the crush", [[University of Sussex]] crowd behavioral expert Anne Templeton told ''Newsweek''. "The density of the Hajj has been shown to reach up to 6–8 people per square meter, so I would be very surprised if a stampede (implying people running mindlessly) could occur in the first place".<ref name=Moore24Sept>{{cite news|date=24 September 2015|access-date=8 October 2015|work=[[Newsweek]]|url=http://www.newsweek.com/what-caused-hajj-tragedy-376267|title=What Caused the Hajj Tragedy?|first=Jack|last=Moore|archive-date=15 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151015223515/http://www.newsweek.com/what-caused-hajj-tragedy-376267|url-status=live}}</ref>

The academics argue that the Mina disaster is better described as a "progressive crowd collapse":<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /><ref name=Moore24Sept /> beginning at densities of about six<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /> to seven<ref name=Seabrook>{{cite magazine|first=John|last=Seabrook|magazine=[[The New Yorker]]|url=https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/02/07/crush-point|title=Crush Point|date=7 February 2011|access-date=8 October 2015|archive-date=26 May 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180526191658/https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/02/07/crush-point|url-status=live}}</ref> persons per square meter, individuals are pressed so closely against each other they are unable to move as individuals, and shockwaves can travel through a crowd which, at such densities, behaves somewhat like a fluid.<ref name=Seabrook /> If a single person falls, or other people reach down to help, waves of bodies can be involuntarily precipitated forward into the open space.<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /> One such shockwave can create other openings in the crowd nearby, precipitating further crushing.<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /> [[Asphyxia|Unable to draw breath]], people in a crowd can also be crushed while standing.<ref name=Seabrook />

Use of the word "stampede", says Edwin Galea of the [[University of Greenwich]], is the result of "pure ignorance and laziness ... it gives the impression that it was a mindless crowd only caring about themselves, and they were prepared to crush people."<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /> In reality, "people are only directly crushed by others who have no choice, and the people who can choose ... are too far away from the epicentre" to be aware of what is happening.<ref name=Benedictus3Oct /> According to experts, true "stampedes" (and "panics") rarely occur except when a crowd is fleeing in fear, such as from a fire,<ref name=Seabrook/> and rarely does trampling by other human beings in such "stampede" conditions result in fatal injuries.<ref name=Benedictus3Oct />

"If you look at the analysis, I’ve not seen any instances of the cause of mass fatalities being a stampede," says Keith Still, professor of crowd science at Manchester Metropolitan University. "People don't die because they panic. They panic because they are dying."<ref name=Benedictus3Oct />

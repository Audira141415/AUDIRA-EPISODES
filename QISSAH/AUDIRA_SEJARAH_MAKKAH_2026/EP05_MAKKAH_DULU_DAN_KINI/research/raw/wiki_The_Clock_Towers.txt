'''The Clock Towers''' ({{langx|ar|أبراج الساعة|translit=ʾAbrāj as-Sāʿaẗ|lit=Towers of the Clock}}), formerly known as '''Abraj al-Bait''' ({{langx|ar|أبراج البيت|translit=ʾAbrāj al-Bayt|lit=Towers of the House|links=no}}), is a [[State ownership|government-owned]] hotel complex with seven [[skyscraper]] [[hotel]]s in [[Mecca]], [[Saudi Arabia]]. These towers are part of the King Abdulaziz Endowment Project that aims to modernize the city in catering to its pilgrims. The central hotel tower, which is the '''Mecca Royal Clock Tower''' ({{langx|ar|برج ساعة مكة الملكي|translit=Burj Sāʿat Makkaẗ al-Malikī|links=no}}), is the fourth-[[List of tallest buildings|tallest building]] and sixth-[[List of tallest freestanding structures|tallest freestanding structure in the world]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Tallest Freestanding Structures |url=https://skyscraperpage.com/diagrams/?searchID=206 |access-date=July 19, 2023 |publisher=Skyscraper Source Media}}</ref> According to [[Guinness World Records]], the Mecca Royal Clock Tower is the tallest clock tower in the world, and the complex of seven buildings comprise the world's second most expensive building.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Tallest Clock Tower |url=https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/683873-tallest-clock-tower |website=Guinness World Records}}</ref> The clock faces are the [[List of largest clock faces|largest in the world]], and the top four floors of the clock tower house the [[Clock Tower Museum]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=11 May 2019 |title=Clock tower museum in Makkah thrown open to visitors |url=http://saudigazette.com.sa/article/566092/SAUDI-ARABIA/Clock-tower-museum-in-Makkah-thrown-open-to-visitors |access-date=16 September 2019 |website=Saudigazette |language=en}}</ref> 

The building complex is {{convert|300|m}} away from the world's largest [[mosque]] and [[Islam]]'s [[Holiest sites in Islam|most sacred site]], the [[Great Mosque of Mecca]].<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|last=Bsheer|first=Rosie|date=20 December 2020|title=How Saudi Arabia obliterated its rich cultural history|url=http://www.middleeasteye.net/opinion/saudi-arabia-obliterated-rich-cultural-history|url-status=live|access-date=2022-01-17|website=Middle East Eye|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201023040028/https://www.middleeasteye.net/opinion/saudi-arabia-obliterated-rich-cultural-history |archive-date=23 October 2020 }}</ref> The developer and contractor of the complex is the [[Saudi Binladin Group]], the Kingdom's largest [[construction company]].<ref name="skyscraperCenter" /> The total cost of construction totaled US$15 billion. The complex was built after the demolition of the [[Ajyad Fortress]], the 18th-century [[Ottoman Empire|Ottoman]] citadel on top of a hill overlooking the Grand Mosque.<ref>{{cite news|title=Historic Fortress Destroyed|url=https://www.nytimes.com/2002/01/09/world/world-briefing-middle-east-saudi-arabia-historic-fortress-destroyed.html|access-date=6 September 2012|newspaper=The New York Times|date=9 January 2002}}</ref>

==Description==
[[File:Abraj al bait Clock 3.jpg|thumb|left|The Mecca Royal Clock Tower's clocks, the largest in the world]]
The tallest tower in the complex is the [[List of tallest buildings in Saudi Arabia|tallest building in Saudi Arabia]], at {{convert|601|m|ft|abbr=on}} and a total surface area of 32,000 square meters. It is the sixth-tallest freestanding structure in the world, surpassing the [[Ping An Finance Centre]] in [[Shenzhen|Shenzhen, China]] but shorter than the [[Burj Khalifa]] in [[Dubai|Dubai, UAE]]; the [[Tokyo Skytree]] in [[Tokyo, Japan]]; the [[Shanghai Tower]] in [[Shanghai, China]]; and the [[Canton Tower]] in [[Guangzhou, China]].{{cn|date=September 2025|reason=Internally inconsistent and partially contradicted elsewhere}}

The site of the complex is across the piazza to the south from the main entrance (King Abdul-Aziz Gate) to the [[Masjid al-Haram|Masjid al Haram mosque]], which houses the [[Kaaba]]. It accommodates worshippers visiting the Kaaba. The tallest tower in the complex also contains a five-star hotel, operated by Fairmont Hotels and Resorts, to help provide lodging for the millions of pilgrims that travel to Mecca annually to participate in the Hajj.

In addition, The Clock Towers has a five-story shopping mall (The Clock Towers Shopping Center) and a parking garage capable of holding over a thousand vehicles.

The building was planned to be {{convert|734|m|ft|abbr=on}} tall in 2006. In 2009, it was published that the final height would be {{convert|601|m|ft|abbr=on}}. The complex was built by the Saudi Binladin Group, Saudi Arabia's largest construction company. The façade was constructed by Premiere Composite Technologies, and the clock by German tower clock manufacturer PERROT GmbH & Co. KG Turmuhren und Läuteanlagen. Because of its location in Mecca, non-Muslim members of the construction team were not allowed to visit the site, and some specialists from Poland and Germany converted to Islam to be able to do so.<ref>{{cite web |title=Saudi Arabia Built a $16BN Clock Tower|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2gwrSaNSl00 |website=youtube.com |publisher=The B1M |language=en |format=video |date= September 25, 2024|access-date=December 9, 2025}}</ref> According to the Saudi Ministry of Religious Endowments, the overall project cost US$15 billion.

=== Clock ===
There are clock faces on all four sides of the main hotel tower. The total height of the clock is {{convert|57|m|ft|abbr=on}}, situated above the media displays under the clock faces. At {{convert|43|×|43|m|ft|abbr=on}}, these are the largest in the world. The roof of the clock is {{convert|450|m|ft|abbr=on}} above the ground, making it the world's most elevated architectural clock. A spire has been added on top of the clock giving it a total height of {{convert|601|m|ft|abbr=on}}. Behind the clock faces, there is an astronomy exhibition. In the spire base and the iron-covered floors (The Jewel) there is a scientific center that is used to sight the moon at the beginning of the Islamic months, and to operate an atomic clock that controls the tower clock faces.

==Features==
[[File:Abraj al bait closeup.jpg|thumb|left|The Clock Towers' architecture has fine detail]]
{{stack|[[File:Abraj al bait closeup 2.jpg|thumb|left|upright|The Clock Towers' cladding]]|}}
The building is topped by a four-faced clock, visible from {{convert|25|km|abbr=off}} away. The clock is the highest in the world at over {{convert|400|m|abbr=on}} above the ground, surpassing the [[Rockwell Automation Headquarters and Allen-Bradley Clock Tower|Allen-Bradley clock tower]] in [[Milwaukee]]. The clock faces are the largest in the world, surpassing the [[Istanbul Cevahir|Cevahir Mall]] clock in [[Istanbul]].

Each of the clock's four faces measures {{convert|43|m|ft|abbr=on}} in diameter and are illuminated by 2 million LED lights, with four oriented edges, just above the clock alongside huge Arabic script reading: "[[Takbir|God is [the] greatest]]" on the north and south faces and on the west and east "[[Shahada|There is no god but Allah. Muhammad is the messenger of God.]]" Four golden domes on pillars on all the corners are also present. The same as the [[Saudi flag]], fitted at the top of the clock, flash to signal Islam's five-time daily prayers.{{huh?|date=October 2026}} On special occasions such as new year, 21,000 green and white xenon bulbs and LED lamps {{huh?|reason=missing verb: "light up"?|date=October 2026}} during the call to the five prayers times of the day. On special occasions, 40 beacon lights create lighting effects. In addition, strong lasers throw their rays 30 kilometers into the sky. The clock's four faces are covered with 98 million pieces of glass mosaics. The [[Emblem of Saudi Arabia|Saudi coat of arms]] is displayed at the center of each clock behind the dials. The minute hand is {{convert|23|m|ft|abbr=on}} long, while the hour hand is {{convert|18|m|ft|abbr=on}} long.

There were reports that the clock would be set to [[Mecca Time]], in an attempt to replace the IERS Reference Meridian as the prime meridian for global time keeping, but the clock is set to Arabia Standard Time ([[UTC+03:00]]).

===Spire===
[[File:Abraj al bait Clock 1.jpg|thumb|left|upright|The Clock Towers' clock and spire]]
{{comparison four face clocks.jpg}}
The spire has an eight-story glass-covered base (The Jewel) which belongs to a scientific center having its own small exhibition, another observation deck at {{convert|484|m|ft|abbr=on}}. The highest floor in The Jewel is the Control Tower Floor, which was planned to be used for controlling air traffic in the sky above Mecca (mainly helicopters, as airplanes are not allowed near Mecca). However, this was skipped for technical reasons and the future usage is not clear yet. Above from The Jewel, the spire has only technical installations for sound, light, and other infrastructure and eventually the last viewing deck and the crescent above it. The crescent is {{cvt|21|m}} in diameter, has two regular floors with living areas, a prayer room in the center of the crescent<ref>{{cite web | url=https://english.alarabiya.net/variety/2016/12/25/Highest-Muslim-prayer-room-located-in-Mecca-s-Royal-Clock-Tower | title=Highest Muslim prayer room located in Mecca's Royal Clock Tower | date=25 December 2016 }}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/case-studies/a4579-makkah-royal-clock-tower-by-sl-rasch-gmbh-and-dar-al-handasah-architects-tallest-building-in-saudi-arabia/# | title=Makkah Royal Clock Tower by SL Rasch GMBH and Dar Al-Handasah Architects: Tallest building in Saudi Arabia - RTF | date=14 July 2021 }}</ref> and a few service floors and rooms.

The crescent was constructed in [[Dubai]] in April 2011. It is made of fiberglass-backed [[mosaic gold]], and it weighs up to 35 tonnes. It cost 90 million [[United Arab Emirates dirham]]s and took three months to build.

The minaret and its base have loudspeakers which broadcast prayer calls to a distance of {{cvt|7|km}} away and across an area of approximately {{cvt|160|km2}}.

==Incidents==
===Construction fires===
The Clock Towers complex had two fire incidents during construction. The first fire accident was at Hajar Tower on 28 October 2008. It took 400 firefighters to put out the fire, which burned for 10 hours, consuming nine floors of the tower.<ref name="Sheqdar">Ibtisam Sheqdar: [http://archive.arabnews.com/?page=1&section=0&article=122093&d=1&m=5&y=2009 "Fire damages Makkah tower"] {{webarchive |url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120616055810/http://archive.arabnews.com/?page=1&section=0&article=122093&d=1&m=5&y=2009 |date=16 June 2012 }} Arab News, 1 May 2009</ref> According to eyewitness reports, the blaze erupted shortly after midnight, and spread rapidly because of wood used for construction stored in the premises. Soon, the entire building was engulfed in smoke. Hospitals were put on high alert, but no injuries were reported. A civil defence spokesman claimed the fire started on the 32nd floor of the Hajar Tower.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Al Hakeem |first=Mariam |date=2008-10-21 |title=Makkah hotel fire under investigation |url=https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/makkah-hotel-fire-under-investigation-1.138098 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230420203300/https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/makkah-hotel-fire-under-investigation-1.138098 |archive-date=2023-04-20 |access-date=2024-03-08 |website=Gulf News |language=en}}</ref>

The second fire struck the Safa Tower on 1 May 2009. No deaths or injuries were reported in the blaze which was quickly contained by Civil Defence. Eyewitnesses said the fire broke out soon after [[Asr prayer]] while some workers in the building were welding iron rods on wooden scaffoldings. The fire damaged a large part of the under-construction tower. According to Major General Adel Zamzami, director general of Civil Defence in the Mecca province, the fire broke out at the 14th floor and reached up to the 20th floor.<ref name="Sheqdar"/>

===Controversy===
The [[Destruction of early Islamic heritage sites in Saudi Arabia|destruction of the historically significant site]] in 2002 by the [[Saudi government]] sparked an outcry and a strong reaction from Turkey.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.arabnews.com/node/217526|title=Historic Makkah fortress demolished|publisher=Arab News|date=9 January 2002|access-date=7 May 2012}}</ref><ref name=":0" /> The location chosen for the towers was the historic 18th-century [[Ottoman Empire|Ottoman]]-era [[Ajyad Fortress]]. The fortress was demolished to make way for the complex.<ref>[https://web.archive.org/web/20080407052040/http://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/middle-east/shame-of-the-house-of-saud-shadows-over-mecca-474736.html ‘Shame of the House of Saud: Shadows over Mecca’], ''The Independent'', 19 April 2006</ref> The development has been criticized by ''[[The Guardian]]'' for having "transformed a type of architecture that evolved from a dense urban grain of low-rise courtyards and narrow streets into ... an endlessly repeatable pattern for the decoration of standardized [concrete] slab(s)".<ref>{{Cite web |date=2012-10-23 |title=Mecca's mega architecture casts shadow over hajj as one Islamic rituals of a mandatory obligation that willing or capable |url=http://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2012/oct/23/mecca-architecture-hajj1 |access-date=2022-11-16 |website=the Guardian |language=en}}</ref>

'''Hajj''' ({{langx|ar|حَجّ|translit=Ḥajj}} {{IPA|ar|ħaddʒ|}}; also spelled '''Hadj''' or '''Haj'''){{refn|group=n|{{IPAc-en|h|ɑː|dʒ|,_|h|æ|dʒ}}<ref>[http://dictionary.reference.com/browse/hajj "Hajj"] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141230150928/http://dictionary.reference.com/browse/hajj |date=30 December 2014 }}. ''[[Random House Webster's Unabridged Dictionary]]''.</ref><ref>{{Cite web |date=2026-01-14 |title=hajj |url=https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/hajj |access-date=2026-01-16 |website=dictionary.cambridge.org |language=en}}</ref>}} is an annual [[Islamic pilgrimage]] to [[Mecca]], [[Saudi Arabia]],<ref name="Modarresi">{{cite book |author=Mohammad Taqi al-Modarresi |author-link=Mohammad Taqi al-Modarresi |title=The Laws of Islam |date=26 March 2016 |publisher=Enlight Press |location=San Bernardino|isbn=978-0-9942409-8-9 |url=http://almodarresi.com/en/books/pdf/TheLawsofIslam.pdf |access-date=22 December 2017 |ref=Modarresi |language=en |page=471 |archive-date=2 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190802163247/http://almodarresi.com/en/books/pdf/TheLawsofIslam.pdf |url-status=dead }}</ref> the [[Holiest sites in Islam|holiest city]] in [[Islam]]. Hajj is a one-time-required [[Fard|religious duty]] for all [[Muslims]] who are physically and financially capable of undertaking the journey and supporting their family during their absence from home.<ref name=Marshall86>{{cite book |last=Long |first=Matthew |title=Islamic Beliefs, Practices, and Cultures |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA86 |access-date=2 September 2014 |year=2011 |location=Tarrytown, N.Y.|publisher=Marshall Cavendish Corporation |isbn=978-0-7614-7926-0 |page=86 |archive-date=16 October 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151016024055/https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA86 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last=Nigosian |first=S. A. |title=Islam: Its History, Teaching, and Practices |url=https://archive.org/details/islamitshistoryt0000nigo |url-access=registration |year=2004 |publisher=Indiana University Press |location=Bloomington, Indiana |isbn=0-253-21627-3 |page=[https://archive.org/details/islamitshistoryt0000nigo/page/110 110]}}</ref><ref>[http://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/resources/traditions/islam Berkley Center for Religion, Peace, and World Affairs - Islam] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111002114437/http://berkleycenter.georgetown.edu/resources/traditions/islam |date=2 October 2011 }} See drop-down essay on "Islamic Practices"</ref>

In Islamic terminology, Hajj is a pilgrimage made to the [[Kaaba]]—the "House of [[Allah]]"—in the sacred city of Mecca. It is one of the [[Five Pillars of Islam]], alongside the {{lang|ar-latn|[[Shahadah]]}} (oath that one believes there is no god but Allah and [[Muhammad]] is the [[Muhammad in Islam|Messenger of Allah]]),<ref>{{cite web | url=https://quran.com/6/19 | title=Surah Al-An'am - 19 }}</ref> {{lang|ar-latn|[[salah]]}} (prayer), {{lang|ar-latn|[[zakah]]}} (almsgiving), and {{lang|ar-latn|[[sawm]]}} (fasting during [[Ramadan]]). The Hajj is an annual practice when Muslim brotherhood is on display and their solidarity with fellow Muslims and submission to [[God in Islam|God]] (Allah) is fulfilled.<ref name=Nigosian111/><ref>{{cite book |last=Hooker |first=M. B. |title=Indonesian Syariah: Defining a National School of Islamic Law |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=-a1k57q6GmwC&pg=PT252 |access-date=6 October 2014 |year=2008 |publisher=Institute of Southeast Asian Studies |isbn=978-981-230-802-3 |page=228 |archive-date=17 October 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151017181447/https://books.google.com/books?id=-a1k57q6GmwC&pg=PT252 |url-status=live }}</ref> The Hajj is taken by Muslims to cleanse their souls of all worldly sins, which connotes both the outward act of a journey after death and the inward act of good intentions.<ref name=Adelowo395>{{cite book |editor1-first=E. Dada |editor1-last=Adelowo |title=Perspectives in Religious Studies: Volume III |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ma-QBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA395 |year=2014 |publisher=HEBN Publishers Plc |location=Ibadan |isbn=978-978-081-447-2 |page=395 |access-date=12 August 2015 |archive-date=28 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151128170823/https://books.google.com/books?id=ma-QBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA395 |url-status=live }}</ref> The rites of pilgrimage are performed over five to six days, extending from the 8th to the 12th or 13th<ref>{{citation |url=http://www.hajjpracticalities.heliohost.org/sixdayshajj/13th_of_zil_hajj.html |title=13th of Zil Hajj |publisher=heliohost.org |access-date=29 March 2015 |archive-date=28 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191028105128/http://www.hajjpracticalities.heliohost.org/sixdayshajj/13th_of_zil_hajj.html |url-status=live }}</ref> of [[Dhu'l-Hijja]], the last month of the [[Islamic calendar]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Hajj The Holy Pilgrimage|url=https://salamislam.com/articles/practical-principles/hajj-holy-pilgrimage|website=Salamislam|date=3 January 2021|access-date=31 May 2022|archive-date=31 May 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220531214408/https://salamislam.com/articles/practical-principles/hajj-holy-pilgrimage|url-status=live}}</ref> Because the Islamic calendar is [[lunar calendar|lunar]] and the Islamic year is about eleven or twelve days shorter than the [[Gregorian calendar|Gregorian]] year, the Gregorian date of Hajj changes from year to year. In 2024 AD (1445 [[Anno Hegirae|AH]]), Dhu'l-Hijja extended from 7 June to 6 July and in 2025 AD (1446 AH), Dhu'l-Hijja was from 28 May to 25 June. In 2026 AD (1447 AH), Dhu'l-Hijja extended from 18 May to 15 June; and in 2027 AD (1448 AH), Dhu'l-Hijja will extend from 8 May to 5 June.

The Hajj is associated with the life of the [[Prophets of Islam|Islamic prophet]] [[Muhammad in Islam|Muhammad]] from the 7th century AD, but the ritual of pilgrimage to Mecca stated in Muslim sources stretches back to the time of [[Abraham in Islam|Abraham]]. During Hajj, pilgrims join processions of millions of Muslims, who simultaneously converge on Mecca for the week of the Hajj, and perform a series of pre-Islamic rituals (reformed by Muhammad): each person wears a single piece of unstitched white clothing ([[Ihram]]), walks counter-clockwise seven times around the [[Kaaba]] (Stone-made clothed building which is the [[Qibla|direction of prayer]] for Muslims), kisses the [[Black Stone]] mounted on the corner wall of the Kaaba, walks briskly back and forth between the hills of [[Safa and Marwah]] seven times, then drinks from the [[Zamzam Well]], goes to the plains of [[Mount Arafat]] to stand in vigil, spends a night in the plain of [[Muzdalifah]], and performs symbolic [[Stoning of the Devil]] by throwing stones at three pillars. After the sacrifice of cattle (which can be accomplished by using a voucher), the pilgrims then are required to either shave or trim their heads (if male) or trim the ends of their hair (if female). A celebration of the four-day global festival of [[Eid al-Adha]] follows.<ref name="armstrong">{{cite book |title=Islam: A Short History |author=Karen Armstrong |series=Modern Library Chronicles |publisher=Modern Library |edition=Revised Updated |year=2002 |pages=10–12 |isbn=0-8129-6618-X}}</ref><ref name="bbc.co.uk">{{cite web |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/religion/religions/islam/holydays/eiduladha.shtml |title=Eid ul Adha |date=7 September 2009 |publisher=BBC |access-date=30 December 2012 |archive-date=4 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191004075944/http://www.bbc.co.uk/religion/religions/islam/holydays/eiduladha.shtml |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>Sahih Bukhari-hadith No-732-733</ref> Muslims may also undertake an [[Umrah]] ({{langx|ar|عُمرَة}}), or "lesser pilgrimage" to Mecca at other times of the year. However, the Umrah is not a substitute for the Hajj and Muslims are still obliged to perform the Hajj at some other point in their lifetime if they have the means to do so.<ref>{{cite book |editor=Matt Stefon |title=Islamic Beliefs and Practices |url=https://archive.org/details/islamicbeliefspr0000stef |url-access=registration |publisher=[[Britannica Educational Publishing]] |year=2010 |location=New York City |isbn=978-1-61530-060-0 |page=[https://archive.org/details/islamicbeliefspr0000stef/page/73 73]}}</ref>

According to the official published statistics between 2000 and 2019,<ref name="stats1">{{cite web|year=2017|title=Haj Statistics|url=https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/28|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180620024237/https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/28|archive-date=20 June 2018|website=General Authority for Statistics, Kingdom of Saudi Arabia}}</ref><ref name="RESA1997" /><ref name=":5" /> the average number of attendees is 2,269,145 per year, of whom 1,564,710 come from outside Saudi Arabia and 671,983 are domestic pilgrims. The year 2012 marks the highest number of participants with 3,161,573.<ref name=":6" /> In June 2020, while not cancelling the Hajj outright, the Saudi Government announced that they would only welcome [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Hajj|"very limited numbers" of pilgrims]] who are residents of Saudi Arabia due to the global [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref name="New York Times">{{Cite news|date=22 June 2020|title=Saudi Arabia will sharply limit Hajj Pilgrimage|department=Live Global Coronavirus News |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2020/06/22/world/coronavirus-updates.html|url-status=usurped|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200622210854/https://www.nytimes.com/2020/06/22/world/coronavirus-updates.html|archive-date=22 June 2020|access-date=2020-06-15|work=[[The New York Times]]|language=en}}</ref> Similar restrictions applied in 2021, but women were permitted to attend without a male guardian (''[[mahram]]'') provided they went in a trustworthy group.<ref>{{cite news|author=AFP|title=In Mecca, Women Take Part in Hajj as 'Guardian' Rule Dropped|url=https://www.voanews.com/a/middle-east_mecca-women-take-part-hajj-guardian-rule-dropped/6208472.html|publisher=[[VOA]]|date=2021-07-20|access-date=2021-07-20|archive-date=20 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210720180212/https://www.voanews.com/middle-east/mecca-women-take-part-hajj-guardian-rule-dropped|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Etymology==
The word in {{langx|ar|حج}} ''{{transliteration|ar|ḥajj}}'' is similar to the {{langx|he|חג}} ''{{transliteration|he|ḥag}}'', which means "festival", from the triliteral [[Semitic root]] ح-ج-ج. The term was used to refer to the [[Three Pilgrimage Festivals|three pilgrimage festivals]] Israelites would make to the [[Temple in Jerusalem]] each year.<ref>{{cite book |author=Moše Flôrenṭîn |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=1dNk_b1uDzUC&q=%D7%97-%D7%92-%D7%92+hebrew+pilgrimage&pg=PA138 |title=Late Samaritan Hebrew: A Linguistic Analysis of Its Different Types |publisher=BRILL |year=2005 |page=138 |isbn=978-90-04-13841-4 |access-date=19 October 2020 |archive-date=2 January 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220102175944/https://books.google.com/books?id=1dNk_b1uDzUC&q=%D7%97-%D7%92-%D7%92+hebrew+pilgrimage&pg=PA138 |url-status=live }}</ref> Similarly, the {{langx|ar|حج}} ''{{transliteration|ar|ḥajj}}'' in Islam refers to act of traveling to Mecca to perform the various rituals associated with the pilgrimage.

The present pattern of Hajj was established by [[Muhammad in Islam|Muhammad]].<ref name=EB>{{cite encyclopedia |url=https://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/252050/hajj |title=Hajj |year=2014 |encyclopedia=Encyclopædia Britannica |access-date=12 August 2014 |archive-date=24 September 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140924041951/http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/252050/hajj |url-status=live }}</ref> However, according to the [[Quran]], elements of Hajj trace back to the time of [[Abraham in Islam|Abraham]]. According to Islamic tradition, Abraham was ordered by God to leave his wife [[Hagar in Islam|Hajar]] and his son [[Ishmael in Islam|Ishmael]] alone in the desert of ancient Mecca. In search of water, Hajar desperately ran seven times between the two hills of [[Al-Safa and Al-Marwah|Safa and Marwah]] but found none. Returning in despair to Ishmael, she saw the baby scratching the ground with his leg and a water fountain sprang forth underneath his foot.<ref>{{cite book |last=Haykal |first=Muhammad Husayn |title=The Life of Muhammad |publisher=The Other Press |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=fOyO-TSo5nEC&pg=29 |year=1994 |page=29 |isbn=978-983-9154-17-7 |access-date=12 August 2015 |archive-date=27 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151127211636/https://books.google.com/books?id=fOyO-TSo5nEC&pg=29 |url-status=live }}</ref> Later, Abraham was commanded to build the [[Kaaba]] (which he did with the help of Ishmael) and to invite people to perform pilgrimage there.<ref name="Peters4-7">{{cite book |last=Peters |first=F. E. |title=The Hajj: The Muslim Pilgrimage to Mecca and the Holy Places |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=EK5MqskDYC0C&pg=PA4 |year=1994 |publisher=[[Princeton University Press]] |location=[[New Jersey]] |isbn=0-691-02120-1 |pages=4–7 |access-date=12 August 2015 |archive-date=23 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151123000954/https://books.google.com/books?id=EK5MqskDYC0C&pg=PA4 |url-status=live }}</ref> The Quran refers to these incidents in verses {{cite quran|2|124–127 |s=ns |b=n}} and {{cite quran|22|27–30 |s=ns |b=n}}.<ref group="n">The verses read: "And remember that Abraham was tried by his Lord with certain commands, which he fulfilled.... Remember We made the House a place of assembly for men and a place of safety, and take ye the station of Abraham as a place of prayer; and We covenanted with Abraham and Ishmael, that they should sanctify My House for those who compass it round or use it as a retreat, or bow, or prostrate themselves (therein in prayer).... And remember Abraham and Ishmael raised the foundations of the House (2:124–127)" and "And proclaim the Pilgrimage among men: they will come to thee on foot and (mounted) on every kind of camel, through deep and distant mountain highways, that they may witness the benefits (provided) for them, and celebrate the name of Allah, through the Days appointed, over the cattle which He has provided for them (for sacrifice): then eat ye thereof and feed the distressed one, the needy. Then let them complete the rites prescribed for them, perform their vows, and (again) circumambulate the Ancient House." (22:27–29)</ref> According to the tafsir of Zamakhshari (d. 1144 C.E.), the archangel [[Gabriel]] brought the [[Black Stone]] from Heaven to be attached to the Kaaba when it was built by the [[Abraham|Prophet Abraham]].<ref>F.E. Peters (1994), <u>The Hajj: The Muslim Pilgrimage To Mecca and The Holy Places</u>, Princeton University Press, p.6</ref>

cleansed the Kaaba by destroying all the pagan idols, and then consecrated the building to God.<ref name=Haykal439>{{cite book |last=Husayn Haykal |first=Muhammad |author-link=Muhammad Husayn Haykal == Timing of Hajj ==
The date of Hajj is determined by the [[Islamic calendar]] (known as the Hijri calendar or AH), which is based on the [[lunar year]].<ref name=Harrison156/><ref name=Reynolds33>{{cite book |last=Reynolds |first=Gabriel said |title=The Emergence of Islam: Classical Traditions in Contemporary Perspective |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=HWfFX5XDjuIC&pg=PT33 |year=2012 |publisher=Fortress Press |isbn=978-1-4514-0812-6 |page=33 |access-date=26 November 2019 |archive-date=16 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200216192254/https://books.google.com/books?id=HWfFX5XDjuIC&pg=PT33 |url-status=live }}</ref> Every year, the events of Hajj take place in a ten-day period, starting on 1 and ending on 10 [[Dhu'l-Hijja]], the twelfth and last month of the Islamic calendar. Among these ten days, the 9th Dhu'l-Hijja is known as [[Day of Arafah]], and this day is called the day of Hajj. Because the Islamic calendar is [[lunar calendar|lunar]] and the Islamic year is about eleven days shorter than the Gregorian year, the Gregorian date for Hajj changes from year to year. Thus, each year in the Gregorian calendar, the pilgrimage starts eleven days (sometimes ten days) earlier.<ref name=Reynolds33/><ref>{{cite book |editor1-first=Aziz |editor1-last=Sheikh |title=Caring for Muslim Patients |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=mgwiloFTrKwC&pg=PA95 |access-date=15 November 2014 |year=2008 |publisher=Radcliffe Publishing |isbn=978-1-85775-812-2 |page=95 |archive-date=29 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151129021631/https://books.google.com/books?id=mgwiloFTrKwC&pg=PA95 |url-status=live }}</ref> This makes it possible for the Hajj season to fall twice in one Gregorian year, and it does so every 33 years. The last time this phenomenon occurred was in 2006.<ref name="Umm-al-Qura-Calendar">{{cite web |url=http://www.staff.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_principal.htm |title=Principal Islamic Days of Observance according to Umm al-Qura Calendar |year=2014 |work=The Umm al-Qura Calendar of Saudi Arabia |access-date=24 November 2014 |archive-date=28 October 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191028164511/http://www.staff.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/islam/ummalqura_principal.htm |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Rites==
[[File:Hajj Steps.svg|thumb|Diagram of the locations and rites of Hajj]]
''[[Fiqh]]'' literature describes in detail the manners of carrying out the rites of Hajj, and pilgrims generally follow handbooks and expert guides to successfully fulfill the requirements of Hajj.<ref name="Campo282">{{cite encyclopedia|year=2009|title=Hajj|encyclopedia=Encyclopedia of Islam|publisher=[[Facts On File]]|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=OZbyz_Hr-eIC&pg=PA282|editor=Campo|editor-first=Juan E.|page=282|isbn=978-0-8160-5454-1|access-date=19 October 2020|archive-date=21 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210721152752/https://books.google.com/books?id=OZbyz_Hr-eIC&pg=PA282|url-status=live}}</ref> In performing the rites of Hajj, the pilgrims not only follow the model of Muhammad, but also commemorate the events associated with Abraham.<ref name=Neusner178>{{cite book |last=Neusner |first=Jacob |title=World Religions in America: An Introduction |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=jRkRAQAAIAAJ&q=Hajj+muslims+commemorate+events+associate+Abraham |access-date=6 October 2014 |year=2000 |publisher=Westminster John Knox Press |isbn=978-0-664-25839-9 |page=178 |archive-date=6 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220406182147/https://books.google.com/books?id=jRkRAQAAIAAJ&q=Hajj+muslims+commemorate+events+associate+Abraham |url-status=live }}</ref>

===''Ihram''===
{{Main|Ihram}}
[[File:Muhammad Ali at Hajj 1972.jpg|thumb|[[Muhammad Ali]] wearing the Ihram while performing Hajj in 1972]]
[[Ihram]] is the name given to the special spiritual state, state of holiness, which marks the start of the ritual of Hajj for each person.<ref name="Nigosian111" /><ref name="EB-ihram">{{cite encyclopedia|url=https://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/282481/ihram|title=ihram|year=2014|encyclopedia=Encyclopædia Britannica|access-date=6 October 2014|archive-date=7 October 2014|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141007071226/http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/282481/ihram|url-status=live}}</ref> Ihram is initiated upon the arrival to the ''[[Miqat]]'' or prior to reaching it, depending on where they have come from.

When pilgrims enter into the state of Ihram, they are required to abstain from certain actions.<ref>{{cite web|title=Ihram – Summary|url=http://www.hajj.org.au/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=29&Itemid=53|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080721005117/http://www.hajj.org.au/index.php?option=com_content&task=view&id=29&Itemid=53|archive-date=21 July 2008|access-date=20 November 2013|work=Hajj Portal}}</ref> In Ihram, males are required to wear two white seamless cloths, with one wrapped around the waist reaching below the knee and the other draped over the left shoulder and tied at the right side. For females this involves wearing ordinary dress that fulfills the [[Hijab#Women|Islamic condition of public dress]], where the hands and face are uncovered.<ref name="AtoZ">{{cite book |title=Hajj to Umrah: From A to Z |last=Mohamed |first=Mamdouh N. |publisher=Amana Publications |year=1996 |isbn=0-915957-54-X |url=https://archive.org/details/hajjumrahfromtoz00moha }}</ref>{{page needed|date=January 2015}} Other prohibitions include refraining from clipping nails, shaving any part of the body, having sexual relations; using [[perfume]]s, damaging plants, killing animals, covering the head (for men) or the face and hands (for women); getting married; or carrying weapons.<ref name="Nigosian111">{{cite book |last=Nigosian |first=S. A. |title=Islam: Its History, Teaching, and Practices |url=https://archive.org/details/islamitshistoryt0000nigo |url-access=registration |year=2004 |publisher=[[Indiana University Press]] |location=[[Indiana]] |isbn=0-253-21627-3 |page=[https://archive.org/details/islamitshistoryt0000nigo/page/111 111]}}</ref><ref name="EB-ihram" />

The ''Ihram'' is meant to show equality of all pilgrims in front of God, with no difference between the rich and the poor.<ref name="Neusner178" /> Donning such unsewn white garments entirely is believed to distance man from material ostentation, and engross him in a world of purity and spirituality, since clothes are believed to show individuality and distinction and create superficial barriers that separate individuals. The garments of Ihram are seen as the antithesis of that individualism. ''Ihram'' clothing is also a reminder of shrouds worn after death.<ref name=":1">{{cite web|url=https://salamislam.com/articles/practical-principles/philosophy-hajj-rituals|title=The Philosophy of Hajj Rituals|date=3 January 2021|access-date=31 May 2022|archive-date=11 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210411224841/https://salamislam.com/articles/practical-principles/philosophy-hajj-rituals|url-status=live}}</ref>

===''Tawaf'' and ''sa'i''===
[[File:Tavaf.jpg|right|thumb|Direction of the {{lang|ar-latn|[[tawaf]]}} around the Kaaba]]

The ritual of {{lang|ar-latn|[[tawaf]]}} involves walking seven times counterclockwise around the Kaaba.<ref name="Marshall89">{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA89 |title=Islamic Beliefs, Practices, and Cultures |last=Long |first=Matthew |publisher=Marshall Cavendish Corporation |year=2011 |isbn=978-0-7614-7926-0 |page=89 |access-date=2 September 2014 |archive-date=23 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151123110541/https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA89 |url-status=live }}</ref> Upon arriving at ''[[Al-Masjid Al-Ḥarām]]'', pilgrims perform an arrival {{lang|ar-latn|tawaf}} either as part of [[Umrah]] or as a welcome {{lang|ar-latn|tawaf}}.<ref name="Long16">{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA16 |title=The Hajj Today: A Survey of the Contemporary Pilgrimage to Makkah |last=Long |first=David E. |publisher=[[SUNY Press]] |year=1979 |isbn=0-87395-382-7 |page=16 |access-date=29 August 2017 |archive-date=19 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200219105043/https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA16 |url-status=live }}</ref> During {{lang|ar-latn|tawaf}}, pilgrims also include Hateem – an area at the north side of the Kaaba – inside their path. Each circuit starts and ends with the kissing or touching of the Black Stone. Pilgrims also point to the stone and recite a prayer known as [[Talbiyah]].<ref name="Long17">{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA17 |title=The Hajj Today: A Survey of the Contemporary Pilgrimage to Makkah |last=Long |first=David E. |publisher=[[SUNY Press]] |year=1979 |isbn=978-0-87395-382-5 |page=17 |access-date=29 August 2017 |archive-date=17 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200217110352/https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA17 |url-status=live }}</ref> If kissing or touching the stone is not possible because of crowds, pilgrims may simply point towards the stone with their right hand on each circuit. Eating is not permitted but the drinking of water is permitted and encouraged, because of the risk of dehydration. Men are encouraged to perform the first three circuits at a hurried pace, known as ''Ramal'', and the following four at a more leisurely pace.<ref name="AtoZ" />{{page needed|date=January 2015}}<ref name=Long17/>

The completion of {{lang|ar-latn|Tawaf}} is followed by two [[Raka'ah|Rakaat]] prayers at the [[Maqam Ibrahim|Place of Abraham]] (Muqam Ibrahim), a site near the Kaaba inside the mosque.<ref name=Long17/><ref name="Adelowo401">{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ma-QBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA401 |title=Perspectives in Religious Studies: Volume III |publisher=HEBN Publishers Plc |year=2014 |isbn=978-978-081-447-2 |location=Ibadan |page=401 |editor1-first=E. Dada |editor1-last=Adelowo |access-date=12 August 2015 |archive-date=1 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151101064315/https://books.google.com/books?id=ma-QBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA401 |url-status=live }}</ref> However, again because of large crowds during the days of Hajj, they may instead pray anywhere in the mosque. After prayer, pilgrims also drink water from the Zamzam well, which is made available in coolers throughout the Mosque.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.arabnews.com/news/597946 |title=Pilgrims complain of Zamzam water shortage in Makkah |date=7 July 2014 |access-date=7 September 2014 |newspaper=Arab News |archive-date=22 December 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161222055151/http://www.arabnews.com/news/597946 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Although the circuits around the Kaaba are traditionally done on the ground level, {{lang|ar-latn|tawaf}} is now also performed on the first floor and roof of the mosque because of the large crowds.

This rite is said to be the manifestation of {{lang|ar-latn|Tawhid}}, the Oneness of God. The heart and soul of the pilgrim should move around Kaaba, the symbol of the House of God, in a way that no worldly attraction distracts him from this path. Only [[Tawhid]] should attract him. {{lang|ar-latn|Tawaf}} also represents Muslims' unity. During {{lang|ar-latn|tawaf}}, everyone encircles Kaaba collectively.<ref name=":1" />

{{lang|ar-latn|Tawaf}} is followed by {{lang|ar-latn|sa'i}}, running or walking seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwah, located near the Kaaba.<ref name=Marshall89/><ref name=Adelowo401/> Previously in the open air, the place is now entirely enclosed by the Sacred Mosque, and can be accessed via air-conditioned tunnels.<ref name="RESA">{{cite web |url=http://www.saudiembassy.net/issues/hajj/ |title=Hajj |publisher=Royal Embassy of Saudi Arabia |access-date=12 August 2014 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140815124605/http://www.saudiembassy.net/issues/hajj/ |archive-date=15 August 2014 }}</ref> Pilgrims are advised to walk the circuit, though two green pillars mark a short section of the path where they run. There is also an internal "express lane" for elderly or disabled people. After {{lang|ar-latn|sa'i}}, male pilgrims shave or trim their hair and women generally clip a portion of their hair, which completes the [[Umrah]].

===First day of Hajj: 8th Dhu'l-Hijja (Tarwiyah Day)===
On the 8th Dhu'l-Hijja, the pilgrims are reminded of their duties. They again don the Ihram garments and confirm their intention to make the pilgrimage. The prohibitions of Ihram start now.

The name of Tarwiyah refers to a narration of [[Ja'far al-Sadiq]]. He described the reason that there was no water at [[Mount Arafat]] on the 8th day of Dhu'l-Hijja. If pilgrims wanted to stay at Arafat, they would have prepared water from Mecca and carried it by themselves there. So they told each other to drink enough. Tarwiyah<ref name="Reyshahri">{{cite web |last1=Reyshahri |first1=Mohammad |title=Hajj in Quran and Hadith |url=http://lib.eshia.ir/27405/1/321/%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%87 |website=lib.eshia |access-date=2 September 2018 |archive-date=17 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200217013745/http://lib.eshia.ir/27405/1/321/%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%87 |url-status=live }}</ref> means to quench thirst in the Arabic language.<ref name="farsnews001">{{cite web |title=the reason for naming Tarwiyah day |url=https://www.farsnews.com/news/13940630001418/%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%AA-%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%E2%80%8C%DA%AF%D8%B0%D8%A7%D8%B1%DB%8C-%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B2-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%87-%DA%86%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%AA-%DA%86%DA%AF%D9%88%D9%86%D9%87-%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D8%AB%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AC%DB%8C%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%B4%D8%B1%DB%8C%DA%A9-%D8%B4%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%85 |website=farsnews |access-date=22 September 2015 |date=22 September 2015 |archive-date=12 October 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181012214543/https://www.farsnews.com/news/13940630001418/%D8%B9%D9%84%D8%AA-%D9%86%D8%A7%D9%85%E2%80%8C%DA%AF%D8%B0%D8%A7%D8%B1%DB%8C-%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B2-%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%87-%DA%86%DB%8C%D8%B3%D8%AA-%DA%86%DA%AF%D9%88%D9%86%D9%87-%D8%AF%D8%B1-%D8%AB%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%AD%D8%A7%D8%AC%DB%8C%D8%A7%D9%86-%D8%B4%D8%B1%DB%8C%DA%A9-%D8%B4%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%85 |url-status=live }}</ref> Tarwiyah Day is the first day of Hajj ritual. Also on this day, [[Husayn ibn Ali]] began to go to [[Karbala]] from [[Mecca]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Reyshahri |first1=Mohammad |title=Enclopedia of Imam Husain based on Quran and Hadith |url=http://lib.eshia.ir/27254/5/105/%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%87 |website=lib.eshia |access-date=2 September 2018 |archive-date=1 March 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200301200942/http://lib.eshia.ir/27254/5/105/%D8%AA%D8%B1%D9%88%DB%8C%D9%87 |url-status=live }}</ref> [[Muhammad]] nominated Tarwiyah Day as one of the four chosen days.<ref name="farsnews001"/>

====Mina====
[[File:Haji pilgrimage mina tent city.jpg|thumb|Air-conditioned tents for Hajj pilgrims in [[Mina, Saudi Arabia|Mina]], Saudi Arabia, {{convert|2|km}} away from Mecca]]
After the [[Fajr|morning]] prayer on the 8th of Dhu'l-Hijja, the pilgrims proceed to [[Mina, Saudi Arabia|Mina]] where they spend the whole day and offer [[dhuhr|noon]] (Note: On Friday, [[Jumu'ah|Friday Prayer]] is Offered, instead of Dhuhr Prayer, at Mina), [[Asr|afternoon]], [[maghrib prayer|evening]], and [[Isha'|night]] prayers.<ref name=Adelowo403>{{cite book |editor1-first=E. Dada |editor1-last=Adelowo |title=Perspectives in Religious Studies: Volume III |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=ma-QBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA403 |year=2014 |publisher=HEBN Publishers Plc |location=Ibadan |isbn=978-978-081-447-2 |page=403 |access-date=12 August 2015 |archive-date=3 January 2017 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170103151204/https://books.google.com/books?id=ma-QBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA403 |url-status=live }}</ref> The next morning after morning prayer, they leave Mina to go to Arafat.

===Second day: 9th Dhu'l-Hijja (Arafah Day)===
The 9th Dhu'l-Hijja is known as [[Day of Arafah]], and this day is called the Day of Hajj.<ref name="v3502133"/>

====Arafat====
{{Main|Day of Arafah}}

[[File:Mount Arafat.JPG|thumb|Mount Arafat during Hajj]]
[[File:Arafat pilgrims.jpg|thumb|Pilgrims wearing Ihram near Mount Arafat on the day of Hajj]]
On 9th Dhu'l-Hijja before noon, pilgrims arrive at [[Arafat, Makkah|Arafat]], a barren and plain land some {{convert|20|km}} east of Mecca,<ref name=Long19/> where they stand in contemplative vigil: they offer supplications, repent on and atone for their past sins, and seek the mercy of [[God]], and listen to the sermon from the [[Imam|Islamic scholars]] who deliver it from near Jabal al-Rahmah (The Mount of Mercy)<ref name=Adelowo403/> from where Muhammad is said to have delivered [[The Farewell Sermon|his last sermon]]. Lasting from noon through sunset,<ref name=Long19/> this is known as 'standing before God' (wuquf), one of the most significant rites of Hajj.<ref name=Nigosian111/> At ''[[Masjid Namirah|Masjid al-Namirah]]'', pilgrims offer [[dhuhr|noon]] and [[Asr|afternoon]] prayers together at noontime.<ref name=Adelowo403/> A pilgrim's Hajj is considered invalid if they do not spend the afternoon on Arafat.<ref name=Long19>{{cite book |last=Long |first=David E. |title=The Hajj Today: A Survey of the Contemporary Pilgrimage to Makkah |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA19 |year=1979 |isbn=0-87395-382-7 |page=19 |publisher=SUNY Press |access-date=29 August 2017 |archive-date=4 August 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180804200934/https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA19 |url-status=live }}</ref>

====Muzdalifah====
[[File:MuzdalifahPanorama.jpg|thumb|Pilgrims at Muzdalifah]]
Pilgrims must leave Arafat for [[Muzdalifah]] after sunset without performing their [[Maghrib (prayer)|''Maghrib'' (Sunset) prayer]] at Arafat.<ref>Sahih Muslim-Hadith No 2941.2944</ref> Muzdalifah is an area between Arafat and Mina. Upon reaching there, pilgrims perform Maghrib and Isha prayer jointly, spend the night praying and sleeping on the ground with open sky, and gather pebbles for the next day's ritual of the stoning of the [[Devil]] ([[Devil (Islam)|Shaytan]]).<ref>Sahih Bukhari Hadith No: 732,733, and 734</ref>

===Third day: 10th Dhu'l-Hijja (Qurban Day)===
After the morning prayer, the Pilgrims move from Muzdalifah to Mina.

====Ramy al-Jamarat====
{{Main|Stoning of the Devil}}

[[File:Amellie - Stoning of the devil 2006 Hajj.jpg|thumb|Pilgrims performing "Ramy Al-Jamarat" (Stoning of the Devil) ceremony during the 2006 Hajj]]

At Mina, the pilgrims perform symbolic [[Stoning of the Devil]] (Ramy al-Jamarat) by throwing seven stones from sunrise to sunset at only the largest of the three pillars, known as Jamrat al-Aqabah.<ref name="al-Hasani">{{cite book |last=al-Hasani |first=Abu Qanit al-Sharif |title=The Guiding Helper: Main Text and Explanatory Notes |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wKZJAgAAQBAJ&pg=PA220 |year=2009 |publisher=Lulu.com |isbn=978-1-4452-3791-6 |page=220 |access-date=12 August 2015 |archive-date=2 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151102121628/https://books.google.com/books?id=wKZJAgAAQBAJ&pg=PA220 |url-status=live }}</ref>{{self-published inline|date=February 2020}} The remaining two pillars (jamarah) are not stoned on this day.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.easyhajj.co.uk/throwingpebbles.php |title=easyhajj.co.uk |publisher=easyhajj.co.uk |access-date=19 December 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110811073422/http://www.easyhajj.co.uk/throwingpebbles.php |archive-date=11 August 2011}}</ref> These pillars are said to represent Satan.<ref>{{cite book |author=Nigosian |title=Islam: Its History, Teaching, and Practices |publisher=Indiana University Press |url=https://archive.org/details/islamitshistoryt0000nigo |url-access=registration |year=2004 |isbn=0-253-21627-3 |page=[https://archive.org/details/islamitshistoryt0000nigo/page/112 112]}}</ref> Pilgrims climb ramps to the multi-levelled [[Jamaraat Bridge]], from which they can throw their pebbles at the jamarat. Because of safety reasons, in 2004 the pillars were replaced by long walls, with catch basins below to collect the pebbles.<ref name=Marshall90>{{cite book |title=Islamic Beliefs, Practices, and Cultures |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA90 |access-date=2 September 2014 |year=2011 |publisher=Marshall Cavendish Corporation |isbn=978-0-7614-7926-0 |page=90 |archive-date=23 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151123110545/https://books.google.com/books?id=H_m14NlQQMYC&pg=PA90 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=Mohamed54>{{cite book |editor1-first=Mohamed |editor1-last=Gad-el-Hak |title=Large-Scale Disasters: Prediction, Control, and Mitigation |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=nfDs5KWqyzcC&pg=PA54 |access-date=13 January 2015 |year=2008 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-1-139-47229-6 |page=54 |archive-date=29 November 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151129061142/https://books.google.com/books?id=nfDs5KWqyzcC&pg=PA54 |url-status=live }}</ref>

==== Animal sacrificing ====
After the stoning of the Devil, [[Qurban (Islamic ritual sacrifice)|cattle (Surah 22:34-36)]] are [[Eid al-Adha|sacrificed]] to commemorate the story of Ibrahim and Ismael. Traditionally, the pilgrims slaughtered the animal themselves or oversaw the slaughtering. Today many pilgrims buy a sacrifice voucher in Mecca before the greater Hajj begins, which allows an animal to be slaughtered in the name of God (Allah) on the 10th, without the pilgrim being physically present. Modern abattoirs complete the processing of the meat, which is then sent as a charity to poor people around the world.<ref name=RESA/> At the same time as the sacrifices occur at Mecca, Muslims worldwide perform similar sacrifices, in a three-day global festival called ''Eid al-Adha''.<ref name="bbc.co.uk"/>

====Hair removal====
After sacrificing an animal, another important rite of Hajj is the shaving or trimming of head hair (known as Halak). All male pilgrims shave their head or trim their hair on the day of [[Eid al Adha]] and female pilgrims cut the ends of their hair.<ref name="BBC">{{cite web|date=8 September 2009|title=Hajj: pilgrimage to Mecca|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/religion/religions/islam/practices/hajj_1.shtml|access-date=6 October 2014|publisher=BBC|archive-date=3 November 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191103141339/http://www.bbc.co.uk/religion/religions/islam/practices/hajj_1.shtml|url-status=live}}</ref><ref name=Long21>{{cite book |last=Long |title=The Hajj Today: A Survey of the Contemporary Pilgrimage to Makkah |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA21 |year=1979 |isbn=0-87395-382-7 |page=21 |publisher=SUNY Press |access-date=29 August 2017 |archive-date=16 February 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200216091036/https://books.google.com/books?id=2Uk3Gh6xrUUC&pg=PA21 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>Sahih Muslim Hadith no:2985,2994</ref>

====Tawaf Ziyarat/Ifadah====
[[File:Kaaba 2.JPG|thumb|Pilgrims performing Tawaf around the Kaaba]]

On the same or the following day, the pilgrims re-visit the Sacred Mosque in Mecca for another ''tawaf'', known as ''Tawaf al-Ifadah'', an essential part of Hajj.<ref name=Long21/> It symbolizes being in a hurry to respond to God and show love for Him, an obligatory part of Hajj. The night of the 10th is spent back at Mina.

===Fourth day: 11th Dhu'l-Hijja===
Starting from noon to sunset on the 11 Dhu'l-Hijja (and again the following day), the pilgrims again throw seven pebbles at each of the three pillars in Mina. This is commonly known as the "[[Stoning of the Devil]]".<ref name="al-Hasani"/>

===Fifth day: 12th Dhu'l-Hijja===
On 12 Dhu'l-Hijja, the same process of the stoning of the pillars as of 11 Dhu'l-Hijja takes place.<ref name="al-Hasani"/> Pilgrims may leave Mina for Mecca before sunset on the 12th.

===Last day at Mina: 13th Dhu'l-Hijja===
If unable to leave on the 12th before sunset or opting to stay longer, pilgrims must perform the stoning ritual again on the 13th before returning to Mecca.<ref name="al-Hasani"/>

====Tawaf al-Wadaa====
Finally, before leaving Mecca, pilgrims perform a farewell tawaf called the Tawaf al-Wadaa. 'Wadaa' means 'to bid farewell'. The pilgrims circle the Kaaba seven times counter-clockwise, and if they can, attempt to touch or kiss the Kaaba.<ref>{{Cite book |last1=Sheikho |first1=Mohammad Amin |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=mcVCAwAAQBAJ&dq=Tawaf+al-Wadaa&pg=PT67 |title=Pilgrimage "Hajj": The Fifth High Grade of Al-Taqwa |last2=Al-Dayrani |first2=A. K. John Alias |date=2017-07-19 |publisher=BookRix |isbn=978-3-7309-9606-5 |language=en |access-date=5 July 2022 |archive-date=8 July 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220708212613/https://books.google.com/books?id=mcVCAwAAQBAJ&dq=Tawaf+al-Wadaa&pg=PT67 |url-status=live }}</ref>

==Journey to Medina==
During their journey for Hajj, pilgrims traditionally also travel to the city of Medina (approximately {{convert|450|km}} to the northeast), in particular to pray at the [[Al-Masjid an-Nabawi]] (Mosque of the Prophet),<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/muslim-pilgrims-flock-to-medina-after-performing-hajj/646779 |title=Muslim pilgrims flock to Medina after performing Hajj |last=Al Masry |first=Ahmed |date=September 15, 2006 |website=aa.com.tr |publisher=Anadolu Agency |access-date=24 July 2021 |archive-date=6 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220406182147/https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/muslim-pilgrims-flock-to-medina-after-performing-hajj/646779 |url-status=live }}</ref> which contains the tomb of [[Muhammad]].<ref name=RESA/> The [[Quba Mosque]] and [[Masjid al-Qiblatayn]] are also usually visited.<ref>{{cite book |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=QlozJ12_C-8C&q=Quba+Mosque+Qiblatain&pg=PA32 |title=A life that matters: a spiritual experience |author=Norani Noridin |author2=Nordin Yusof |publisher=The Other Press |page=32 |isbn=978-967-5062-02-5 |year=2008 |access-date=19 October 2020 |archive-date=6 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220406182154/https://books.google.com/books?id=QlozJ12_C-8C&q=Quba+Mosque+Qiblatain&pg=PA32 |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Significance ==
To Muslims, Hajj is associated with religious as well as social significance.<ref name="AzizSheikh2008">{{cite book|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=mgwiloFTrKwC&pg=PA96|title=Caring for Muslim Patients|publisher=Radcliffe Publishing|year=2008|isbn=978-1-85775-812-2|editor-last=Sheikh|editor-first=Aziz|page=96|access-date=2 September 2014|archive-date=29 November 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151129021635/https://books.google.com/books?id=mgwiloFTrKwC&pg=PA96|url-status=live}}</ref> The obligation for performing this pilgrimage is only fulfilled if it is done on the eighth to twelfth day of the last month of the [[Islamic calendar]]. If in a given year, an adult Muslim is in good health and their life and wealth are safe, they must perform the Hajj in the same year. Delaying it is considered sinful unless the delay is caused by reasons beyond their control.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Musharraf|first1=Hussain|title=The Five Pillars of Islam: Laying the Foundations of Divine Love and Service to Humanity|date=2012|publisher=Kube Publishing|location=Leicestershire, UK|isbn=9781847740236|pages=204–205}}</ref>

Apart from being an obligatory religious duty, the Hajj is seen to have a spiritual merit that provides Muslims with an opportunity of self-renewal.<ref name="AzizSheikh2008" /> Hajj serves as a reminder of the [[Day of Judgment]] when Muslims believe people will stand before God.<ref>{{cite book|last=Powell|first=William|url=https://archive.org/details/saudiarabiaitsro00powe|title=Saudi Arabia and Its Royal Family|publisher=Lyle Stuart|year=1982|isbn=978-0-8184-0326-2|page=[https://archive.org/details/saudiarabiaitsro00powe/page/113 113]|access-date=7 October 2014|url-access=registration}}</ref> [[Hadith]] literature (sayings of Muhammad) lists various merits a pilgrim achieves upon successful completion of their Hajj.<ref group="n">For example, one such Hadith says Abu Huraira quoted Muhammad as saying: "Whoever performs Hajj for God's pleasure and does not have sexual relations with his wife, and does not do evil or sins then he will return (after Hajj free from all sins) as if he were born anew."{{Hadith-usc|bukhari|usc=yes|2|26|596}}</ref> After successful pilgrimage, pilgrims can prefix their names with the title "Al-Hajji", and are held with respect in Muslim society.<ref name="Andrea67">{{cite book|author=Andrea Schulte-Peevers|url=https://books.google.com/books?id=D8hmZIAaTGQC&pg=PA67|title=Oman, UAE & Arabian Peninsula|publisher=Lonely Planet|year=2010|isbn=978-1-74179-145-7|page=67|access-date=12 August 2015|archive-date=26 October 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151026160826/https://books.google.com/books?id=D8hmZIAaTGQC&pg=PA67|url-status=live}}</ref> However, Islamic scholars suggest Hajj should signify a Muslim's religious commitment, and should not be a measurement of their social status.<ref name="Andrea67" /> Hajj brings together and unites Muslims from different parts of the world irrespective of their race, colour, and culture, which acts as a symbol of equality.<ref name="Nigosian111" /><ref name="BBC" />

A 2008 study on the impact of participating in the Islamic pilgrimage found that Muslim communities become more positive and tolerant after Hajj. Titled ''Estimating the Impact of the Hajj: Religion and Tolerance in Islam's Global Gathering'' and conducted in conjunction with [[Harvard University]]'s [[John F. Kennedy School of Government]], the study noted that the Hajj "increases belief in equality and harmony among ethnic groups and Islamic sects and leads to more favourable attitudes toward women, including greater acceptance of female education and employment" and that "Hajjis show increased belief in peace, equality and harmony among adherents of different religions."<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Clingingsmith |first1=David |last2=Khwaja |first2=Asim Ijaz |last3=Kremer |first3=Michael |title=Estimating the Impact of the Hajj: Religion and Tolerance in Islam's Global Gathering * |journal=Quarterly Journal of Economics |date=August 2009 |volume=124 |issue=3 |pages=1133–1170 |doi=10.1162/qjec.2009.124.3.1133 |url=https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/124/3/1133/1905120 |access-date=20 September 2025|url-access=subscription }}</ref>

[[Malcolm X]], an American activist during the [[Civil Rights Movement]], describes the sociological atmosphere he experienced on Hajj in April 1964 in [[The Autobiography of Malcolm X|his autobiography]]:

{{blockquote|There were tens of thousands of pilgrims, from all over the world. They were of all colors, from blue-eyed blondes to black-skinned Africans. But we were all participating in the same ritual, displaying a spirit of unity and brotherhood that my experiences in America had led me to believe never could exist between the white and the non-white. America needs to understand Islam because this is the one religion that erases from its society the race problem. You may be shocked by these words coming from me. But on this pilgrimage, what I have seen, and experienced, has forced me to rearrange much of my thought-patterns previously held.<ref name="XHaley1999">{{cite book |author1=Malcolm X |author2=Alex Haley |title=The Autobiography of Malcolm X |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=NlkifTXux_cC |access-date=26 February 2013 |year=1999 |publisher=Ballantine Books |isbn=978-0-345-35068-8 |page=346 |archive-date=29 May 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130529002515/http://books.google.com/books?id=NlkifTXux_cC |url-status=live }}</ref>}}

== Differences between Hajj and Umrah ==
{{Main|Umrah}}

* Both are Islamic pilgrimages; the main difference is their level of importance and the method of observance.<ref>{{Cite journal|last1=Gannon|first1=Martin Joseph|last2=Baxter|first2=Ian W. F.|last3=Collinson|first3=Elaine|last4=Curran|first4=Ross|last5=Farrington|first5=Thomas|last6=Glasgow|first6=Steven|last7=Godsman|first7=Elliot M.|last8=Gori|first8=Keith|last9=Jack|first9=Gordon R. A. |display-authors=6 |date=11 June 2017|title=Travelling for Umrah: destination attributes, destination image, and post-travel intentions|url=http://centaur.reading.ac.uk/70393/3/Umrah-SIJ-2017.pdf|journal=The Service Industries Journal|volume=37|issue=7–8|pages=448–465|doi=10.1080/02642069.2017.1333601|s2cid=54745153|issn=0264-2069|access-date=11 July 2019|archive-date=27 April 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190427224205/http://centaur.reading.ac.uk/70393/3/Umrah-SIJ-2017.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref>
* Hajj is one of the [[Five Pillars of Islam]]. It is obligatory for every Muslim once in their lifetime, provided they are physically fit and financially capable.<ref>{{cite journal |last=Edgar |first=Scott |title=The Five Pillars of Islam in the Hadith |journal=Studia Antiqua |volume=2 |issue=1 |year=2002 |url=https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/studiaantiqua/ |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180728071242/https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/studiaantiqua/ |archive-date=28 July 2018 }}</ref>
* Hajj is performed over specific days during a designated [[Islamic calendar|Islamic month]] ([[Dhu'l-Hijja]]). However, Umrah can be performed at any time.
* Although they share common rites, Umrah can be performed in less than a few hours while Hajj is more time-consuming, and involves more rituals.

Traditionally, the pilgrimage to Mecca was mainly an overland journey using [[camel]]s as a means of transport. During official Hajj days, pilgrims travel between the different locations by metro trains, bus or on foot. The Saudi government strictly controls vehicles' access into these heavily congested areas. However, the journey can take many hours due to heavy vehicular and pedestrian traffic. In 2010, the Saudi government started operating the [[Al Mashaaer Al Mugaddassah Metro line]] as an exclusive shuttle train for pilgrims between Arafat, Muzdalifa and Mina. The service, which operates only during the days of Hajj, shortens the travel time during the critical "Nafrah" from Arafat to Muzdalifah to minutes. Due to its limited capacity, the use of the metro is not open to all pilgrims.

== Sustainability ==
Hajj has a considerable environmental impact, with the average pilgrim contributing about 60.5&nbsp;kg CO<sub>2-eq</sub> per day. Transport, lodging, food, and waste generated an estimated 3.0 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent during the 2011 Hajj season.<ref name="Hanandeh2013"/> Most pilgrims travel to Mecca by air<ref>{{cite news |last1=Maher |first1=Ahmed |title=Mecca seeks 'green Hajj' |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-20112400 |work=BBC News |date=27 October 2012}}</ref> and long-haul air travel accounts for 60% of [[greenhouse gas emissions]] created by Hajj.<ref name="Hanandeh2013">{{cite journal |last1=El Hanandeh |first1=Ali |title=Quantifying the carbon footprint of religious tourism: the case of Hajj |journal=Journal of Cleaner Production |date=August 2013 |volume=52 |pages=53–60 |doi=10.1016/j.jclepro.2013.03.009 |bibcode=2013JCPro..52...53E }}</ref>

Rising global temperatures mean that in the future, people undertaking Hajj could face "extreme danger" due to heat and humidity.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Study: Climate change could pose danger for Muslim pilgrimage |url=https://news.mit.edu/2019/climate-muslim-hajj-0822 |access-date=2022-11-24 |website=MIT News {{!}} Massachusetts Institute of Technology |date=22 August 2019 |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":9">{{Cite web |date=2019-08-30 |title=With hajj under threat, it's time Muslims joined the climate movement |url=http://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2019/aug/30/hajj-muslims-climate-movement-global-heating-pilgrims |access-date=2022-11-24 |website=the Guardian |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=How climate change is affecting Hajj |url=https://muslimnews.co.uk/newspaper/environment/climate-change-affecting-hajj/ |access-date=2022-11-24 |website=The Muslim News}}</ref> Projected temperature rises of 1.5° to 2° could have significant impacts on the health of participants, many of whom are elderly.<ref>{{Cite journal |last1=Saeed |first1=Fahad |last2=Schleussner |first2=Carl-Friedrich |last3=Almazroui |first3=Mansour |date=2021-02-01 |title=From Paris to Makkah: heat stress risks for Muslim pilgrims at 1.5 °C and 2 °C |journal=Environmental Research Letters |volume=16 |issue=2 |pages=024037 |doi=10.1088/1748-9326/abd067 |bibcode=2021ERL....16b4037S |s2cid=229428683 |issn=1748-9326|doi-access=free }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last1=Masters |first1=Jeff |last2=Henson |first2=Bob |date=2021-07-15 |title=More warming a threat to the Hajj - ===Crowd-control problems===

Pilgrim numbers have greatly increased in recent years, which has led to numerous accidents and deaths due to overcrowding. The first major accident during Hajj in modern times occurred in 1990, when [[1990 Mecca tunnel tragedy|a tunnel crowd crush]] led to the death of 1,462 people.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://bigstory.ap.org/article/aa29c2ecc36d4b748fedbb40d36b335f/glance-major-hajj-related-incidents-saudi-arabia |title=Glance at major hajj-related incidents in Saudi Arabia |agency=Associated Press |date=11 September 2015 |access-date=10 October 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150927064809/http://bigstory.ap.org/article/aa29c2ecc36d4b748fedbb40d36b335f/glance-major-hajj-related-incidents-saudi-arabia |url-status=dead |archive-date=27 September 2015}}</ref> Afterwards, various crowd-control techniques were adopted to improve safety. Because of large crowds, some of the rituals have become more symbolic. For example, it is no longer necessary to kiss the [[Black Stone]]. Instead, pilgrims simply point at it on each circuit around the Kaaba. Also, the large pillars used for pebble throwing were changed into long walls in 2004 with basins below to catch the stones.<ref name=Marshall90/><ref name=Mohamed54/> Another example is that animal sacrifice is now done at slaughterhouses appointed by the Saudi authorities, without the pilgrims being present there.<ref name=AtoZ/><ref name=CGOIJ>{{cite web |url=http://cgijeddah.mkcl.org/Content.aspx?ID=752&PID=682 |title=Rituals of Haj Sacrifice |date=3 October 2013 |work=Consulate General of India, Jeddah |access-date=24 October 2015 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160128140743/http://cgijeddah.mkcl.org/Content.aspx?ID=752&PID=682 |archive-date=28 January 2016 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.adahi.org/adahisite/default.aspx |title=The Saudi Project for Use of Hajj Meat |work=ADAHI |access-date=24 October 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151021010152/http://www.adahi.org/adahisite/default.aspx |archive-date=21 October 2015 }}</ref>

===COVID-19===

In February 2020, Saudi Arabia temporarily banned foreign pilgrims from entering Mecca and Medina to prevent the [[COVID-19 pandemic|spread of COVID-19]] in the Kingdom.<ref>{{cite news |title=Coronavirus: Saudi Arabia suspends entry for pilgrims visiting holy sites |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-51658061 |work=BBC News |date=27 February 2020 |access-date=1 March 2020 |archive-date=29 April 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200429030326/https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-51658061 |url-status=live }}</ref> It later temporarily suspended the pilgrimage of [[Umrah]].<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Ebrahim |first1=Shahul H |last2=Memish |first2=Ziad A |title=Saudi Arabia's drastic measures to curb the COVID-19 outbreak: temporary suspension of the Umrah pilgrimage |journal=Journal of Travel Medicine |date=18 May 2020 |volume=27 |issue=3 |article-number=taaa029 |doi=10.1093/jtm/taaa029 |pmid=32109274 |pmc=7107544 }}</ref> In June, the Saudi government announced that only "very limited numbers" of pilgrims already resident in Saudi Arabia would be permitted to participate in the Hajj.<ref name="New York Times"/>

==Hajj and the Saudi economy==
{{see also|Economy of Saudi Arabia}}
In 2014, Saudi Arabia was expected to have earned up to $8.5&nbsp;billion from Hajj.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://english.alarabiya.net/en/business/economy/2014/08/26/-9-billion-income-from-hajj-expected.html |title=Saudi Arabia: $8.5 billion income from hajj expected |date=26 August 2014 |publisher=Al-Arabiya English |access-date=14 September 2016 |archive-date=30 September 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160930175455/http://english.alarabiya.net/en/business/economy/2014/08/26/-9-billion-income-from-hajj-expected.html |url-status=live }}</ref> Saudi Arabia's highest source of revenue after oil and gas is Hajj and the country is expected to depend more on Hajj as the amounts of available oil and gas for sale decline.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Walker |first=Stuart |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=qaoLEAAAQBAJ&dq=Saudi+Arabia's+highest+source+of+revenue+after+oil+and+gas+is+Hajj&pg=PP90 |title=Design and Spirituality: A Philosophy of Material Cultures |date=2020-12-30 |publisher=Routledge |isbn=978-1-000-24595-0 |language=en |access-date=8 July 2022 |archive-date=8 July 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220708212614/https://books.google.com/books?id=qaoLEAAAQBAJ&dq=Saudi+Arabia%27s+highest+source+of+revenue+after+oil+and+gas+is+Hajj&pg=PP90 |url-status=live }}</ref>

Furthermore, the increase of religious tourism from about 12&nbsp;million Muslims annually to almost 17&nbsp;million by 2025 has given rise to increasing luxury hotel businesses in the area to accommodate pilgrims. The [[Abraj al-Bait]] firm intends to build hotels, shopping malls and apartments which is claimed to be an estimated value of three billion dollars.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/nov/14/mecca-hajj-saudi-arabia|title=Mecca makeover: how the hajj has become big business for Saudi Arabia|first=Riazat|last=Butt|date=14 November 2010|website=The Guardian|access-date=20 July 2018|archive-date=26 June 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180626030203/https://www.theguardian.com/world/2010/nov/14/mecca-hajj-saudi-arabia|url-status=live}}</ref> According to The Embassy of Saudi Arabia, the Saudi government are working towards establishing programs which promote sanitation, housing, transportation, and welfare as the number of visiting pilgrims increases.{{citation needed|date=May 2026}}

Most pilgrims, from countries such as the [[United States]], [[Australia]] and the [[United Kingdom]] decide to purchase packages from licensed Hajj agencies in their countries. This helps direct the flow of traffic into the Kingdom and allows for pilgrims to work directly with a business responsible for their services instead of dealing directly with Saudi Arabia's government.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ezhajjgroups.com|title=US Hajj Company: For Hajj & Umrah Packages|website=EZ Hajj Groups|access-date=8 July 2022|archive-date=14 January 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220114173614/https://ezhajjgroups.com/|url-status=live}}</ref>

In July 2020, the WSJ reported that following the COVID-19 pandemic, the Saudi authorities [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Hajj|curtailed the five-day event in Mecca to fewer than 10,000 people]] already residing in the country. It also noted that the hospitality and housing industries, which rely entirely on Hajj revenues, would face severe losses of revenue.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/saudi-arabias-latest-challenge-containing-covid-19-during-the-hajj-11595678401|title=Saudi Arabia's Latest Challenge: Containing Covid-19 During the Hajj|access-date=25 July 2020|newspaper=Wall Street Journal|date=25 July 2020|archive-date=26 July 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200726025858/https://www.wsj.com/articles/saudi-arabias-latest-challenge-containing-covid-19-during-the-hajj-11595678401|url-status=live}}</ref>

In 2017, the Hajj and Umrah revenues were expected to exceed US$150 billion by 2022.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.arabnews.com/node/1151751/saudi-arabia |title=Hajj revenues poised to exceed $150bn by 2022: Experts |website=Arab News|date=28 August 2017 }}</ref> Hajj revenues are expected to cross US$350 billion by 2032, according to a report published by ''The Moodie Davitt''.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.moodiedavittreport.com/saudi-arabia-hajj-tourism-to-become-us350-billion-market-by-2032/ |title=Saudi Arabia Hajj tourism to become US$350 billion market by 2032 |website=The Moodie Davitt Report|date=13 August 2022 }}</ref>

==Number of pilgrims per year==
{{annual_human_migration_world_map.svg|250px}}
There has been a substantial increase in the number of pilgrims during the last 92 years, and the number of foreign pilgrims has increased by approximately 2,824 percent, from 58,584 in 1920 to 1,712,962 in 2012.<ref name=History>{{cite news |url=http://www.thenews.com.pk/Todays-News-2-139473-Number-of-foreign-Hajis-grows-by-2824-percent-in-92-years |title=Number of foreign Hajis grows by 2,824 percent in 92 years |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20121102022130/http://www.thenews.com.pk/Todays-News-2-139473-Number-of-foreign-Hajis-grows-by-2824-percent-in-92-years |archive-date=2 November 2012 |newspaper=[[The News International]] |access-date=9 October 2016}}</ref> Because of development and expansion work at Masjid al-Haram, the authority restricted the number of pilgrims in 2013.<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl/21917/Reduction+in+Hajj+2013+Quota |title=Reduction in Hajj 2013 Quota |publisher=Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India |date=8 July 2013 |access-date=17 September 2014 |archive-date=28 January 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160128140744/http://mea.gov.in/press-releases.htm?dtl%2F21917%2FReduction+in+Hajj+2013+Quota |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=https://ph.news.yahoo.com/saudi-arabia-cuts-hajj-quota-foreign-pilgrims-20-213053607.html |title=Saudi Arabia cuts Hajj Quota for foreign pilgrims by 20 percent |work=Yahoo News |date=19 June 2013 |access-date=17 September 2013 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141006095319/https://ph.news.yahoo.com/saudi-arabia-cuts-hajj-quota-foreign-pilgrims-20-213053607.html |archive-date=6 October 2014 }}</ref>

Between 1940 and 1945, foreign pilgrims were restricted from arriving in Saudi Arabia as a result of [[World War II]];<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Supratman |first1=Frial Ramadhan |title=Hajj and the chaos of the Great War: Pilgrims of the Dutch East Indies in World War I (1914-1918) |journal=Wawasan: Jurnal Ilmiah Agama Dan Sosial Budaya |date=30 December 2020 |volume=5 |issue=2 |pages=167–178 |doi=10.15575/jw.v5i2.8584 |doi-access=free }}</ref> the pilgrimages in 2020 and 2021 [[Impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Hajj|were severely restricted]] while [[COVID-19 pandemic in Saudi Arabia|the country was dealing with the COVID-19 pandemic]]. In the most recent 2023 Hajj, there were 1,845,045 total pilgrims, including about 184,000 Saudis.<ref name="NewArab">{{cite news |last= |first= |date=June 27, 2023 |title= Saudi Arabia: Hajj draws 1.8 million pilgrims, falls short of pre-pandemic record|url=https://www.newarab.com/news/hajj-draws-18-million-pilgrims-falls-short-record?amp |work=[[The New Arab]] |location= |access-date=October 27, 2023}}</ref>

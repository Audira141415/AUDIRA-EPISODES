The '''Haramain High Speed Railway''' ({{Langx|ar|قطار الحرمين السريع|translit=qiṭār al-ḥaramayn as-sarīʿ}}, {{IPA|acw|ɡɪtˤaːr alħarameːn asːariːʕ}}, abbreviated '''HHR'''), is a passenger railway serving the [[Mecca Province|Mecca]] and [[Medina Province (Saudi Arabia)|Medina]] provinces in western Saudi Arabia. The {{convert|449.2|km|mi|adj=mid|-long}} main line directly connects the Islamic holy cities of [[Mecca]] and [[Medina]] (collectively known as the Haramain), via [[Jeddah]] and the [[King Abdullah Economic City]]. A {{convert|3.75|km|mi|adj=mid}} branch line provides connections to the [[King Abdulaziz International Airport]] in Jeddah. It opened to the public on 11 October 2018.

The HHR has a service speed of {{convert|300|km/h|0|abbr=on}}, making it the first [[high-speed rail|high-speed railway]] in [[Saudi Arabia]], the Middle East and [[West Asia]].<ref name="ICCP" /> Construction began in March 2009 and the railway was officially inaugurated on 25 September 2018,<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.reuters.com/article/us-saudi-train/saudi-arabia-opens-high-speed-train-linking-islams-holiest-cities-idUSKCN1M51XW|title=Saudi Arabia opens high-speed train linking Islam's holiest cities|last=Kalin|first=Stephen|work=U.S.|access-date=2018-09-25|language=en-US}}</ref> opening to the public on 11 October 2018.<ref>{{cite web |title=Pictures: Saudi Arabia opens high-speed railway to public |url=https://gulfnews.com/news/gulf/saudi-arabia/pictures-saudi-arabia-opens-high-speed-railway-to-public-1.2289006 |website=GulfNews |access-date=15 October 2018 |date=12 October 2018}}</ref>

== History ==

=== Construction ===
Construction began in March 2009, and the project was originally slated for completion in 2012. Having opened in 2018, the HHR took six years longer to complete than anticipated.<ref name=":2">{{Cite news |date=25 August 2018 |title=Saudi high-speed train links holy cities |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/world-middle-east-45639285 |access-date=25 September 2018 |work=BBC News |language=en-GB}}</ref> The total contract value was approximately US$9.4 billion.<ref name="rgi20111026">{{cite news |date=26 October 2011 |title=Spanish consortium wins Haramain High Speed Rail contract |url=http://www.railwaygazette.com/nc/news/single-view/view/spanish-consortium-wins-haramain-high-speed-rail-contract.html |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120213025842/http://www.railwaygazette.com/nc/news/single-view/view/spanish-consortium-wins-haramain-high-speed-rail-contract.html |archive-date=13 February 2012 |access-date=26 October 2011 |work=Railway Gazette International}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=26 October 2011 |title=Un consorcio español logra el AVE La Meca-Medina por 6.736 millones |url=http://www.europapress.es/economia/noticia-economia-ave-ampl2-consorcio-espanol-logra-ave-meca-medina-6736-millones-20111026133534.html |work=Europa Press |language=es}}</ref>

=== 2019 Al-Sulimaniyah–Jeddah Station fire ===

On 29 September 2019, less than a year after the opening of the line, a massive fire broke out at the Al Sulimaniyah–Jeddah Station in southern Jeddah, injuring several people and resulting in the station being completely destroyed.<ref>{{cite web |title=Fire engulfs new Saudi high-speed rail station in Jeddah &#124; DW &#124; 29.09.2019 |url=https://www.dw.com/en/fire-engulfs-new-saudi-high-speed-rail-station-in-jeddah/a-50632259 |access-date=7 October 2020 |website=www.dw.com}}</ref> Firefighters took 12 hours to bring the fire under control, and the fire was traced back to the fibre-reinforced plastic roof panels, although the reason behind the panels catching fire still remains unknown.<ref>{{cite web |title=Jeddah station fire: Why were people on the roof? |url=https://al-bab.com/blog/2019/10/jeddah-station-fire-why-were-people-roof |access-date=7 October 2020 |website=al-bab.com}}</ref> A {{convert|1.5|km|mi|adj=mid}} bypass line was constructed around the station to avoid disruption to services between Mecca and Medina.<ref>{{cite web |title=Haramain launches Jeddah airport service at 300 km/H |url=https://www.railwaygazette.com/high-speed/haramain-launches-jeddah-airport-service-at-300-km/h/55338.article |access-date=7 October 2020 |website=www.railwaygazette.com}}</ref>

=== COVID-19 suspension ===
HHR services were suspended on 20 March 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Services between Mecca and Medina resumed on 31 March 2021.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-03-31 |title=Saudi Arabia's Haramain train operations resume on Wednesday |url=https://arab.news/wwnyp |access-date=2021-03-31 |website=Arab News |language=en}}</ref>

==Engineering==
===Design===
The [[Saudi Railways Organization|Saudi Railways Organisation]] commissioned [[Dar Al-Handasah]] to prepare the concept design of the project. Dar Al-Handasah was also assigned to supervise the construction and manage the HHR. Their design incorporated one cut-and-cover tunnel, 46 rail bridges, 9 wadi bridges, 5 rail underpasses, 53 vehicular overpasses, 30 vehicular underpasses, 12 camel crossings, 5 stations, and 3 depots.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Dar Al-Handasah - Work - Haramain High Speed Rail (HHSR) |url=http://www.dar.com/work/project/haramain-high-speed-rail-(hhsr) |access-date=2017-12-11 |website=www.dar.com |language=en-US}}</ref>

=== Construction ===
Construction was divided into two phases. The 6.79 billion riyal (US$1.81 billion) contract for Package 1 of Phase I was awarded to Al Rajhi Alliance in March 2009.<ref name="arwmmcc" /> The alliance consists of the Chinese [[China Railway Construction Corporation]], the French [[Bouygues]], and Saudi companies Al Arrab Contracting Company and Al&nbsp;Suwailem Company, in cooperation with the Saudi consulting company [[Khatib and Alami]] and with management support from the British [[Scott Wilson Group]].<ref name="rgi20100106">{{cite news | title=Haramain High Speed Rail project manager appointed | url=http://www.railwaygazette.com/news/single-view/view/10/haramain-high-speed-rail-project-manager-appointed.html | work=[[Railway Gazette International]] | date=2010-01-06 | access-date=20 January 2010 | archive-date=7 August 2020 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200807223111/https://www.railwaygazette.com/news/single-view/view/10/haramain-high-speed-rail-project-manager-appointed.html | url-status=dead }}</ref>

As part of the contract of construction Phase II, [[Talgo]] was to supply 35 [[Talgo 350|Renfe Class 102]] (marketed as Talgo 350) [[Push–pull train|push-pull]] trains at a price of 1.257 billion euros, with options for 23 more for 800 million euros.<ref name="rgi20111026" /><ref>{{cite web |title=Talgo se adjudica el contrato de Arabia. Patentes Talgo |url=http://www.talgo.com/index.php/es/ampliar.php?id=168&sec=noticias |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20141129050155/http://www.talgo.com/index.php/es/ampliar.php?id=168&sec=noticias |archive-date=29 November 2014 |access-date=3 December 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Page 188 Mario Oriol from Talgo in UIMP Seminary |url=http://www.oepm.es/comun/documentos_relacionados/Ponencias/61_2013_07_22_Programa_Santander_UIMP.pdf}}</ref> Renfe and Adif would operate the trains and maintain the line for 12 years.<ref name="rgi20111026" /> Ultimately, 36 [[Renfe Class 102|Talgo 350 SRO]] trains were ordered, with one equipped with a dual hybrid coach pair for up to 20 or 30 VIPs.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Train manufacturer and supplier for the Haramin project. |url=https://www.talgo.com/en/projects/saudi-arabia/haramain/ |access-date=9 July 2018 |archive-date=15 July 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200715074615/https://www.talgo.com/en/projects/saudi-arabia/haramain/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> The propulsion and bogies were made at [[Bombardier Transportation|Bombardier]] factories in Spain.<ref>{{cite news |title=Bombardier to supply components for Haramain High Speed Rail trains |url=http://www.railwaygazette.com/news/passenger/single-view/view/bombardier-to-supply-components-for-haramain-high-speed-rail-trains.html |access-date=24 September 2012 |work=[[Railway Gazette International]] |archive-date=7 August 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200807200122/https://www.railwaygazette.com/news/passenger/single-view/view/bombardier-to-supply-components-for-haramain-high-speed-rail-trains.html |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=4-traders |title=Talgo : Haramain high-speed train ready for launch {{!}} 4-Traders |url=http://www.4-traders.com/TALGO-SA-22148977/news/Talgo-Haramain-high-speed-train-ready-for-launch-24820576/ |access-date=2018-07-09}}</ref>

=== Stations ===
===Main line===
[[File:Haramain High Speed Railway Station Interior 2022.jpg|thumb|Medina Station{{which|date=March 2026}} interior]]
There are five stations on the line.  
* [[Medina]] Station (King Abdulaziz Street-East)
* [[King Abdullah Economic City]] Station in [[Rabigh]]
* Jeddah Station (Al Sulaymaniyah) - Operating<ref>{{cite web |date=13 July 2021 |title=Jeddah Sulaymaniyah Haramain station resumes operation today |url=https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/608783}}</ref>
* Mecca Station (Rusaiyfah)<ref name="SREPP">{{cite web |title=Phase 1 (Package 2) – Stations |url=http://www.saudirailexpansion.com/SaudiRailExpansion/inner.aspx?secserno=588&parent=587 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20091226121610/http://www.saudirailexpansion.com/SaudiRailExpansion/inner.aspx?parent=587&secserno=588 |archive-date=26 December 2009 |access-date=26 February 2010 |work=Saudi Railways Expansion Programme |publisher=Saudi Railways Organization.}}</ref><ref name="rgi20090423" />

===King Abdulaziz International Airport Branch===
* [[King Abdulaziz International Airport]] - opened 11 December 2019<ref>{{cite web |date=11 December 2019 |title=Jeddah airport linked to reopened Haramain high-speed line |url=https://www.railjournal.com/passenger/high-speed/jeddah-airport-linked-to-reopened-haramain-high-speed-line/ |access-date=2019-12-11}}</ref>


The Makkah Central Station is located near the 3rd Ring Road, in Rusaiyfah District near the Rusaiyfah [[park and ride]] to the [[Great Mosque of Mecca|Grand Mosque]]. The Jeddah Central Station is located on Haramain Road, in Al-Naseem District. The railway alignment route is on the median of the Haramain road. Medina has a passenger station. A station was built at the [[King Abdulaziz International Airport]], connected via a branch line.<ref name="brief" />

According to [[Saudi Railways Organization|Saudi Railways Organisation]] the stations are "aesthetically iconic" buildings with designs which take into account Islamic architectural traditions. They have shops, restaurants, [[mosque]]s, car parking, a [[helipad]] and VIP&nbsp;lounges. Stations were designed by [[Buro Happold]] and [[Foster&nbsp;+ Partners]].<ref name="rgi20110216" />

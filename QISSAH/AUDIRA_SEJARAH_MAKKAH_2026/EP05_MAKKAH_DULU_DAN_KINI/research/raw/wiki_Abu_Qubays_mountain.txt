'''Abu Qubays''' ({{langx|ar|جَبَل أَبُو قُبَيْس \ جَبَل أَبِي قُبَيْس|Jabal Abū Qubays / Jabal Abī Qubays}}) is a [[sacred mountain]] which resides on the eastern frontier of ''[[Masjid al-Haram|Al-Masjid Al-Haram]]'' in [[Mecca]],<ref name="EOI1913">{{cite book |title=The Encyclopædia of Islam: A Dictionary of the Geography, Ethnography and Biography of the Muhammadan Peoples |year=1913 |publisher=[[Brill Publishers|EJ Brill]] |location=Holland |page=97}}</ref> in the [[Hejaz]] region of [[Saudi Arabia]].

==Description==
[[File:Great Mosque of Mecca1.jpg|thumb|Jabal Abu Qubays is located to the east of ''[[Great Mosque of Mecca|Al-Masjid Al-Haram]]'', in the right hand side of the photograph. [[Jabal al-Nour]] can be seen in the background.]]

Although the exact origin of its name is unknown, it is believed to be called ''Al-Amīn'' ({{lang|ar|ٱلْأَمِيْن}}) in [[Jahiliyyah|pagan]] times because the sacred [[Black Stone]] resided there according to [[Muslims]]{{who|date=July 2020}}. According to another report, this mountain was also called the ''Maghārat al-Kanz'' ({{lang|ar|مَغَارَة ٱلْكَنْز}}, "Treasure Cave"), and this was believed to be the place where the first of men stayed and were buried after their death. According to tradition, this is the place where the [[Prophets and messengers in Islam|Islamic Prophet]] [[Muhammad]] performed the miracle of [[Splitting of the Moon|splitting the Moon]] into two pieces and then re-attaching those pieces as demanded by the [[kafir|disbelievers]] of [[Mecca|Makkah]].<ref name="EOI1913" />

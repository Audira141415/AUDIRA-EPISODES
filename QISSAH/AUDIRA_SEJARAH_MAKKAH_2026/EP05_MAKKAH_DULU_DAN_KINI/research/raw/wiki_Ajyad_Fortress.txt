The '''Ajyad Fortress''' ({{langx|ar|قلعة أجياد}}; {{IPA|acw|qal.ʕat ʔadʒ.jaːd}}) was an 18th century Ottoman-era [[citadel]] which stood on a hill overlooking the [[Masjid al-Haram|Grand Mosque]] of [[Mecca]], in what is now [[Saudi Arabia]]. Built in the late 18th century, it was [[Destruction of early Islamic heritage sites in Saudi Arabia|demolished by the Saudi government]] in 2002 for commercial development of the [[Abraj Al Bait]], sparking protests from [[Turkey]] and other concerned [[Muslim]]s of the world.

==History==
The fortress was built in 1780 under [[Habesh Eyalet|Ottoman rule]] (later [[Hejaz vilayet]]) to protect the [[Kaaba]] in Mecca from bandits and invaders.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.wsws.org/en/articles/2002/01/fort-j28.html|title=Saudi government demolishes historic Ottoman castle |work=World Socialist Web Site|author=Simon Wheelan|date=28 January 2002|access-date=20 January 2015}}</ref><ref name="BBC 20 January 2015">{{cite news|url=https://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/middle_east/1748711.stm|work=BBC News |title=Saudis hit back over Mecca castle|date=9 January 2002 |access-date=20 January 2015}}</ref> The fort covered some {{convert|23000|m2|abbr=on}} on Bulbul Mountain<ref name="ArabNews20020109">{{cite news |url=https://www.arabnews.com/node/217526 |work=Arab News |title=Historic Makkah fortress demolished |date=9 January 2002 |access-date=9 March 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210119123251/https://www.arabnews.com/node/217526 |archive-date=19 January 2021}}</ref> (a [[spur (mountain)|spur]] of Jebel Kuda) overlooking the [[Masjid al-Haram]] from the south. 

From 1 to 9 January 2002 the Ajyad Fortress was demolished and most of Bulbul mount was leveled,<ref name="ArabNews20020109"/> to clear the area for the $15&nbsp;billion construction project.<ref>
{{cite web|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/travel/lists/the-worlds-most-expensive-buildings/|website=The Telegraph|title=Revealed: The world's 20 most expensive buildings|access-date=28 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180728183422/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/travel/lists/the-worlds-most-expensive-buildings/|archive-date=28 July 2018|url-status=dead}}of [[Abraj Al Bait Towers]].</ref><ref>{{cite news|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110809114318/http://www.bt.com.bn/classification/business/2007/04/15/mekah_residents_claim_their_needs_being_ignored |archive-date=9 August 2011 |url=http://www.bt.com.bn/classification/business/2007/04/15/mekah_residents_claim_their_needs_being_ignored |url-status=usurped |work=Brunei Times |title=Mekah residents claim their needs being ignored |first=Pakinam |last=Amer |date=15 April 2007}}</ref> The project involved the development of the hotel complex of [[The Clock Towers|Abraj Al-Bait (The Clock Towers)]], designed to accommodate up to 65,000 residents.<ref>{{Cite book |last=Records |first=Guinness World |url=https://www.google.com.sa/books/edition/Guinness_World_Records_2016/f896CgAAQBAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=abraj+al+bait+can+accommodate+residents&pg=PA126&printsec=frontcover |title=Guinness World Records 2016 |date=2015-09-10 |publisher=Guinness World Records |isbn=978-1-910561-03-4 |pages=126 |language=ar}}</ref> Located in close proximity to Masjid Al-Haram, the complex was developed to serve the millions of Muslim pilgrims who visit Mecca throughout the year.

==Reactions==

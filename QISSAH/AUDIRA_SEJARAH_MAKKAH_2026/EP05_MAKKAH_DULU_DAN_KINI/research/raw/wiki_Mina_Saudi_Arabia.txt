'''Mina''' ({{langx|ar|مِنَى|translit=Minā}}), nicknamed the "City of the [[Tent]]s",<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Patowary |first=Kaushik |title=Mina, The City of Tents |url=https://www.amusingplanet.com/2014/08/mina-city-of-tents.html |access-date=2020-08-07 |website=Amusing Planet}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last1=Tashkandi |first1=Hala |last2=Alameri |first2=Rua'a |date=2019-08-10 |title=Mina tent city hosts over 2 million Hajj pilgrims |url=https://www.arabnews.com/node/1538126/saudi-arabia |access-date=2020-08-07 |newspaper=[[Arab News]] |language=en}}</ref> is a valley located {{convert|8|km|mile|abbr=off|round=5}} southeast of the city of [[Mecca]], in the district of Masha'er, [[Mecca Province|Province of Makkah]] in the [[Hejaz]]i region of [[Saudi Arabia]]. Covering an area of approximately {{cvt|20|km²}}, Mina incorporates the tents, the area of ''[[Stoning of the Devil|Jamarat]]'', and the [[slaughterhouse]]s just outside the tents.<ref name=":0" />

Mina is most famous for its role in the ''[[Hajj]]'' ("Pilgrimage"). To accommodate the pilgrims who stay in Mina over multiple nights in the month of [[Dhu al-Hijjah]], more than 100,000 [[Air conditioning|air-conditioned]] tents have been built in the area, giving Mina the nickname "City of Tents." With a capacity of up to 3 million people, Mina has been called the largest [[tent city]] in the world.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":3">{{Cite web |title=WATCH: Flyover of Mina, the world's largest city of tents |url=https://www.iol.co.za/capeargus/watch-flyover-of-mina-the-worlds-largest-city-of-tents-10974647 |access-date=2020-08-07 |website=www.iol.co.za |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Mecca's $7,000-per-night makeshift room |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2015/09/mecca-7000-night-makeshift-room-150922100150365.html |access-date=2020-08-07 |newspaper=[[Al Jazeera English|Al Jazeera]]}}</ref> The three Jamarat, located in the Mina valley, are the location of the Jamarat, performed between [[sunrise]] and [[sunset]] in the final days of the Hajj. The stone throwing ritual commemorates the [[Prophets and messengers in Islam|Islamic prophet]] [[Abraham in Islam|Ibrahim]] ([[Abraham]])'s [[stoning of the Devil]], who wanted to prevent him from carrying out the command of [[God in Islam|Allah]] to [[Human sacrifice|sacrifice]] his son, [[Ishmael in Islam|Isma'il]] ([[Ishmael]]).<ref name=":5" />

== History ==
In Islamic tradition, Ibrahim left his wife, [[Hagar in Islam|Hajar]] ([[Hagar]]) and their infant son, Isma'il, in the valley of Mecca. Upon one of his visits to his family in Mecca, he was ordered by [[God]] in a [[dream]] to sacrifice his son in the Mina valley. While carrying out his son's sacrifice, he was interrupted by the ''[[Shaitan]]'' ([[Devil]]), and commanded by [[Allah]] to stone the Devil. The ritual of ''Jamarat'' is a commemoration of this belief.<ref name=":5">{{Cite web|title=The Jamarat|url=https://www.islamiclandmarks.com/makkah-hajj-places/jamarat|access-date=2020-10-30|language=en}}</ref> Mina is also believed to be the location of the [[Second pledge at al-Aqabah|pledges in Al-Aqabah]] of the [[Ansar (Islam)|Ansar]] to the Islamic prophet [[Muhammad in Islam|Muhammad]].<ref name="Mecca.net">{{cite web |website=Mecca.net |title=Nimra Mosque, Al-Khaif Mosque, Masjid Uqbah and Taneem Mosque |url=https://mecca.net/pictures-and-information-about-nimra-mosque-khaif-mosque-and-masjid-taneem/ |access-date=2023-04-11}}</ref>

Before the tents were built, pilgrims would bring their own tents to the valley and dismantle them while returning from the Hajj. Sometime in the 1990s, permanent cotton tents were installed by the Saudi government. These cotton tents were especially susceptible to fires, which took many pilgrims' lives. After the [[Mecca fire of 1997|1997 Mina fires]] which resulted in the deaths of more than 340 pilgrims, more than 100,000 permanent tents, measuring {{Convert|8|×|8|m|ft|abbr=}}, were built.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1" /><ref name=":2" /> These are constructed of [[fiberglass]] with an outer coating of [[Polytetrafluoroethylene|Teflon]] to protect them from fires. The tents are grouped into camps, each with their own exterior walls, and divided by the nationalities of pilgrims, providing temporary accommodation to up to 3 million pilgrims.<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":3" /> Each camp is equipped with a [[kitchen]], [[bathroom]]s, and [[Wudu|ablution]] facilities, and is connected to the other camps by pathways. The tents are also marked with unique color-and-number pairs to make them more identifiable.<ref name=":0" />

== Geography ==
[[File:Public Domain Mecca Maps 72.jpg|right|thumb|250x250px|An [[Aerial mapping|aerial map]] of Mina prepared by the [[Umm al-Qura University|Umm al-Qura' University]] in [[Mecca]]. The white tents can be seen in this picture, cut by the multiple pathways between them.]]

Mina is situated in the eponymous Mina valley, at an elevation of approximately {{convert|400|m|ft|abbr=on}}.<ref name=":4">{{Cite journal|last=Refai|first=Mohammed|date=2015-03-18|title=Solutions for Stoning the Devil in Pilgrimage using Simulation|journal=International Journal of Computer Applications|volume=113|issue=11|pages=5–8|doi=10.5120/19868-1853|issn=0975-8887|doi-access=free|bibcode=2015IJCA..113k...5R }}</ref> It is bordered by the ''al-'Aziziyah'' district of Mecca in the west, the 4th Ring Road to the north, [[Muzdalifah]] to the east, and the ''al-Jami'ah'' district to the south. The westernmost features of Mina are the three Jamarat, which are stoned during the ''Jamarat''. These are, the ''Jamarat al-Sughra'<nowiki/>'', the westernmost and smallest, the ''Jamarat al-Wusta'<nowiki/>'', the intermediary, and the ''Jamarat al-Kubra''', also called ''Jamarat al-'Aqabah,'' the largest and easternmost. In a 2003 study, the distance between the first two was measured to be {{convert|135|m|ft|abbr=on}} and the distance between the latter two was measured at {{convert|225|m|ft|abbr=off}}.<ref name=":4" /> To the north-east of Mina are the slaughterhouses, where the [[Animal sacrifice|sacrificial animals]] of the ''Hajj'' pilgrims are slaughtered. The ''Masjid al-Khayf'' is located in the centre of the Mina valley, and is active only during the Hajj.

=== Transport ===
[[Highway 40 (Saudi Arabia)|Highway 40]], one of Saudi Arabia's most important highways, is less than {{Convert|3|km|mile|abbr=on}} from Mina and is accessible through the Al Hajj Street. [[Al Mashaaer Al Mugaddassah Metro line|The Mashaer Al-Muqaddassah Metro]], currently Saudi Arabia's only complete metro line, is active only during the Hajj, and ends in Mina, near the ''Jamarat al-'Aqaba'', with Mina Station 3. Running northwest, it starts in [[Mount Arafat|'Arafat]], going through Muzdalifah, before reaching Mina Station 1, near the Armed Forces Hospital. The next station, less than {{convert|1|km|mile|abbr=off}}, is the Mina Station 2, located near the Mina al-Jisr Hospital. Finally, the line terminates near the Jamarat, at Mina Station 3.

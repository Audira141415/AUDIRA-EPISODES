'''Daniel van der Meulen''' (4 September 1894{{sndash}}24 September 1989) was a Dutch civil servant who served as the consul to [[Jeddah]] from 1926 through 1931. Born in [[Laren, North Holland|Laren]], van der Meulen entered the civil service and was assigned to the [[Dutch East Indies]] in 1915. He was chosen by the Islamicist [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]] to serve as the Netherlands' consul in Jeddah, focusing mainly on facilitating the [[hajj]] pilgrimage for residents of the Dutch East Indies. After his time as consul ended, van der Meulen returned to the Indies, though he made several further trips to the [[Arabian Peninsula]]. He maintained diplomatic roles in Jeddah during the Second World War and in [[Jakarta]] during the [[Indonesian National Revolution]].

==Early life and consular career==
Van der Meulen was born in [[Laren, North Holland]],{{sfn|Trouw|1989|p=8}} on 4 September 1894. He entered the Dutch civil service in 1915,{{sfn|De Nieuwe Limburger|1961|p=17}} spending some time stationed among the [[Batak people]] of northern Sumatra in the [[Dutch East Indies]].{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=530}}

Van der Meulen was later selected by the Islamicist [[Christiaan Snouck Hurgronje]] to serve as the Netherlands' consul in [[Jeddah]].{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=530}} In preparation for the assignment, van der Meulen was educated in the teachings of Islam and the Arabic language by Snouck Hurgronje.{{sfn|van der Meulen|1961|p=ix}} He arrived in Saudi Arabia in 1926.{{sfn|Morrien|1977|p=5}} Despite the title of consul, writing in 1961, van der Meulen recalled having few consular duties.{{sfn|van der Meulen|1961|p=ix}} Rather, his main duty was facilitating the [[hajj]] pilgrimage for residents of the Dutch East Indies.{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=530}} 

Van der Meulen's other duties in Jeddah were varied. He helped coordinate a Dutch{{ndash}}Saudi response to health crises in the region, working with the Dutch doctor [[Pieter van der Hoog]].{{sfn|Ryad|2017|p=191}} He facilitated the filming of [[George Krugers]]'s 1928 documentary ''[[The Great Mecca Feast]]'', appearing towards the end of the film on a ship transporting pilgrims.{{sfn|Reichling|2022|p=353}} In his memoir, he also recalled being tasked with identifying members of the burgeoning [[Indonesian National Awakening|Indonesian nationalist movement]] who were fleeing to the [[Hejaz]] to escape prosecution in the Indies, requesting extradition from King [[Ibn Saud]], and returning them to the Indies for internment. Van der Meulen remained consul through 1931.{{sfn|Morrien|1977|p=5}}

==Later life==
After completing his term as consul in Jeddah, van der Meulen returned to the Indies.{{sfn|Morrien|1977|p=5}} However, he also undertook several trips through the Arabian Peninsula. In 1931, he undertook a trip to the [[Yemeni Imamate]] via [[Hodeidah]] and [[Sanaa]] to establish a treaty with the Yemeni government. That year, he also accompanied the German geographer [[Hermann von Wissmann (geographer)|Hermann von Wissmann]] to the [[Hadhramaut]] to chart the region. Another trip with Wissman was undertaken in 1939, this time to identify new routes while simultaneously collecting archaeological, botanical, and geological data. The second trip required them to pay for protective escorts from local warlords.{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=531}} 

In 1941, during the Second World War, the [[Dutch government-in-exile]] dispatched van der Meulen to Jeddah, tasking him with preventing Arab rulers from supporting [[Nazi Germany]]. He remained in this position, which also required him to facilitate the few hajj pilgrims entering the region, until 1945.{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=530}} In this capacity, he undertook another trip to the Hadhramaut, seeking information on the consequences of the [[Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies|Japanese occupation]] government's ban on people in the Indies sending money to the region.{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=532}} At war's end, van der Meulen returned to the Indies amidst the [[Indonesian National Revolution]]. He participated in numerous discussions, including the [[Malino Conference]]. In his memoir, van der Meulen characterized the Dutch response as divided, with representatives in [[Jakarta]] more interested in a compromise than those back in the Netherlands.{{sfn|Morrien|1977|p=5}}

Van der Meulen was the focus of a 1961 episode of ''Silhouet'', a series of biographical documentaries produced by the [[Katholieke Radio Omroep]].{{sfn|De Nieuwe Limburger|1961|p=17}} In 1977, he published {{lang|nl|Hoort Gij de Donder Niet?}} (''Do You Hear the Thunder?''), a history of his experiences in the Dutch East Indies and Saudi Arabia.{{sfn|Morrien|1977|p=5}} Van der Meulen died on 24 September 1989. A memorial service was announced for the Reformed Church in [[Eefde]], with burial in the public cemetery in [[Gorssel]] to follow.{{sfn|Trouw|1989|p=8}}

==Legacy==
Van der Meulen was a member of the [[Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences]] from 1947 until his resignation in 1955.{{sfn|KNAW}} In 1947, he was granted the [[Gold Medal (RGS)|Patron's Medal]] by the [[Royal Geographical Society]] for his travels in the Hadhramaut and documentation of Saudi Arabia.{{sfn|RGS|2025}} Van der Meulen was also an officer in the [[Order of Orange-Nassau]].{{sfn|Trouw|1989|p=8}}

Van der Meulen took numerous photographs of the Arabian Peninsula. These were exhibited at a 1995 exhibition at the [[Tropenmuseum]] in Amsterdam.{{sfn|Amelink|1995|p=4}} In 2003, a collection of photographs taken by van der Meulen during his journeys to Yemen was compiled by Steven Vink and published as ''Daniel van der Meulen in Arabia Felix. Travels and Photographs of a Dutch Diplomat in Yemen, 1931–1944''. This book, 128 pages in length,{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=530}} contains approximately a hundred photographs of the region. Most photographs focus on landscapes and towns, including the [[wadi]]s and [[adobe]] architecture of the region. Several photographs depict people, including [[Yemenite Jews|the Jewish minority]] of Sanaa, as well as prominent leaders in al-Quwairah and [[Seiyun]].{{sfn|de Jong|2004|p=532}}

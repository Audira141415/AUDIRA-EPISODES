## https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_–_Men%27s_100_metre_butterfly
U.S. swimmer [[Michael Phelps]] set a new Olympic record of 50.58 to defend his title in the event, edging out Serbia's [[Milorad Čavić]] (50.59) by one hundredth of a second (0.01). He also earned his seventh Olympic gold at a single Games, tying [[Mark Spitz]]'s 1972 record for the most gold medals. [REF https://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/news/olympics-swimming-huge-back-half-gives-michael-phelps-olympic-record-in-100-fly-matches-mark-spitz-with-seven-golds-1-million-richer/]  [REF http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/sports/olympics/beijing/swimming/2008-08-15-phelps-100-butterfly_n.htm]  Australia's [[Andrew Lauterstein]] earned a bronze in 51.12, finishing in a close race against world record holder [[Ian Crocker]] by the slimmest margin. Phelps' triumph occurred after Čavić had remarked that it would be better for the sport if Phelps was defeated. Phelps' margin of triumph was so close that the Serbian team filed a protest, but after officials reviewed the video, the [[International Swimming Federation]] (FINA) announced that Phelps did touch the wall first and his victory would be upheld.
The final took place on 16 August, at 10:10 CST. [REF http://results.beijing2008.cn/WRM/ENG/Schedule/SW_2008-08-16.shtml]  Before the race, Milorad Čavić made headlines by saying in an interview that it would be better for swimming if he beat Phelps. Phelps' coach [[Bob Bowman (coach)|Bob Bowman]] used the quote to provide motivation to his protégé. In an interview, Phelps said that doubters like Čavić "fires me up more than anything, I always welcome comments. It definitely motivates me even more." Almost immediately after the race started, Čavić took the lead with Phelps getting off to a slow start. At the turn, Čavić was first, followed by Ian Crocker, [REF https://www.usatoday.com/sports/olympics/beijing/swimming/2008-08-15-phelps-100-butterfly_N.htm]  while Phelps made the split in seventh place, just 0.62 seconds behind Čavić. As the two approached the finish, Čavić tried to coast to the wall on one last stroke, while Phelps, who had misjudged the end, took an extra half-stroke, causing both competitors to touch the wall at almost exactly the same time. [REF https://www.nytimes.com/2008/08/16/sports/olympics/16swim.html?_r=1&oref=slogin]  It turned out that Phelps had actually finished one one-hundredth of a second ahead of Čavić, with a time of 50.58 seconds. [REF https://sports.yahoo.com/olympics/beijing/swimming/news?slug=ap-swm-swimming&prov=ap&type=lgns]  Phelps even admitted that, at first, he thought the extra stroke he took had cost him the gold medal, until he looked at the scoreboard displaying the results. [REF http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/2008/olympics/2008/08/15/phelps.100.butterfly.ap/]  [[Andrew Lauterstein]] won the bronze medal, also beating Crocker by one one-hundredth of a second.
Several records were broken at the final. For the first time at the 2008 Summer Olympics, Phelps did not break the current world record in a final, finishing eighteen one-hundredths of a second behind team-mate Crocker's 50.40-second world record from 2005; he did though set a new [[List of Olympic records in swimming|Olympic record]]. Three continental records were broken in the final, with Fujii setting a new [[List of Asian records in swimming|Asian record]], Čavić a new [[List of European records in swimming|European record]], and Lauterstein a new [[List of Oceanian records in swimming|Oceanic record]]. [REF http://www.scmsom.se/records/events/100%20m%20Butterfly%20Men%20Long%20Course.htm]  Although he finished last, swimmer Ryan Pini made history as the first swimmer from [[Papua New Guinea]] to swim in an Olympic final, and even received a call from the [[Prime Minister of Papua New Guinea|prime minister]] [[Michael Somare]] congratulating him. [REF http://www.radioaustralia.net.au/news/stories/200808/s2337520.htm?tab=sport]  Most notably though, Phelps won his seventh gold medal at these Games, tying [[Mark Spitz]]'s [[List of multiple Olympic gold medalists at a single Games|record for most gold medals won at a single Olympic Games]]. For being able to emulate Spitz's record, [[Speedo]], a sponsor of Michael Phelps, awarded him a US$1 million bonus, which had already been offered to him at the [[2004 Summer Olympics]], under the same conditions. After the final, the [[NBC|National Broadcasting Company]] (NBC) arranged a joint interview with Phelps and Spitz, where Spitz praised Phelps' effort, telling him that "what you did tonight was epic" and even though at one point, Phelps was more than half a second behind Čavić, Spitz "never thought for one moment you (Phelps) were out of that race."

## https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Phelps
Before the final of the 100-meter butterfly, U.S. born Serbian swimmer [[Milorad Čavić]] caused a minor stir when he said it would be "good" if Phelps lost. "It'd be good for him if he loses. It would be nice if historians talk about Michael Phelps winning seven gold medals and losing the eighth to 'some guy.' I'd like to be that guy", Čavić said. [REF ?]  Phelps responded, "When people say things like that, it fires me up more than anything." [REF ?]  On August 16, Phelps won his seventh gold medal of the Games in the men's 100-meter butterfly, setting an Olympic record for the event with a time of 50.58 seconds and edging out his nearest competitor Čavić, by one hundredth (0.01) of a second. [REF ?] 
Phelps's finish 0.01 seconds ahead of Čavić prompted the Serbian delegation to file a protest. Subsequent analysis of the video by the [[International Swimming Federation|FINA]] panel, which required analyzing frames shot 1/10,000th of a second apart, was used to officially confirm Phelps's victory, [REF ?]  but the images were not immediately released to the press. The initial refusal by official timekeeper Omega to release underwater photos of the finish also raised questions due to Phelps's sponsorship relationship with Omega. [REF https://www.nytimes.com/2008/08/21/sports/olympics/21longman.html]  Čavić later wrote in his blog, "People, this is the greatest moment of my life. If you ask me, it should be accepted and we should move on. I've accepted defeat, and there's nothing wrong with losing to the greatest swimmer there has ever been." [REF https://www.nytimes.com/2008/08/17/sports/olympics/17cavic.html] 
Phelps's seventh gold medal of the Games tied [[Mark Spitz]]'s [[List of multiple Olympic gold medalists at a single Games|record]] for gold medals won in a single Olympic Games, set in the 1972 Olympics. It was also his fifth individual gold medal in Beijing, tying the [[List of multiple Olympic gold medalists at a single Games#List of most individual gold medals won at a single Olympic Games|record]] for individual gold medals at a single Games originally set by [[Eric Heiden]] in the 1980 Winter Olympics and equaled by [[Vitaly Scherbo]] at the 1992 Summer Games. Said Phelps upon setting his seventh-straight Olympic record of the Games in as many events, "Dream as big as you can dream, and anything is possible ... I am sort of in a dream world. Sometimes I have to pinch myself to make sure it is real." [REF http://sports.espn.go.com/oly/summer08/swimming/news/story?id=3537831] 
On August 17, Phelps won his eighth gold medal in the 4×100-meter medley relay, breaking [[Mark Spitz]]'s record of seven gold medals won in a single Olympic Games, which had stood since 1972. [REF ?]  Phelps, along with teammates [[Brendan Hansen]], [[Aaron Peirsol]], and Jason Lezak, set a new world record in the event with a time of 3 minutes and 29.34 seconds, 0.7 seconds ahead of second-place Australia and 1.34 seconds faster than the previous record set by the United States at the 2004 Olympic Games in Athens. When Phelps dived in to swim the 100-meter butterfly leg, the third leg of the 400-meter medley, the United States had been trailing Australia and Japan. Phelps completed his split in 50.1 seconds, the fastest butterfly split ever for the event, giving teammate Jason Lezak a more than half-second lead for the final leg, which he held onto to clinch the event in world record time. [REF http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/2008/olympics/2008/08/16/bc.oly.swm.phelps.medley.relay.ap/]  Said Phelps, upon completing the event that awarded him his eighth gold medal and eighth Olympic record in as many events, "Records are always made to be broken no matter what they are ... Anybody can do anything that they set their mind to." [REF http://edition.cnn.com/2008/SPORT/08/17/phelps.history.eight.golds/] 

## https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milorad_%C4%8Cavi%C4%87
Born in [[Anaheim, California]] and a citizen of both [[Serbia]] and the [[United States]], he attended [[Tustin High School]] in [[Tustin, California]], where he set four CIF records and a national high school mark in the 50&nbsp;yd freestyle. [REF https://web.archive.org/web/20080829142655/http://www.ocregister.com/articles/cavic-phelps-olympic-2125547-won-games]  While swimming for the [[University of California, Berkeley]] and training with [[Mike Bottom]], Čavić set a new school and [[Pac-12 Conference|Pac-10]] record in the 100&nbsp;yd butterfly (45.44&nbsp;s).
On August 14, 2008 at the [[2008 Summer Olympics|Beijing Olympics]], Čavić broke the 100 meter butterfly Olympic Record during the preliminary heats, finishing ahead of [[Michael Phelps]] and also recorded the fastest time in the semi-finals. Čavić came in second to Phelps by one hundredth of a second in the final, and became Serbia's inaugural Olympiad medalist. It was Phelps' seventh gold medal of the Olympiad. The Serbian team initially challenged the result but FINA later confirmed the finish. It was later confirmed by the FINA and Omega timekeeping officials that Čavić indeed arrived first but it was Phelps who, in the milliseconds after touching the wall, applied more force to trigger an electronic touchpad first. [REF https://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/news/mark-spitz-allegedly-claims-michael-phelps-did-not-win-2008-100-fly-olympic-gold/]  Čavić later wrote in his blog: "People, this is the greatest moment of my life. If you ask me, it should be accepted and we should move on. I’ve accepted defeat, and there’s nothing wrong with losing to the greatest swimmer there has ever been". [REF ?] 

## https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics
At the 2008 Summer Olympics, new [[world records in swimming|world records]] were set 25 times (affecting 21 distinct world records) and new [[Olympic Games records in swimming|Olympic records]] were set 65 times and one other was equalled (affecting 30 distinct Olympic records). [REF http://results.beijing2008.cn/WRM/ENG/INF/SW/C81B/SW0000000.shtml#SWM054901]  Only [[Ian Thorpe]]'s 3:40.59 in the 400 metres freestyle and [[Inge de Bruijn]]'s 56.61 in the 100 metres butterfly, both set in Sydney, remained Olympic records. [[Michael Phelps]] of the United States also broke the record for the most gold medals ever won by an Olympian with a total of 14; 8 of which were won during the 2008 Summer Olympics - this was also a world record.
Another big change to swimming occurred when [[Speedo]] launched the [[LZR Racer]] swim suits on February 13, 2008. The suits, developed by the [[Australian Institute of Sport]], [REF http://www.physorg.com/news123416635.html=en]  were designed to repel water, allow oxygen to flow to the muscles, and hold the body in a more hydrodynamic position. [REF http://news.bbc.co.uk/2/hi/in_depth/7434159.stm]  [REF https://www.sciencehistory.org/distillations/magazine/winning-skin]  The suits had been proven to give the swimmer a lower time by 1.9 to 2.2%. Due to the advantage provided by the suits, some swimmers complained about the fairness in its use, because some people used multiple swimsuits to improve buoyancy and compressing of body; [REF http://www.gizmodo.com.au/2008/04/speedo_lzr_racer_swimsuit_spawns_copycats_and_controversy-2.html]  the official blog for the [[National Collegiate Athletic Association]] pondered whether they were "[[technology doping]]" and what was the difference between gaining advantage from a swimsuit and gaining advantage from [[performance-enhancing drugs]]. [REF http://search.japantimes.co.jp/cgi-bin/fd20080810pb.html]  In response to these complaints, the [[International Swimming Federation]] (FINA) scheduled a meeting with Speedo to discuss the suits. After the meeting, FINA dismissed the claims of cheating, and endorsed the suits for future swimming meets. By August 14, 2008, 62 world records had been broken by swimmers wearing the [[LZR Racer]]. [REF https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB121865005500237497] 

## https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Spitz
'''Mark Andrew Spitz''' (born February 10, 1950) is an American former competitive [[swimmer]] and nine-time [[Olympic Games|Olympic]] champion. He was the [[Lists of Olympic medalists#Medalist with most medals by Olympiad|most successful]] athlete at the [[1972 Summer Olympics]] in [[Munich]], winning [[Swimming at the 1972 Summer Olympics|seven gold medals]], each in world-record time. [REF https://www.olympics.com/en/athletes/mark-spitz]  This achievement set a record that lasted for 36 years, until it was surpassed by fellow American [[Michael Phelps]], who won eight golds at the [[2008 Summer Olympics]] in [[Beijing]]. Phelps, like Spitz, set seven world records.
At the [[Swimming at the 1972 Summer Olympics|1972 Summer Olympics]] in [[Munich]], Spitz was back to repeat his quest for the six gold medals. He did even better, winning seven Olympic gold medals. Furthermore, Spitz set a new world record in each of the seven events – 100-meter freestyle (51.22), 200-meter freestyle (1:52.78), 100-meter butterfly (54.27), 200-meter butterfly (2:00.70), 4×100-meter freestyle relay (3:26.42), 4×200-meter freestyle relay (7:35.78), and 4×100-meter medley relay (3:48.16). Spitz was originally reluctant to swim the 100-meter freestyle, fearing that he would not win the gold medal. Minutes before the race, he confessed on the pool deck to [[American Broadcasting Company|ABC]]'s [[Donna de Varona]], "I know I say I don't want to swim before every event, but this time I'm serious. If I swim six and win six, I'll be a hero. If I swim seven and win six, I'll be a failure." Spitz won by half a stroke in a world-record time of 51.22 seconds. ## https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquatic_timing_system
Prior to the 1950s, competitive swimmers relied on the sound of a [[starting pistol]] to start their races and [[Mechanical watch|mechanical]] [[stopwatch]]es to record their times at the end of a race. A limitation of [[analog clock|analog timekeeping]] was the technology's inability to reliably record times accurately below one tenth (0.1) of a second. [REF https://www.swisstiming.com/sports/swimming/]  In 1967, the Omega company of Switzerland developed the first electronic timing system for swimming that attempted to coordinate the physical the recorded time. This new system placed contact pads (known as '''Touchpad''') in each lane of the pool, calibrated in such a fashion that the incidental water movement of the competitors or wave action did not trigger the pad sensors; the pad was only activated by the touch of the swimmer at the end of the race. [REF https://www.istime.com/]

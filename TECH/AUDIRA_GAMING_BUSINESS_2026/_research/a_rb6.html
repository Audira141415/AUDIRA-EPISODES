The Roblox Creator Economy in 2026: The Statistics

Maya Collins·07/08/2026·Updated 09/09/2026·9 min read

Roblox enters 2026 as the largest user-generated content economy in gaming, having crossed a symbolic threshold in 2025: creators collectively cashed out more than $1.5 billion through its Developer Exchange program. With a record daily audience, a maturing over-18 cohort and an improved payout rate, the creator side of Roblox is now a genuine, if steeply unequal, earnings engine. This page compiles the most recent verified figures for reporters covering the platform. Free to use with a link back to ShaneTheGamer as the source.

Key Takeaways for Journalists

  • $1.5B+ paid to creators in 2025, Roblox’s DevEx program passed the milestone for the first time, per Roblox.
  • $477M in a single quarter, Q4 2025 DevEx fees grew 70% year over year, according to Roblox’s Q4 earnings.
  • 144M daily active users, Roblox hit a record DAU count in Q4 2025, up 69% year over year.
  • ~29,000 DevEx creators, the number cashing out real money, with a median payout of $1,440 over the year to June 2025.
  • $1.3M average for the top 1,000, the platform’s leading creators earned that on average in 2025, up more than 50% year over year.
  • DevEx rate up 8.5%, the standard rate rose to $0.0038 per earned Robux on 5 September 2025.
  • 3.5M developers, the estimated size of the creator community building on Roblox.
  • ~85% of developers earn under $100/month, a stark reminder of the platform’s power-law distribution.
  • $4B+ paid all-time, cumulative DevEx payouts since the program launched.
  • $1.8B–$2.0B projected for 2026, an independent estimate of creator payouts if the payout ratio holds.

How Big the Creator Payout Pool Got in 2025

The headline number for 2025 is unambiguous. In 2025, Roblox creators earned over $1.5 billion through DevEx, a program that lets creators exchange their earned Robux for real money, according to Roblox’s own newsroom. That figure is a step-change from the prior year, as of September 2025, developers had earned over $1 billion, compared with $923 million for the full year in 2024.

The trajectory through the year accelerated. In the first quarter, DevEx was up 39% year on year at $281,000,000. That’s 27% of revenue, 23% of bookings, Roblox CEO David Baszucki told investors. By year-end the pace had picked up sharply: in Q4, developer exchange (DevEx) fees grew 70% year over year to $477 million, reflecting both platform growth and an 8.5% increase in the DevEx rate announced in September.

Independent trackers frame 2025 as a breakout. One creator-economy analysis characterised it as a $1.5B in total payouts year with a Q4 2025 record-breaking $477M quarter. Cumulatively, the picture is larger still, cumulative all-time payouts exceed $4 billion since the DevEx program launched.

Looking ahead, the same analysis projects continued growth. Roblox’s 2026 revenue guidance of $6.02B-$6.29B implies that developer payouts could reach $1.8 billion to $2.0 billion in 2026, assuming the payout-to-revenue ratio continues its gradual improvement toward 31-32%. This would represent a 20-33% increase over 2025 payouts. These are estimates, not guarantees.

Who Actually Earns: The Power-Law Distribution

The aggregate payout pool disguises extreme concentration. According to a creator-earnings analysis, roughly 85 percent of developers earn under $100 per month and many earn nothing. About 10 percent make $100 to $1,000, around 3 to 4 percent earn $1,000 to $10,000 as a full-time income, and under 0.1 percent earn $100,000 or more monthly with annual earnings exceeding $1M.

Roblox’s own economic-impact data confirms how modest the median is. Of the millions of creators monetizing on Roblox, over 29,000 are part of our DevEx Program, with the median creator participating in the DevEx Program receiving $1,440 USD during the 12 months ended June 30, 2025. Note that this median is for the minority who reach DevEx eligibility at all, the vast majority of the community never crosses the cash-out threshold.

The top of the pyramid, by contrast, is lucrative and growing. For the full year, the top 1,000 creators earned an average of $1.3 million, up more than 50% from the prior year. Roblox’s 2024 report showed the tiers above that: the top 10 developers earned an average of $33.9 million, and the top 100 developers earned an average of $6 million, up over 450% and 500%, respectively, since 2019.

Encouragingly, the “viable middle” appears to be expanding. According to a creator-economy trend report, the number of developers earning more than $10,000 annually grew by an estimated 35%, outpacing the growth in total payouts and suggesting that the distribution is slowly broadening.

The DevEx Rate and How Cash-Out Works

DevEx is the mechanism that turns in-game currency into real income. DevEx lets eligible Roblox creators convert earned Robux to USD at $0.0038 per Robux (as of September 5, 2025). That was an increase: all creators will now earn approximately 8.5% more when turning their Earned Robux into cash. Before the change, the standard exchange rate was 0.0035, which comes out to $105 USD for 30,000 Earned Robux.

Eligibility rules set a floor that filters out casual creators. You need at least 30,000 earned Robux in your account balance to submit a DevEx request. Only monetisation earnings count, only Robux earned through monetizing your games count. Purchased Robux, gifted Robux, and Robux received through the old Premium Payouts program (replaced by Creator Rewards in July 2025) have their own eligibility rules based on when they were earned.

Roblox has also begun differentiating the rate by audience. In April 2026 the company announced a 42% increase in the Roblox Developer Exchange (DevEx) rate for spend by U.S. players 18 and older in eligible games starting June 8. The commercial logic is that older users spend more, in the U.S., the 18 to 34 user cohort on Roblox monetizes over 50% higher than our under 18 users.

Management frames these payouts as strategy rather than cost. As one outlet summarised, Roblox’s content model depends on sustained creator participation at scale. Management has been explicit that it expects DevEx rates to rise over time.

Platform Scale Driving the Economy

The creator pool grows because the audience grows. In Q4 2025, Roblox generated $1.4 billion in revenue, up 43% year over year, and $2.2 billion in bookings, up 63%. Average daily active users reached 144 million, a 69% increase, while hours engaged climbed to 35 billion.

Full-year figures underscore the scale. Roblox generated $4.9 billion in revenue for the full year 2025, up 36% from $3.6 billion in 2024. Bookings, the measure of Robux purchased by players, totaled $6.8 billion, a 55% year-over-year increase. Because bookings represent the gross Robux players buy, they are the pool from which creator earnings ultimately flow.

Engagement was seasonal but sharply higher. The platform started Q1 at 97.8 million DAUs, climbed to 111.8 million in Q2, peaked at 151.5 million in Q3 driven by summer breaks and the viral success of Steal a Brainrot, then settled at 144 million in Q4. Across the year, Roblox averaged 126.5 million daily active users across all four quarters of 2025.

On a monthly basis the reach is vast. Roblox has 381.8 million active users, roughly one in nine of the estimated 3.42 billion active video game players worldwide. The platform also surpassed 1.5 billion registered accounts globally by the end of 2025. For readers tracking wider industry scale, our gaming industry statistics page offers context.

Top Games and Where the Money Concentrates

Creator income is dominated by a handful of mega-hits. According to Statista, as of January 2026, Brookhaven RP was the most popular Roblox game of all time, with over 78.19 billion visits. Anime-inspired Blox Fruits came in second, having amassed 58.49 billion visits.

New entrants can scale extraordinarily fast. Grow a Garden was released in March 2025 and has been breaking user records since its release. The farming simulator already ranked fifth among the most-visited Roblox games of all time. Grow a Garden was the fastest experience on Roblox to reach one billion total visits on the platform, a feat the game achieved in its first 33 days.

Estimated revenues for the biggest titles run into the tens of millions. Independent analysis puts Adopt Me at $50 to $80M plus, Brookhaven at $30 to $50M plus (largely built by a single developer), Blox Fruits at $40 to $70M plus, and Murder Mystery 2 at $10 to $20M plus. These are estimates, since Roblox does not publish per-game revenue.

The economics of building a sustainable studio are demanding but attainable. One analysis estimates that given Roblox’s approximately 30% developer revenue share, this translates to needing roughly $1.4 million in gross game revenue, which typically requires sustaining 5,000-15,000 daily active players with strong monetization.

Monetization Streams and Global Reach

The 2025–2026 monetisation toolkit is broader than ever. Roblox has rolled out new monetization features, including Price Optimization, Rewarded Video, Creator Rewards, and Regional Pricing. Rewarded video advertising is the newest pillar: in pilots, games participating in the pilot in late 2025 reported rewarded ads contributing 5-15% of total revenue with minimal impact on player experience metrics. As the program scales to hundreds of experiences in early 2026, this is expected to become a meaningful fourth pillar of developer monetization.

The creator base is genuinely global, and DevEx eligibility is spreading fast in emerging markets. Roblox reports that between Q4 2022 and Q4 2025, the percentage of creators eligible to receive DevEx payouts has increased 176% in Indonesia, 415% in Japan, and 303% in Korea, underscoring the tremendous growth in these countries.

Beyond direct payouts, Roblox quantifies a wider economic footprint. Its 2024 U.S. report found that in the United States alone, Roblox has generated a substantial economic impact, contributing $445 million in GDP to the economy in 2024. From 2017 to 2024, Roblox’s total GDP impact on the U.S. economy reached $1.62 billion.

Access to that global audience runs largely through mobile. According to one 2026 statistics roundup, mobile devices account for 72% of Roblox activity, PC handles 25%, and consoles make up the remaining 3%. Roblox is available on iOS, Android, PC, Mac, Xbox, and Meta Quest VR headsets. For adjacent creator-economy comparisons, see our Twitch statistics reference.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much did Roblox creators earn in 2025?

Creators earned over $1.5 billion through DevEx in 2025, a first-ever milestone, according to Roblox, up from $923 million for full-year 2024.

What is the current DevEx exchange rate?

As of 5 September 2025, the standard DevEx rate is $0.0038 per earned Robux, an 8.5% increase from the previous $0.0035 rate.

How much do top Roblox creators make?

The top 1,000 creators averaged $1.3 million in 2025, up more than 50% year over year, while Roblox’s 2024 report showed the top 10 averaging $33.9 million.

How many developers cash out real money on Roblox?

More than 29,000 creators participate in DevEx, with a median payout of $1,440 for the 12 months ending June 30, 2025, per Roblox.

What share of developers actually make significant money?

Roughly 85% earn under $100 a month and under 0.1% earn over $1M a year, reflecting a steep power-law distribution, per independent analysis.

Methodology & Sourcing

Figures on this page are compiled from Roblox Corporation’s Q1 and Q4 2025 earnings materials and newsroom posts, Roblox’s Economic Impact Reports (conducted by Nordicity and Access Partnership), Statista visit data, and independent creator-economy analyses. Where Roblox does not disclose a precise value, for example per-game revenue or the earnings distribution, we label figures as estimates or ranges and identify the third party responsible. Quarterly Q2/Q3 DevEx values in the payout chart are approximated to reconcile with the verified $1.5B full-year total and should be read as illustrative. Projections for 2026 are explicitly labelled and depend on Roblox’s revenue guidance and an assumed payout-to-revenue ratio; actuals may differ materially.

    Wemade to Maintain WEMIX Ecosystem, Pursue Overseas Relisting First

    CEO says domestic exchange return will take time; executive pay cap proposal rejected

    • Published 2026.03.30 10:07

    Wemade said it will continue to develop its blockchain platform WEMIX over the long term, even as the company seeks to relist its cryptocurrency on overseas exchanges before attempting a return to domestic won-based platforms.

    Overseas Listings Prioritized as Domestic Relisting Faces Delays

    Park Kwan-ho, chief executive of Wemade, said at a shareholder meeting on March 27 that relisting WEMIX on South Korea’s won-based exchanges “will take time.”

    “Listings are continuing on global exchanges, including in Thailand and the Philippines, and we expect results soon,” Park said during a post-meeting investor session.

    WEMIX is both a cryptocurrency issued by Wemade and the name of its blockchain platform, forming the foundation of the company’s Web3 business. Wemade launched the token in 2020 and expanded the platform with “WEMIX 3.0” in 2022.

    However, the token was delisted from major domestic exchanges for a second time following a 2025 hacking incident and related controversy over the company’s response. Market participants have expressed skepticism about the likelihood of a third relisting in South Korea. The company has also scaled down its blockchain-related organization from 11 divisions across three centers to a single unit.

    Despite these setbacks, Park emphasized that the company has no plans to abandon the WEMIX ecosystem.

    “We have already built a structure in which WEMIX can be utilized through our online transaction systems if games succeed,” he said. “We are not aiming for immediate results, but we will continue without giving up.”

    CEO Pay Cap Proposal Fails to Pass

    At the same shareholder meeting, all agenda items were approved as proposed, except for a resolution on executive compensation limits.

    The proposal was unusually split into two separate votes at shareholders’ request: one covering the CEO’s compensation cap (20 billion won) and another covering other directors (60 billion won). While the latter passed, the CEO compensation cap failed to secure the required shareholder approval.

    Wemade said it will proceed with its planned game pipeline and expects meaningful contributions from major new titles beginning in the first half of next year.

    Key projects currently in development include the PC and console-based open-world action RPG “Project TAL,” the mobile MMORPG “Night Crows 2,” and the subculture RPG “MO TF.”

    Separately, affiliate Wemade Next has undergone a leadership reshuffle, with roughly one-third of its workforce reportedly leaving through voluntary resignation. CEO Park Jung-soo has stepped down, to be succeeded by Sung Jung-kuk, a development executive known for his role in “MIR4.”

- Born c. 936 near Cordoba; died c. 1013. Medieval surgeon of Andalusian Spain.
- Britannica says court physician to caliph Abd al-Rahman III al-Nasir (other sources say al-Hakam II; Spink & Lewis 1973 say there is no contemporary evidence for either).
- Wrote Al-Tasrif li-man ajaz an al-ta'alif ("The Method"), a medical work in 30 parts.
- Much of it based on earlier authorities, especially the Epitomae of 7th-century Byzantine physician Paul of Aegina; contained original observations incl. earliest known description of hemophilia.
- Last chapter (surgery) has drawings of more than 200 instruments; Britannica calls it the first illustrated independent work on surgery.
- Largely ignored by physicians in the eastern Islamic world; huge influence in Christian Europe.
- Translated into Latin in 12th c. by Gerard of Cremona; nearly 500 years the leading surgery textbook in Europe, preferred even to Galen for its concise lucidity.

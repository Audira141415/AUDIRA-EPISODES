Read 2026-11-11 via browser (curl blocked by Cloudflare). Notes (paraphrase + short quotes):
- Born c. 965 Basra, Iraq; died c. 1040 Cairo.
- "Conflicting stories are told about the life of Ibn al-Haytham", esp. the Nile scheme.
- Version told by Ibn al-Qifti (d. 1248): invited by al-Hakim (r. 996-1021, "The Mad Caliph") to regulate the Nile; reconnoitred near southern border of Egypt; confessed inability; given official post; feared for life, feigned madness, confined to his home until end of al-Hakim's caliphate. Then earned living copying manuscripts; al-Qifti claimed to own a manuscript in his hand from 1040.
- Three lists of writings, first with autobiography (1027), almost 100 works.
- "It has recently been plausibly argued that there were two Ibn al-Haythams": al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan (optics mathematician) and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan (astronomer-philosopher, autobiography, lists 1-2). [Hypothesis of R. Rashed]
- Kitab al-manazir (Optics): correct model of vision = passive reception by eyes of light rays reflected from objects, not emanation from eyes. Combines experiment with math. Complete laws of reflection; refraction explained by light moving slower in denser media. Alhazen's problem.
- Sura al-kusuf ("On the Shape of the Eclipse") includes a discussion of the camera obscura.
- Optics neglected in the East until Kamal al-Din al-Farisi's commentary (d. 1320). Latin translation by unknown scholar, probably early 13th c. Influenced Roger Bacon, Johannes Kepler (1571-1630).

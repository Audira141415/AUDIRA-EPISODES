The '''Catalan Atlas''' ({{langx|ca|Atles català}}, {{IPA|ca|ˈatləs kətəˈla|label=Eastern Catalan:}}) is a medieval world map, or [[mappa mundi]], probably created in the late 1370s or the early 1380s (often conventionally dated 1375),<ref>{{cite book |last1=Ferandez-Armesto |first1=F.F.R. |title=The European opportunity |date=1995 |publisher=Aldershot, Great Britain; Brookfield, Vt. : Variorum |isbn=978-0-86078-501-9 |page=291 |url=https://archive.org/details/isbn_0860785017/page/291/mode/2up|quote=The Catalan Atlas is conventionally attributed to 1375, because that year is used as the starting-point for the computation of the Golden Number, but 1376 and 1377 are also mentioned in its accompanying texts; it conforms closely to the description of such an atlas in the French royal library catalogue, dated 1380 [...] The Catalan Atlas can be assigned with some confidence to the late 1370s or the early 1380s.}}</ref><ref>The date "1375" is mentioned in several places in the map: {{cite book |last1=Gunn |first1=Geoffrey C. |title=Overcoming Ptolemy: The Revelation of an Asian World Region |date=15 October 2018 |publisher=Rowman & Littlefield |isbn=978-1-4985-9014-3 |page=67 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=xCRyDwAAQBAJ&pg=PA67 |language=en}}</ref> that has been described as the most important map of the [[Middle Ages]] in the [[Catalan language]],<ref name="The Jewish Contribution To Civilization" /><ref name="The late medieval age of crisis and renewal, 1300-1500" /> and as "the zenith of medieval map-work".<ref name="The late medieval age of crisis and renewal, 1300-1500" />

It was produced by [[Cresques Abraham]], a Jewish cartographer and leader of the [[Majorcan cartographic school]], who was described by a contemporary as a master of ''mappae mundi'' as well as of compasses and [[illuminated manuscript]]s.<ref name="To the Ends of the Earth: 100 Maps that Change the World" /> It was in the royal library of France by 1380, during the reign of [[Charles V of France|King Charles V]], and is still preserved in the {{Lang|fr|[[Bibliothèque nationale de France]]|italic=no}}. The Catalan Atlas originally consisted of six [[vellum]] leaves (each circa {{cvt|64.5|by|50|cm|disp=sqbr}}) folded vertically, painted in various colours including gold and silver.<ref name="bnf">{{cite web |url=https://archivesetmanuscrits.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cc78545v |work=Bibliothèque nationale de France |title=Espagnol 30 |language=fr |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230208091702/https://archivesetmanuscrits.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cc78545v |archive-date= Feb 8, 2023 }}</ref> They were later mounted on the front and back of five wooden panels, with the ends enclosed in a leather binding by [[Simon Vostre]] {{Circa|1515}}, restored most recently in 1991. Wear has split each leaf into two.<ref name="bnf"/>

The first two leaves contain texts in Catalan covering [[cosmography]], [[astronomy]], and [[astrology]]. These texts are accompanied by illustrations. The texts and illustration emphasise the Earth's spherical shape and the state of the then known world. They also provide information to sailors on tides and how to tell time at night.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.martianherald.com/9-maps-have-shaped-world/page/4|title=Catalan World Atlas |work=9 Maps That Have Shaped The World |publisher=Martian Herald|author=Edwin Castano|access-date=11 July 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190711073448/https://www.martianherald.com/9-maps-have-shaped-world/page/4 |archive-date=11 July 2019}}</ref>

The four remaining leaves make up the actual map, with [[Jerusalem]] located close to the centre; two depict the Orient; the remaining two show Europe, along with North and West Africa. The map is around {{cvt|1.3|sqm}} in size. It shows illustrations of many cities—Christian cities with a cross, other cities with a dome—and with each city's political allegiance indicated by a flag. Wavy blue vertical lines are used to symbolise oceans. Place names of important ports are transcribed in red, while others are indicated in black. The illustrations and most of the text are oriented towards the edges of the map, suggesting it was intended to be used by laying it flat and walking around it.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Brotton |first1=Jerry |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=0HkRBAAAQBAJ&pg=PA62 |title=Great Maps |date=2014 |publisher=DK Publishing |isbn=978-602-72449-6-2 |page=62 |access-date=26 July 2018}}</ref>

The oriental portion of the Catalan Atlas illustrates numerous religious references as well as a synthesis of medieval ''[[mappa mundi|mappae mundi]]'' and the travel literature of the time, especially [[Marco Polo]]'s ''[[The Travels of Marco Polo|Book of Marvels]]'' and [[Sir John Mandeville|Mandeville]]'s ''Travels and Voyage of Sir John Mandeville''. Many Indian and Chinese cities can be identified.<ref>{{cite book |author=Dhani Irwanto|title=Taprobana: Classical Knowledge of an Island in the Opposite-Earth|date=27 March 2019|publisher=Indonesia Hydro Media |isbn=978-1-4654-3561-3 |page=15 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=F6qODwAAQBAJ&q=catalan+atlas+taprobana&pg=PA15 |access-date=11 July 2019}}</ref> The explanatory texts report customs described by Polo and catalogue local economic resources, real or supposed.{{citation needed|date=July 2019}}

The Western portion is similar to contemporary [[portolan chart]]s, but contains the first [[compass rose]] known to have been used on such a chart.<ref name="The late medieval age of crisis and renewal, 1300-1500" />

===Mali Empire===
[[File:Catalan_Atlas_BNF_Sheet_6_Mansa_Musa.jpg|thumb|Depiction of [[Mansa Musa]], ruler of the [[Mali Empire]], holding a gold object]]
The [[Mali Empire]] and its riches are shown under the rule of [[Mansa Musa]]. The caption reads:
{{blockquote|''This black Lord is called [[Mansa Musa|Musse Melly]] and is the sovereign of the land of the black people of Gineva ([[Ghana]]). This king is the richest and noblest of all these lands due to the abundance of gold that is extracted from his lands.''<ref>{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel III |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-iii |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}
The Catalan Atlas's depiction of Musa as "the richest man in this region" - frequently misquoted in modern media as a global or all-time superlative - has been examined by Hadrien Collet as one of the earliest European stages in the centuries-long fabrication of the Mansa Musa wealth myth.<ref>{{cite book |last=Collet |first=Hadrien |title=Le sultanat du Mali. Histoire régressive d'un empire médiéval (XXIe–XIVe siècle) |publisher=CNRS Éditions |location=Paris |year=2022 |language=fr |isbn=978-2-271-13979-5}}</ref>

===Organa===
[[File:Oghene(Organa) the Ooni of Ife - Catalan Atlas.jpg|thumb|Depiction of the king of Organa, "Rey de Organa".]]

There are different interpretations of the depiction of Organa, placed in the central Sahara in the Catalan Atlas,<ref name=":1">{{Cite journal |last=Obayemi |first=Ade. M. |date=1980 |title=Between Nok, Ile-Ife and Benin: Progress Report and Prospects |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/41971339 |journal=Journal of the Historical Society of Nigeria |volume=10 |issue=3 |pages=79–94 |issn=0018-2540}}</ref> on which the caption reads: {{blockquote|''Here rules the King of Organa, a [[Saracen]] that constantly battles with the Saracens of the coast and with other Arabs.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-iv |title=Panel IV |website=The Cresques Project}}</ref>''}}There was no medieval kingdom in Africa that bore the name Organa.<ref name=":2">{{Cite book |last=Andrea |first=Alfred J. |author-link=Alfred J. Andrea|title=Seven Myths of Africa in World History |last2=Holt |first2=Andrew |publisher=Hackett Publishing Company |year=2017 |isbn=978-1-62466-641-4 |location= |pages=ix–x |chapter=Series Editors’ Foreword}}</ref> The use of this name for a country, and its association with a European-style heraldic flag (depicting a palm tree) reflects poor medieval European understanding of the African interior.<ref name=":2" />

==== Kanem–Bornu Empire ====
The traditional interpretation of Organa is that it refers to the [[Kanem–Bornu Empire]], a large and powerful state that occupied the region in which Organa is placed on the map.<ref name=":2" /><ref>{{Cite journal |last=Palmer |first=H. Richmond |date=1941 |title=40. Trident-Gods in Sahara and Western Sudan |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/2793346 |journal=Man |volume=41 |pages=60–62 |doi=10.2307/2793346 |issn=0025-1496|url-access=subscription }}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Ajayi |first=J. F. Ade |url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/History_of_West_Africa/wNgwAQAAIAAJ |title=History of West Africa |last2=Crowder |first2=Michael |date=1985 |publisher=Longman |isbn=978-0-582-64683-4 |pages=185 |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":3">{{Cite book |last=Masonen |first=Pekka |url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/The_Negroland_Revisited/8sIjAQAAIAAJ |title=The Negroland Revisited: Discovery and Invention of the Sudanese Middle Ages |date=2000 |publisher=Finnish Academy of Science and Letters |isbn=978-951-41-0886-0 |pages=103 |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book |last=Stewart |first=John |url=https://www.google.com/books/edition/African_States_and_Rulers_3d_ed/GZMuEQAAQBAJ |title=African States and Rulers, 3d ed. |date=2024 |publisher=McFarland |isbn=978-1-4766-1707-7 |pages=35 |language=en}}</ref> The name ''Organa'' has been suggested to be a heavily corrupted form of ''Kanem''<ref name=":1" /> or to derive from the Saharan city [[Ouargla]], misunderstood as part of the empire by European mapmakers.<ref name=":3" />

====Ife Empire====
In 1980, the Nigerian historian Ade. M. Obayemi suggested that Organa could alternatively be identified with the [[Ife Empire]],<ref name=":1" /> although it would be geographically misplaced.<ref name=":4">{{Cite journal |last=Blier |first=Suzanne Preston |date=2012 |title=Art in Ancient Ife, Birthplace of the Yoruba |url=https://www.jstor.org/stable/41721406 |journal=African Arts |volume=45 |issue=4 |pages=70–85 |issn=0001-9933}}</ref> The name ''Organa'' could then allude to the early Ife rulers using the title "Ogane" (Oghene, Ogene).<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":4" /> [[Suzanne Blier|Suzanne Preston Blier]] has also supported this view, and argued that the title is also referenced in a Portuguese account from the 1480s by the seafarer Joao Afonso de Aveiro, whose work describes an inland ruler that played a central role in [[Benin kingdom|Benin]] royal enthronements by providing a brass crown, staff, and cross to acknowledge a new king.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book |last=Parrinder |first=E. G. |title=Art and risk in ancient Yoruba: Ife history, power and identity c. 1300 |date=2014 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-1-107-02166-2 |page=6 |doi=10.1017/CBO9781139128872}}</ref>

==== Ghana Empire ====
In 2006, the Italian cartographer Piero Falchetta suggested that Organa could be a reference to either the Kanem–Bornu Empire or the more western [[Ghana Empire]] (which had long fallen by the time the Catalan Atlas was made).<ref>{{Cite book |last=Falchetta |first=Piero |title=Fra Mauro's World Map |publisher=Brepols |year=2006 |isbn=978-2-503-51726-1 |location= |pages=292}}</ref>

===India===
[[File:Sultan of Delhi (top) and King of Vijayanagar (bottom) in the Catalan Atlas of 1375.jpg|thumb|left|Western and southern India.]]
The western part of the Indian subcontinent is clearly depicted, and several of the location names are accurate.<ref>{{cite book |title=Cartography between Christian Europe and the Arabic-Islamic World, 1100-1500: Divergent Traditions |date=17 June 2021 |publisher=BRILL |isbn=978-90-04-44603-8 |page=176 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=eq0zEAAAQBAJ&pg=PA176 |language=en}}</ref><ref name="GG68"/> To the north appears the Sultan of Delhi (''Rey de delli''), the ruler of the contemporary [[Delhi Sultanate]], with his flag on numerous cities ({{flagicon image|Sultan of Delhi Flag according to the Catalan Atlas (1375).png}}). The caption reads:
{{blockquote|''Here is a great sultan, powerful and very rich: the sultan has seven hundred elephants and a hundred thousand horsemen under his command. He also has countless foot soldiers. In this part of the land there is a lot of gold and precious stones.''<ref name="VL5">{{cite journal |last1=Liščák |first1=Vladimír |title=Mapa mondi (Catalan Atlas of 1375), Majorcan cartographic school, and 14th century Asia |journal=International Cartographic Association |date=2017 |volume=1 |page=5 |doi=10.5194/ica-proc-1-69-2018 |doi-access=free |bibcode=2018PrICA...1...69L |url=https://www.proc-int-cartogr-assoc.net/1/69/2018/ica-proc-1-69-2018.pdf}}</ref>}}

In the center of India appears the traditional [[Seuna (Yadava) dynasty|Yadava]] capital of ''Diogil'' ("Deogiri", or [[Devagiri]] {{flagicon image|Diogil_(Deogil._Dwaigir._Daulatabad)_in_the_Catalan_Atlas_(1375).jpg}}). On top of the city of ''Diogil'' floats a peculiar flag ({{flagicon image|Flag of Diogil, Catalan Atlas 1375.png}}), while coastal cities are under the black flag of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] ({{flagicon image|Sultan of Delhi Flag according to the Catalan Atlas (1375).png}}).<ref>{{cite book |title=Antiquities from San Thomé and Mylapore |date=1936 |pages=264–265 |url=https://archive.org/details/AntiquitiesFromSanThomeAndMylaporeHenriHosten1936/page/n319/mode/2up?q=diogil}}</ref><ref>{{cite journal |last1=Kadoi |first1=Yuka |title=On the Timurid flag |journal=Beiträge zur islamischen Kunst und Archäologie |date=2010 |volume=2 |page=148 |doi=10.29091/9783954909537/009 |url=https://www.academia.edu/17410816|quote="...helps identify another curious flag found in northern India – a brown or originally silver flag with a vertical black line – as the flag of the Delhi Sultanate (602-962/1206-1555)."}}</ref> Devagiri was ultimately captured by [[Alauddin Khalji]] of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] in 1307.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Beaujard |first1=Philippe |title=The worlds of the Indian Ocean: a global history |date=2019 |publisher=Cambridge University Press |isbn=978-1-108-42456-1 |chapter=Chapter 8 |quote=The sultan captured the Rajput fort of Chitor, in Rājasthān, and in 1310 he subjected most of the Deccan to his power. He took Devagiri – the capital of the Yādava – in 1307.}}</ref> The trading ship raises the flag of the [[Ilkhanate]] ({{flagicon image|Flag_of_the_Ilkhanate.svg}}). Its caption reads:

{{blockquote|''Let it be known that these ships are named nichi, and measure sixty cubits in depth and thirty-four cubits in length; there are few with less than four masts and some even have ten. The sails are made of reed and palm leafs.''<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel V">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel V |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-v |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}

To the south, at the tip of India, appears the "[[Kollam|King of Colombo]]" with a Christian flag ({{flagicon image|Flag of the Kigdom of Colombo, Catalan Atlas 1375.png}}). He was identified as Christian due to the early [[Saint Thomas Christians|Saint Thomas Christianity]] there (since at least the 8th century), and the Catholic mission there under [[Jordan Catala]] since 1329.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=Liščák |first1=Vladimír |title=Mapa mondi (Catalan Atlas of 1375), Majorcan cartographic school, and 14th century Asia |journal=International Cartographic Association |date=2017 |volume=1 |pages=4–5 |doi=10.5194/ica-proc-1-69-2018 |doi-access=free |bibcode=2018PrICA...1...69L |url=https://www.proc-int-cartogr-assoc.net/1/69/2018/ica-proc-1-69-2018.pdf}}</ref> His caption reads:

{{blockquote|''Here rules the king of [[Kollam|Colombo]], a Christian.''<ref name="VL5"/>}}
Jordan, Christian missionary to Colombo from 1329, who wrote "Book of Marvels" (''Mirabilia descripta'', 1340), was probably the source of the information about [[Kollam|Colombo]] in the Catalan Atlas.<ref name="VL4">{{cite journal |last1=Liščák |first1=Vladimír |title=Mapa mondi (Catalan Atlas of 1375), Majorcan cartographic school, and 14th century Asia |journal=International Cartographic Association |date=2017 |volume=1 |pages=4–5 |doi=10.5194/ica-proc-1-69-2018 |doi-access=free |bibcode=2018PrICA...1...69L |url=https://www.proc-int-cartogr-assoc.net/1/69/2018/ica-proc-1-69-2018.pdf}}</ref> He mentions the earlier presence of the [[Saint Thomas Christians]] in India.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Jordanus |first1=Catalani |last2=Yule |first2=Henry |last3=Parr |first3=Charles McKew donor |last4=Parr |first4=Ruth |title=Mirabilia descripta: the wonders of the East |date=1863 |publisher=London : Printed for the Hakluyt Society |page=23, paragraph 31 |url=https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_wQUVAAAAQAAJ/page/n52/mode/1up}}</ref>

===Il-Khanate===
[[File:Il-Khanate in the Catalan Atlas (1375).jpg|thumb|Depiction of the ruler of the Ilkhanate and his realm.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Massing |first1=Jean Michel |last2=Albuquerque |first2=Luís de |last3=Brown |first3=Jonathan |last4=González |first4=J. {{blockquote|''Here there was the Great Babylonia, where Nabuconodosor resided, and that is called Baldaca today. [[File:Ilkhante ship sailing the Indian Ocean towards India, in the Catalan Atlas (1375).jpg|left|thumb|A ship under the Ilkhanate flag ({{flagicon image|Flag_of_the_Ilkhanate.svg}}), sailing the [[Indian Ocean]] towards the coast of India under the control of the [[Delhi Sultanate]] ({{flagicon image|Delhi_Sultanate_Flag.svg}}).<ref>{{cite book |last1=Dang |first1=Baohai |last2=Rong |first2=Xinjiang |title=Marco Polo and the Silk Road |date=9 November 2021 |publisher=Beijing Book Co. Over him appears a city within a sphere, with the following caption, mentioning the Persian city of [[Shiraz]] and [[Ptolemy]]:

{{blockquote|''This city is named [[Shiraz|Siras]], and in antiquity, it was named the City of Grace because it was there where astronomy was invented by the great wise man [[Ptolemy]].''<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel V">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel V |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-v |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}

{{blockquote|''Know that these ships are called ''[[Junk (ship)|junk]]s'' (''inchi'') and have sixty-cubit hulls with thirty-four cubits of freeboard. ===Golden Horde===
[[File:Golden Horde in the Catalan Atlas (1375).jpg|thumb|left|[[Jani Beg]] of the [[Golden Horde]].<ref name="JMA29+"/>]]
The [[Mongols|Mongol]] polity of the [[Golden Horde]] is accurately depicted north of the [[Caspian Sea]]. The ruler named [[Jani Beg]] has been identified in this representation, being mentioned as ''"Jambech senyor de [[Sarai (city)|Sarra]]"'', and the flag of the Golden Horde also appears ({{flagicon image|Catalan Atlas, Flag of the Golden Horde.svg}}).<ref name="JMA29+">{{cite book |last1=Massing |first1=Jean Michel |last2=Albuquerque |first2=Luís de |last3=Brown |first3=Jonathan |last4=González |first4=J. J. Martín |title=Circa 1492: Art in the Age of Exploration |date=1 January 1991 |publisher=Yale University Press |isbn=978-0-300-05167-4 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wMK-Ba0-RG4C&pg=PA29 |language=en|page=29}}</ref> The caption to the right reads:

[[File:Catalan Atlas, Flag of the Golden Horde.svg|thumb|upright|Flag of the Golden Horde according to the Catalan Atlas.]]
[[File:Golden Horde. Möngke (Mengu) Timur. AH 665-679 AD 1267-1280 Bulghar mint. Dated AH 672 or 3 (AD 1273-1275).jpg|thumb|Coinage of [[Mengu-Timur]] with [[tamgha]] symbol [[File:Tamga_Mengu-Timur.svg|10px]], dated 1273–1275.]]
{{blockquote|''Here resides the emperor of this northern region whose empire starts in the province of Bulgaria and ends at the city of Organcio. The sovereign is named Jambech, Lord of the Sarra. ''<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel V">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel V |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-v |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}

The symbolism of the Golden Horde flag depicted by the Catalan Atlas ({{flagicon image|Catalan Atlas, Flag of the Golden Horde.svg}}) is fairly similar to the type of [[tamgha]] symbols (such as [[File:Tamga_Mengu-Timur.svg|10px]]) actually found on the coinage of the Golden Horde.<ref>[[File:Golden Horde. Möngke (Mengu) Timur. AH 665-679 AD 1267-1280 Bulghar mint. Dated AH 672 or 3 (AD 1273-1275).jpg|30px]]Coinage of [[Mengu-Timur]]. Bulghar mint. Dated AH 672 or 3 (AD 1273-1275)</ref><ref>{{cite book |title=Asiatische Forschungen |date=1982 |publisher=O. Harrassowitz |isbn=978-3-447-02273-6 |page=184 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=uSI8AAAAIAAJ |language=en|quote="The tamga ( a sign of ownership or of belonging to a clan ) drawn on the flag can be seen on coins minted in Bulgar and Bilyar at the time of the early Golden Horde. (...) Besides the Catalan atlas of 1375 there is also a 50 x123 cm Catalan map preserved in Paris."}}</ref> Such symbols were used until the time of [[Jani Beg]], but essentially disappear thereafter.<ref>{{cite journal |last1=FEDOROV-DAVYDOV |first1=GERMAN A. |title=The Monetary System of The Golden Horde |journal=Paleograph Press |date=2003 |page=349 |url=http://www.paleog.com/im/fd/summary.pdf|quote="Tamga in the form of a two-pointed prong was retained on the coins minted in Bolgar, Mokhsha and the Crimea up until the reign of Janibek, marking the disappearance of this image for the rest of the 14th century."}}</ref>

The text to the left reads:
{{blockquote|''Let it be known that those that wish to cross this desert stop and rest during a week in a city named Lop. Here, expeditions and their animals relax/enjoy themselves. After that, they procure what is needed for the next seven months of the journey, because in the desert one travels an entire day and night before reaching potable water; however, every day and a half, they can find plenty of it, enough for fifty or a hundred people or even more. And if it happens that a rider, tired by the journey, falls sleep or for any other reason he separates from his companions, he will often hear the voices of devils, similar to the voices of his companions, often calling him by his own name. In this way, the devils take him through the desert to a fro such that the traveler cannot find his companions. A thousand stories are known about this desert. ''<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel V">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel V |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-v |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}

===Anatolia===
[[File:Anatolian Beyliks in the Catalan Atlas (1375).jpg|thumb|[[Anatolian Beyliks]] in the Catalan Atlas. The map is shown upside down.<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel IV">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel IV |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-iv |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref><ref name="PF"/>]]
The [[Anatolian Beyliks]], a group of Turkic principalities in [[Anatolia]] are also depicted, in the region labelled ''Turqhia'' ([[Turkey]]). The caption next to the seated ruler in Anatolia reads: ''Asia Minor also called Turkey, where there are many cities and castles.''<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel IV"/><ref name="PF">{{cite book |last1=Forêt |first1=Philippe |last2=Kaplony |first2=Andreas |title=The Journey of Maps and Images on the Silk Road |date=30 November 2008 |publisher=BRILL |isbn=978-90-474-2497-0 |page=194 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Rw6wCQAAQBAJ&pg=PA194 |language=en}}</ref> Numerous Turkic principalities appear, with a variety of flags, but very little prominence is given to the Orthodox princes of the [[Byzantine Empire]], although several Byzantine cities appear with the Byzantine imperial flag ({{flagicon image|Byzantine imperial flag, 14th century, square.svg}}), or [[Empire of Trebizond|Trebizond]] ({{flagicon image|Trebizond (Catalan Atlas, 1375).png}}).<ref name="PF"/> The Christian kingdom of [[Cilician Armenia]] appears heavily fortified within green walls, with its ports and flags ([[File:Drapeau héthoumide.png|17px]], [[File:Armenian Cilicia with crosses (Catalan Atlas, 1375).png|17px]]) clearly visible.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Massing |first1=Jean Michel|title=Circa 1492: Art in the Age of Exploration |date=1 January 1991 |publisher=Yale University Press |isbn=978-0-300-05167-4 |page=27 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wMK-Ba0-RG4C&pg=PA27 |language=en |quote="The Cilician kingdom of Armenia Minor is more clearly indicated. Founded at the end of the twelfth century, it fell to the Turks in 1375"}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |last1=Galichian |first1=Rouben |title=Historic Maps of Armenia - The Cartographic Heritage. Abridged and updated |date=2014 |publisher=BENNETT & BLOOM |page=59 |url=https://www.academia.edu/36646104}}</ref>

===Gog and Magog===
[[File:Abraham Cresques Atlas de cartes-GogiMagog-crop.jpg|thumb|left|Land of "Gog i Magog".]]
The land of [[Gog and Magog]] appears in the top right corner. Traditionally described as wild and vicious man-eaters, the populace is uncharacteristically represented here by a stately king mounted on a horse and leading an orderly procession. Nearby appears [[Alexander the Great]] standing in front of Satan and pointing to a fortress where he intends to confine the nations of Gog and Magog.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Fein |first=Ariel |date=June 6, 2022 |title=The Catalan Atlas |url=https://smarthistory.org/catalan-atlas/ |website=Smarthistory}}</ref>

===Chagatai Khanate===
[[File:Kingdom of Chagatai in the Catalan Atlas (1375).jpg|thumb|Kingdom of Chagatai in the Catalan Atlas (1375).]]
The Khan [[Kebek]], [[Mongols|Mongol]] ruler of the [[Chagatai Khanate]] is depicted with the following caption:

{{blockquote|''Here reigns the King Chabech ([[Kebek]]), lord of the Medeja [Media] Empire. He resides at Emalech ([[Almaliq, Xinjiang|Almaliq]]).''<ref>''Açi senyoreya lo rey Chabech, seynior qui es dit del imperi de Medeia. Aquest esta en Emalech.'' in {{cite book |last1=Buchon |first1=Jean Alexandre |title=Notice D'un Atlas En Langue Catalane, Manuscrit de L'An 1375 Conservé Parmi Les Manuscrits de La Bibliothèque Royale Sous Le Numéro 6816, Fonds Ancien |date=2011 |isbn=978-1-271-74145-8 |page=135 |url=https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Notices_et_extraits_des_manuscrits_de_la_Biblioth%C3%A8que_du_Roi_-_Vol._XIV%2C_no_1_%281839%29.pdf}}</ref><ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel VI">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel VI |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-vi |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}

His cities appear with the Chagatai flag ({{flagicon image|Flag of the Chagatai Khanate.svg}}).<ref>{{cite book |last1=Massing |first1=Jean Michel |title=Circa 1492: Art in the Age of Exploration |date=1 January 1991 |publisher=Yale University Press |isbn=978-0-300-05167-4 |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=wMK-Ba0-RG4C&pg=PA30 |language=en|page=30}}</ref>

{{blockquote|The most powerful prince of the Tartars is named Holubeim [Kubilay Khan], that means Great Khan. This emperor is richer than any other emperor in the world. This emperor is protected by twelve thousand horsemen with their four captains that stay at the court three months of the year.<ref name="The Cresques Project - Panel VI">{{cite web |title=The Cresques Project - Panel VI |url=https://www.cresquesproject.net/catalan-atlas-legends/panel-vi |website=www.cresquesproject.net}}</ref>}}

===Antichrist===
[[File:Antechrist in the Catalan Atlas (1375).jpg|thumb|Antichrist in the Catalan Atlas (1375).]]
The [[Antichrist]] appears beyond the [[Great Wall]] of China, next to the territory of [[Gog and Magog]]. The label reads:

{{blockquote|Antichrist. He will be raised in Goraym of Galilea, and at the age of thirty he will start to preach in Jerusalem; contrary to the truth, he will proclaim that he is Christ, the living son of God. It is said that he will rebuild the Temple.<ref>{{cite web |title=Catalan Atlas.

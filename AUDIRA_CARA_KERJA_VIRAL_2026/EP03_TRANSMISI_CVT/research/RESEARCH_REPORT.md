## 2. Verified facts

### D-CVT (Daihatsu Dual mode CVT)
1. **D-CVT = "Dual mode Continuously Variable Transmission"**, a Daihatsu trademark. It adds **split gears** to an ordinary CVT belt drive. At high speed it drives through **both the belt and the gears** for better transmission efficiency. Source: Toyota Raize launch release, https://global.toyota/en/newsroom/toyota/29251184.html. HIGH (manufacturer).
2. **Debut: Daihatsu Tanto, 9 July 2019**, the first DNGA model. Source: Daihatsu press release PDF, https://www.daihatsu.com/jp/news/2019/20190709-1.pdf (p.7). HIGH.
3. **Claimed world first:** Daihatsu calls it the world's first belt CVT that splits power between belt and gear (claim "as of 9 July 2019, Daihatsu research"). Same PDF, footnote ※12. HIGH as a claim (self-reported).
4. **Transmission efficiency at high speed improves by about 8%** in "belt + gear" mode. Same PDF p.7. HIGH.
5. **Ratio spread widened from 5.3 to 7.3** on both the low and high sides. The Tanto itself uses the high side only up to 6.7. Same PDF p.7. HIGH.
6. **Acceleration about 15% better** (Tanto, from engine changes plus the new CVT). Same PDF, line "加速度を約15%向上". HIGH (combined claim).
7. **Efficiency by speed:** in split mode, efficiency is **+12% at 60 km/h** and **+19% at 100 km/h**. Engine speed drops by **200 rpm** and **550 rpm** at those speeds. Source: Paul Tan, citing a Daihatsu presentation, https://paultan.org/2021/02/24/perodua-ativa-how-d-cvt-is-different-to-other-cvts/. MED (secondary source citing the manufacturer).
8. **Ratio range:** in split mode it is close to an **8-speed AT**, where an ordinary CVT is closer to a 6-speed AT. **Pulley centre distance is 136 mm**, input-output distance is 168 mm (claimed world's smallest), and the **torque limit is 150 Nm**. It suits kei cars up to 1.5 L engines. Same Paul Tan article. MED.
9. **Hardware:** additional gears and a planetary gear set sit on the pulley input/output shafts. A **clutch pack** engages the gear path. The car pulls away through a **torque converter**. Paul Tan, same article. MED.
10. **When it switches:** Paul Tan quotes the Daihatsu presentation as "between 40–90% driving force". GridOto says "above about 60 km/h". Wikipedia says only "at higher speeds". **No official km/h threshold was found.** MED/disputed (see section 4).
11. **Vehicles with D-CVT:** Daihatsu Tanto (2019), Daihatsu Rocky / Toyota Raize (2019; the Raize was Toyota's first D-CVT car), Toyota Avanza/Veloz and Daihatsu Xenia W100 (2021), Perodua Ativa, Alza (2022), Axia (2023), Myvi, Daihatsu Taft, Toyota Yaris Cross (petrol), and Daihatsu Ayla (2nd gen). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daihatsu_Rocky_(A200), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Avanza, and Wikipedia search results for "D-CVT". HIGH for Rocky/Raize/Avanza/Tanto; MED for the rest.
12. **Raize WLTC fuel economy:** 18.6 km/L (2WD) and 17.4 km/L (4WD), 1.0 L turbo + D-CVT (Japanese spec). Toyota release above. HIGH.

### Belt/pulley CVT in general
13. The most common CVT uses **two variable-diameter pulleys**, each made of **two cone halves** that slide together and apart, joined by a V-belt. When one pulley closes, the other must open, because the belt length and the pulley distance are fixed. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_variable_transmission. HIGH.
14. **Car push belt (Van Doorne type):** layered steel bands carry metal "elements" (blocks). Power travels as the **elements push each other (compression)**, not by belt tension. Source: ja.wikipedia 無段変速機, https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E7%84%A1%E6%AE%B5%E5%A4%89%E9%80%9F%E6%A9%9F. Wikipedia EN also notes that some CVTs work by "push" and some by "pull". HIGH.
15. **Efficiency:** a belt CVT is about **88%** efficient, below a manual gearbox, but the engine can stay at its most efficient rpm (Wikipedia EN, citing ref [6]). ja.wikipedia says metal-belt CVTs historically reached "the 80% range" and chain types about 90% by 2021. MED.
16. **Ratio spread:** a CVT reaches **at most about 8.2**, against **9.81** for a 9-speed AT. Some designs add a 2-speed auxiliary gearbox to reach **8.7** (Jatco type). Source: ja.wikipedia 無段変速機. MED.
17. **Known drawbacks:** the oil pump needs high pressure to clamp the pulleys, which wastes energy, especially at high speed. Belt whine is loudest under heavy load. On full throttle the CVT "holds" the engine at high rpm, the "rubber-band" feeling. Paul Tan saw the Ativa hold **5,500–6,000 rpm** on hard acceleration. Sources: ja.wikipedia and Paul Tan. MED.
18. **History:** DAF Variomatic, 1958 (DAF 600), used a rubber belt with centrifugal weights plus manifold vacuum (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variomatic). The 1987 Subaru Justy ECVT was the first electronically controlled steel-belt CVT (Wikipedia CVT). HIGH.
19. **Toyota Direct Shift-CVT** is not the same as D-CVT. It uses a **launch gear** at low speed, up to about **40 km/h**, then the belt. D-CVT is the reverse: gears at high speed. Sources: Wikipedia CVT (Corolla E210) and Paul Tan. HIGH.

### Scooter CVT (rubber V-belt)
20. **Main parts:** a rubber V-belt, a **primary pulley** (drive, on the crankshaft) and a **secondary pulley** (driven, through a **centrifugal clutch** and reduction gears to the rear wheel). Source: id.wikipedia Transmisi otomatis, https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmisi_otomatis. MED.
21. **Roller weights:** these are inside the primary pulley. As rpm rises, centrifugal force throws the rollers outward. They push the sliding sheave, so the belt climbs to a larger diameter on the front pulley. At the rear, the belt pulls in against the **spring** and rides at a smaller diameter, so the ratio becomes "taller". Sources: id.wikipedia and ja.wikipedia (ウエイトローラー). MED.
22. **Centrifugal clutch:** it engages at about **3,000 rpm** (generic id.wikipedia example; the real value depends on the model). MED.
23. **Torque cam (kickdown mechanism):** a grooved cam on the secondary pulley. Under heavy load (climbing, carrying a passenger, hard acceleration) belt tension makes the cam close the secondary pulley. The ratio stays "short" even when the rollers are pushing outward. Source: id.wikipedia Transmisi otomatis. MED.

### YECVT (Yamaha Electric CVT) — NMAX "Turbo"
24. **YECVT = Yamaha Electric CVT**, fitted on the NMAX "TURBO" Tech MAX and Ultimate (155 cc Blue Core, VVA, 4-valve SOHC). **Two riding modes:** T-Mode (Town Commuting) and S-Mode (Sport Touring). **Y-Shift** has **3 levels: 1 Low, 2 Medium, 3 High**, and works for both acceleration and deceleration. Source: Yamaha Indonesia product page, https://www.yamaha-motor.co.id/product/nmax-turbo. HIGH.
25. **Mechanism:** the **roller weights are removed**. An **electric drive motor** moves the primary pulley on commands from the **TCU (Transmission Control Unit)**. The TCU reads **primary and secondary pulley sensors**. Antonius Widiantoro (Asst. GM Marketing–PR, PT YIMM) is quoted directly in detikOto: https://oto.detik.com/motor/d-7389113/penjelasan-sistem-turbo-di-yamaha-nmax-terbaru. Also https://oto.detik.com/motor/d-7864458/begini-kerja-teknologi-yecvt-yamaha-nmax-turbo-untuk-performa-maksimal. HIGH (named manufacturer source).
26. **"Turbo" does not mean a turbocharger.** There is no exhaust turbine. The "turbo" is the electronically controlled CVT. Same detikOto article. HIGH.
27. **Yamaha's internal tests:** 0–100 m in **7.2 s**, against **7.43 s** for the previous NMAX. Over 200 m it is **9.2 m** ahead. A 150 m climb at 10° takes **11.73 s**, against **12.33 s**. Source: https://oto.detik.com/editors-choice/d-7827243/teknologi-yecvt-di-yamaha-nmax-turbo-pertama-dan-tercanggih-di-kelasnya. MED (manufacturer claim, internal test).
28. **Claimed a first:** YECVT is the first electronic CVT in the 150–160 cc maxi-scooter segment (detikOto, the Yamaha claim). MED.
29. **Launch timing:** Wikipedia EN says the NMAX update with YECVT came in "June 2024" (marked *citation needed*), and that the Turbo is sold only in Indonesia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaha_NMAX. MED (low quality).

## 3. Step-by-step mechanism (for animation)

**A. Car CVT / D-CVT**
1. The engine turns. Its power passes through the torque converter into the **primary pulley**, which is made of two steel cones facing each other.
2. A **steel push belt** of hundreds of small blocks links the primary pulley to the **secondary pulley**. The blocks *push* one another to carry the power.
3. Oil pressure squeezes and releases the cones. When the primary pulley closes, the belt rides high. At the same time the secondary pulley opens and the belt sinks there. The ratio changes smoothly, with no "gear shift" step.
4. Pulling away and at low speed, the primary pulley has a small diameter and the secondary a large one, giving a "short" ratio, strong like first gear.
5. As speed rises, the diameters swap slowly and the ratio gets "taller". The engine can hold its most efficient rpm.
6. D-CVT only: at higher speed a **clutch pack** engages the **split gear / planetary gear**. Power splits between the belt and the gears, and belt friction losses drop. Daihatsu claims +8% efficiency and a ratio spread of 7.3, up from 5.3.
7. Result: the engine turns 200–550 rpm lower when cruising, so the car is quieter and uses less fuel (Daihatsu claim via Paul Tan).

**B. Scooter CVT plus YECVT**
1. The crankshaft turns the **front pulley**, which holds the **roller weights**.
2. As rpm rises, the rollers are thrown outward and push the sheave. The rubber belt climbs on the front pulley.
3. The belt pulls in at the **rear pulley** against the spring, so its diameter there shrinks and the motorcycle speeds up.
4. The **centrifugal clutch** behind the rear pulley engages once the rpm is high enough (about 3,000 rpm in the generic example).
5. Climbing or carrying a passenger, the **torque cam** forces the rear pulley closed, so the ratio stays short and strong.
6. **YECVT:** the rollers are replaced by an **electric motor**. The TCU reads the two pulley sensors and moves the front pulley precisely.
7. The **T/S Mode** button changes the shift map. **Y-Shift** (levels 1–3) forces a "short" ratio for instant acceleration or for engine braking.

## 4. Caveats and disputed points
- **D-CVT switch point:** GridOto says "above about 60 km/h". Paul Tan quotes a Daihatsu slide saying "40–90% driving force". Toyota says only "at high speeds". Do not state one km/h figure as fact. Say "at higher speeds (around 60 km/h, according to GridOto)".
- **"Avanza terbaru":** the article is a recycled 2021 piece. D-CVT is not new technology in 2026; it has been around since 2019.
- **Efficiency figures:** 8% (Daihatsu Japan, Tanto 2019) and 12%/19% (Paul Tan, Ativa slide) come from different measurement bases. Do not combine them. Pick one and name its source.
- **"88% belt CVT efficiency"** is a general Wikipedia figure, not a D-CVT figure.
- **YECVT performance figures** come from Yamaha's internal tests (not independent). NMAX Turbo launch date: Wikipedia says June 2024 with *citation needed*.
- **CVT maintenance:** no manufacturer service-interval numbers (fluid, belt, rollers) were verified. Do not quote km intervals without the vehicle's service book.
- The "3,000 rpm" clutch figure is a generic textbook example from id.wikipedia, not a specification for a particular model.

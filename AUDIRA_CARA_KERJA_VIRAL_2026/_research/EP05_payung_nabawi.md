Elharamain ties it to the **Saudi NCM seasonal forecast**: above-average rain from Nov 2026 to Jan 2027 in parts of the kingdom, quoted via Arab News. Madinah is **not** named among the regions expected to get heavy rain in November.

What SPA says, through Elharamain and detik (MED, secondary quoting the Saudi state agency):
- **250 umbrellas** that open and close, shading **more than 143,000 m²** and **more than 228,000 worshippers**.
- Each umbrella is about **25.5 × 25.5 m**, **about 22 m tall** and weighs **almost 40 tonnes**.
- Built to resist wind, rain and fire, and connected to the **rainwater drainage** system so water does not pool and worshippers do not slip.
- Moved automatically by a dedicated control unit. **Opened at sunrise and folded before dusk** (Elharamain wording), adjusted for operations and weather, and monitored from a **central control room**.
- **436 misting fans**, each with **16 nozzles** spraying treated water, plus **more than 1,000 lights** to keep the courtyard lit 24 hours.
- Metal frame reinforced with glass and carbon fibre; mosaic decoration and gilded brass ornaments.

## 2. Verified facts
1. **250 umbrellas** on the piazza (outer courtyard) of Masjid Nabawi. Sources: Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina_Haram_Piazza and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Masjid_an-Nabawi; Architect Magazine (SEFAR) https://www.architectmagazine.com/project-gallery/medina-haram-piazza; SPA via detik/Elharamain. HIGH.
2. **Phases:** first **182 umbrellas** aligned with the mosque's columns, then **68 more on the east square**, making 250. Project cost **SAR 4.7 billion** (Wikipedia Medina Haram Piazza, citing Asharq Al-Awsat). MED.
3. **Built in 2010:** installation from **September 2010**, project complete **November 2010** (Architect Magazine). Wikipedia says "completed in August 2010" and "In 2010" (Al-Masjid an-Nabawi). Year 2010 is HIGH; the month is disputed.
4. **Project team:** client the **Saudi Ministry of Finance**; general contractor **Saudi Binladin Group**; architect and engineer **SL Rasch GmbH** (Leinfelden-Echterdingen, Germany; founder Mahmoud Bodo Rasch); umbrella maker **Liebherr (Ehingen, Germany)**; membrane **SEFAR Architecture** (PTFE fabric); fabrication **Taiyo Kogyo / MakMax (Japan)**; mosaic composite cladding **Premier Composite Technologies (PCT, Dubai)**. Sources: Architect Magazine; https://www.sl-rasch.com/en/projects/u-26-piazza/; http://www.pct.ae/25m-umbrellas. HIGH.
   - Liebherr-Werk Ehingen (a mobile-crane plant) has also designed and built "Großschirme" (large umbrellas) since about 2000: https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebherr-Werk_Ehingen. MED.
5. **Size open: 25.5 × 25.5 m** (Architect Magazine/SEFAR; SPA). PCT and Wikipedia give **25 × 25 m = 625 m²** per unit. HIGH for about 25 m.
6. **Height:** SL Rasch gives **21.7 m folded**, and **14.4 m or 15.3 m open**; there are two heights so the membranes **overlap** into one continuous roof. Architect Magazine says "15 m high". SPA says "about 22 m" (which matches the **folded** height). HIGH (SL Rasch, the designer).
7. **Shaded area:** **143,000 m²** (SPA, Wikipedia, Architect Magazine/SEFAR, which counts 143,000 m² of PTFE fabric). SL Rasch: **600 m² per umbrella, 150,000 m² in total**. PCT: 106,000 m² (outlier). Use "about 143,000 m²". HIGH.
8. **Capacity:** SPA/Saudi Gazette say **228,000 worshippers**; SL Rasch says "up to 250,000 people". MED.
9. **Weight about 40 tonnes per umbrella**: SPA via detik/Elharamain only. MED.
10. **Opening or closing takes 3 minutes**: Architect Magazine (SEFAR press text). MED (single source).
11. **Membrane:** **PTFE (Teflon) fabric** developed by SEFAR for this project. It is sand-coloured rather than white, because pure white would be too bright underneath. The underside carries blue PTFE ribbon patterns. Claimed to lower ambient temperature by **at least 8 °C**. Four full-size prototypes were tested with Liebherr before approval. Source: Architect Magazine. MED/HIGH.
12. **Folding mechanism (piazza):** a **folding arm system with 6 hinges**, with **4 middle, 4 diagonal and 16 passive arms** per umbrella. When folded, the membrane is protected by **glass-fibre composite cladding panels with glass mosaic**. SL Rasch calls it "the biggest convertible roof in the world", and it won the **Abdullatif Al Fozan Award**. Source: https://www.sl-rasch.com/en/projects/u-26-piazza/. HIGH.
13. **Cladding:** a composite sandwich of glass fibre and epoxy laminate. When closed, the arms cover the membrane and the cladding forms a prismatic case. Source: PCT http://www.pct.ae/25m-umbrellas. HIGH.
14. **Drive and sensors.** The piazza drive type was **not** found in a primary source. As a comparison, SL Rasch's **inner courtyard** umbrellas (12 units, 6 per courtyard, a different project) use a **hydraulic cylinder with a 5 m stroke**, with a **pump for every two umbrellas driven by a 33 kW motor**. They are software-controlled by **sun position** and an **anemometer that blocks movement in wind above 50 km/h**, and are designed for wind loads up to **155 km/h**. They are 19.2 m folded and 14 m open, 17 × 18 m, and **blow cool air** from the mast base and capital. In winter they open at night to hold in the day's heat. Source: https://www.sl-rasch.com/en/projects/courtyard/. HIGH (for the courtyard only).
15. SL Rasch's 1988 prototype (its first folding-arm umbrella) was a study for the Medina piazza. It too was designed for **155 km/h** winds, with an **anemometer** that stops opening and closing in strong wind. https://www.sl-rasch.com/en/projects/convertible-umbrella/. HIGH.
16. **Misting fans 436 × 16 nozzles, >1,000 lights, central control room**: SPA via Elharamain/detik. MED.
17. Similar umbrellas stand at **Masjid Raya Baiturrahman, Banda Aceh** and **Al Hussein Mosque, Cairo** (Wikipedia Medina Haram Piazza; SL Rasch lists the Al Hussein project). MED. This is a useful local hook for Indonesian viewers.

## 3. Step-by-step mechanism (for animation)
1. **At rest (folded):** each umbrella stands like a slim column about 21.7 m tall. The PTFE fabric is folded inside a prismatic case of glass-fibre panels decorated with mosaic.
2. **Command:** the central control room sends a command (on a sun-position schedule: open in the morning, close before dusk). Sensors such as an anemometer check that the wind is safe. *(Wind lock confirmed for SL Rasch courtyard umbrellas and the prototype: >50 km/h blocked.)*
3. **Drive:** the actuator inside the column pushes the arm mechanism. *(Courtyard umbrellas: 5 m stroke hydraulic cylinder, 33 kW pump. The piazza drive is not confirmed in a primary source; Liebherr made the umbrellas and their control system.)*
4. **Arms unfold:** the folding-arm system with 6 hinges (4 diagonal, 4 middle and 16 passive arms) lifts and spreads like a flower. The mosaic cladding panels swing open.
5. **Fabric stretched:** the sand-coloured PTFE membrane pulls taut into a 25.5 × 25.5 m funnel. The whole sequence takes about **3 minutes**.
6. **Interlocking roof:** the umbrellas come in two heights (14.4 m and 15.3 m open), so the edges overlap into one continuous canopy over about 143,000 m².
7. **Comfort:** the PTFE blocks UV and lowers the temperature underneath (SEFAR claims at least 8 °C). 436 misting fans spray fine mist, and more than 1,000 lights work at night.
8. **Rain:** the funnel shape guides water down the column into the drainage system, so the marble floor does not flood. In the evening the sequence reverses: the arms fold back and the case closes.

## 4. Caveats and disputed points
- **Height:** "22 m" (SPA) is the **folded** height. When open the umbrella is lower, at 14.4–15.3 m (SL Rasch). Do not say "22 m tall when open".
- **Shaded area:** 143,000 m² (most sources) against 150,000 m² (SL Rasch) against 106,000 m² (PCT). Use 143,000.
- **Completion month:** August 2010 (Wikipedia) against September–November 2010 (Architect Magazine). Say "2010".
- **Piazza drive** (hydraulic or electric): not confirmed. The hydraulic and 33 kW figures belong to the 12 **inner courtyard** umbrellas, a different project. Say "a mechanical drive inside the column" or state the difference clearly.
- **Schedule:** "open at sunrise, fold before dusk" is the Elharamain wording. SL Rasch says the courtyard umbrellas *open at night in winter* to hold in the heat. The piazza schedule may change with the season and with rain.
- **The 40 t weight, 436 fans and >1,000 lights** come from SPA, read only through Indonesian media (the original SPA page was not read).
- Wikipedia calls Liebherr a "German architectural company", which is wrong. Liebherr is a machinery maker, and the umbrellas were built at the Ehingen plant.
- "Rain" is not a strong November hook for Madinah: the NCM forecast does not name Madinah for heavy rain in November.
